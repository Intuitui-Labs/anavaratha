## Research question (kept / narrowed)

**Original Q1:**  
*Which pressure types most visibly shorten time-to-act for Sri Lankan material–institutional–ecological systems we can actually observe?*

**Narrowed working form (desk-supported):**  
*For Sri Lanka’s publicly documented Dry Zone / irrigated paddy–cascade systems and the national agri-input chain that feeds them, which pressure types — (i) hydro-meteorological drought cascades, (ii) sudden institutional/policy shocks on material inputs, and (iii) macro-fiscal/forex constraints — most visibly compress the seasonal window in which farmers, irrigators, and relief institutions can still act?*

Units of observation (from public sources only, not a site shortlist): national/seasonal paddy and fertilizer accounts; Dry Zone village-tank / cascade irrigation as a material–institutional system; drought-management mandates described in GoSL planning documents.

## Source analyses (original; not brochure paraphrase)

### 1. FAO & WFP (2022). *Special Report — FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) to the Democratic Socialist Republic of Sri Lanka* (September 2022). Rome.

- **Publisher/authors:** Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations; World Food Programme  
- **Year:** 2022  
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.4060/cc1886en  
- **PDF:** https://www.fao.org/3/cc1886en/cc1886en.pdf  

**Analysis (capability / use / residuality / regeneration / pressure):**  
This mission report is the clearest public secondary trace of an **institutional/policy pressure** that shortened time-to-act inside a material–ecological production season. It records that on 6 May 2021 the government banned chemical-fertilizer imports to push exclusive organic agriculture (EOA); the restrictive window is placed roughly May–November 2021, after which international fertilizer prices were at historic highs and foreign-exchange scarcity kept agrochemicals tight. The mission forecasts 2022 paddy at **3 million tonnes** — a **42 percent** year-on-year decline and the **lowest level since the 2017 drought-affected output** — attributing the collapse mainly to low yields after reduced fertilizer/pesticide application. Maize (feed) is placed about **40 percent** below the five-year average.

For Anavaratha’s vocabulary: **use** of soil nutrient stocks and seed quality continued under a forced input regime; **residuals** included depleted fertilizer stocks, degraded seed-replacement cycles, and households shifting to thinner diets (mission food-security module); **regeneration** paths (organic substitution at national scale, restored chemical supply) were not ready inside one Maha/Yala cycle — so the **time-to-act** window closed mid-season rather than at the meteorological boundary. The same report also shows compound pressure: macro-crisis (rupee depreciation, fuel, medicine) stacked on the policy shock, so “pressure type” is not cleanly single-cause.

**Honesty gap:** CFSAM is a rapid assessment mission, not a controlled causal design; it does not isolate drought vs ban vs forex share of the yield loss for every district. Do not treat the 42% figure as a pure “fertilizer-ban effect.”

### 2. Ministry of Environment, Sri Lanka (2020). *National Drought Plan for Sri Lanka* (September 2020). Hosted via UNCCD.

- **Publisher/author:** Ministry of Environment, Government of Sri Lanka (UNCCD country drought-plan series)  
- **Year:** 2020  
- **URL:** https://www.unccd.int/sites/default/files/country_profile_documents/NDP%20of%20Sri%20Lanka-Final%20Report-Sept%202020.pdf  

**Analysis:**  
The NDP is institutional secondary synthesis that names **pressure subtypes** Sri Lanka already observes: meteorological → agricultural → hydrological → socioeconomic drought, with Dry Zone Maha/Yala paddy as the primary livelihood interface. It states drought is the **most frequent** among the 21 disasters listed in the Disaster Management Act No. 13 of 2005, and candidly describes mandates as **“sectoral and diffused”** — many agencies, mostly **reactive** relief rather than proactive mitigation. Material–institutional capability is located in cascade / minor irrigation systems (on the order of 13,000+ village tanks under Agrarian Development), Water Management Panel seasonal plans, and practices such as *Bethma* (partial command cultivation under shortage).

For Q1, this source matters because it shows **time-to-act is institutionally structured**: slow-onset drought compresses the window after a few months, while institutional fragmentation delays early action; conversely, seasonal operational plans and cascade hydrology are the observable “buffers” that buy time when they still work. Healthcare-cost literature cited in the plan (De Alwis and Noy, 2017) attributes a large share of disaster-related healthcare costs to drought — useful as pressure visibility, not as Anavaratha primary data.

**Honesty gap:** The plan is normative as well as descriptive; “ideal” coordination sections are aspirations. Historical drought-frequency claims inside the plan are secondary citations, not re-verified here.

### 3. World Bank (2025). *Project Appraisal Document: Integrated Rurban Development and Climate Resilience Project* (Report No. PADHI00638; 14 March 2025).

- **Publisher/author:** International Development Association / World Bank (Agriculture and Food Practice, South Asia)  
- **Year:** 2025  
- **URL:** https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099032425093021027/pdf/BOSIB-36bb07ec-d1fc-476b-9456-2e3f45db23f2.pdf  

**Analysis:**  
This PAD synthesizes recent secondary evidence on **climate shock pressure** and **institutional lock-in** that jointly shorten adaptive room. It reports the **2016/2017 drought reduced rice yields by 40 percent**, mainly in dry and intermediate zones, and cites the Climate Risk Index 2021 as ranking Sri Lanka the **30th** most climate-vulnerable country. On the institutional–material side, it states fertilizer subsidies were about **35 percent** of total agriculture expenditure (2017–2022), that the **2021 fertilizer import ban** was associated with a **31.5 percent** drop in paddy production (PAD’s figure; method not reproduced here), and that paddy-centric subsidies plus restrictions on converting paddy land reduce incentives to exit water-intensive use — while dry zones hold roughly **two-thirds** of farmland and most irrigation infrastructure. Cascade siltation and deferred rehabilitation are framed as degrading the water-storage buffer that buys time under drought/flood.

For Anavaratha: this is evidence that **pressure-type comparison is empirical** — climatic extremes visibly truncate seasons (yield collapse), while chronic institutional settings (subsidy/land-use rules) continuously shrink the set of regenerative moves available *before* the next shock. Post-harvest losses for fruits/vegetables are cited from secondary literature at **30–40 percent** — a residual pathway that locks value out of regeneration unless cold-chain/capability exists (Q2-adjacent, not proven here).

**Honesty gap:** A PAD is an investment document; it aggregates citations to justify a project. Treat numbers as reported secondary claims with cited provenance in the PAD, not as Anavaratha-collected statistics. Do not invent district-level attribution.

### 4. (Supporting institutional-response source) UNDP Climate Change Adaptation (project page). *Strengthening the Resilience of Smallholder Farmers in the Dry Zone to Climate Variability and Extreme Events* (CRIWMP / GCF; 2017–2024).

- **Publisher/author:** UNDP with Ministry of Environment / GCF framing  
- **Years covered:** 2017–2024 (current hub copy describes completed status)  
- **URL:** https://www.adaptation-undp.org/projects/strengthening-resilience-smallholder-farmers-dry-zone-climate-variability-and-extreme  

**Analysis:**  
Used only as **secondary institutional framing**, not as impact evaluation. The public project narrative positions Dry Zone village irrigation, cascade-level water security, drinking-water quality (including CKDu-linked concerns as stated by the page), and climate/hydrological forecasting as the bundle of capabilities that must be upgraded when drought/flood pressure rises. That alignment with the NDP’s cascade emphasis supports Q1’s observability claim: the same system class (VIS + cascade + advisories) recurs across GoSL and UN secondary sources as the place where shortened time-to-act becomes visible.

**Honesty gap:** Project pages mix description and advocacy. **No outcome statistics from this page are used as facts** in the judgment below. Field results, partner lists, or informal delivery claims are out of scope for this desk pass.
