---

## Frame A (current / affirmative)

**Orientation:** Life buys time under pressure through cycles of use → residuality → regeneration.

**Implicit picture:**
- Pressure is an external or environmental force that threatens systems.
- Capability is a stock that can be spent and restored.
- Residuals are intermediate states between spending and recovery.
- Regeneration is the hopeful, often ethical, terminus of the cycle — the way systems endure.
- The research task is to trace how endurance works so that, later, interventions might support it.

**Strength:** Makes the cycle legible; matches the living-doc premise; gives Sensemaking a constructive object (models of how capability persists).

**Blind spot:** Treats regeneration as the telos of the cycle and pressure as mostly “what happens to” the system. Can slide into a repair narrative without noticing when “regen” talk is what *authorizes* continued loss.

---

## Frame B (invert / negative)

**Orientation:** Pressure is how the system *appears*; residuals are the real structure; regeneration is often the fantasy that permits continued spending and loss. Research must also show where residuals **refuse** the regeneration myth.

**Implicit picture:**
- What we call “pressure” is not only an outside shock. It is the form in which a system’s contradictions become visible — fiscal, climatic, institutional, extractive. The system *shows up as* pressure.
- Residuals (waste, depleted stocks, institutional scars, latent skill, damaged habitat, frozen SOPs) are not waiting rooms for recovery. They are the actual architecture of what the system has become.
- “Regeneration” frequently functions as a **covering fantasy**: a story that spending can continue because restoration is assumed, promised, or aestheticized. The fantasy is not always a lie in content; it is a lie in *function* when it blocks seeing irreversible or trapped residuals.
- Some residuals do not regenerate. They refuse. That refusal is evidence, not failure of method.

**Strength:** Prevents Sensemaking from becoming a regeneration brochure. Forces evidence classes that can falsify hope narratives. Aligns with “never ship fake readiness.”

**Blind spot:** Pure B can become cynicism — every regen claim dismissed a priori. That also invents readiness (readiness to denounce). Dual optic required.

## Torsion (how A and B scrape each other)

| Moment | A says | B says | Productive scrape |
|--------|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Pressure | Threat to capability | Mode of appearance of the system’s structure | Ask *which* pressures are shocks vs *which* are the system’s own form made visible |
| Use | Spending of capability | Production of the residual structure | Track use not only as depletion but as **writing** the residual world |
| Residuality | Phase between use and regen | The real structure | Prioritize residual inventories over regen success stories |
| Regeneration | How life buys time | Fantasy that can license further use/loss | Split regen into (i) evidenced recomposition and (ii) narrative cover; require criteria to tell them apart |
| “Buying time” | Endurance under stress | Time bought *for whom*, often by displacing cost into residuals elsewhere | Follow displaced residuals (people, places, budgets) that make “time” appear for someone else |

**Rule for analysis:** Every A-path claim needs a B-path stress test. Every B-path refusal needs an A-path check against actual recomposition evidence (so cynicism does not fake itself as rigor).
