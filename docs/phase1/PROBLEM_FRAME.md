## In our words

Anavaratha is a Sri Lanka–scoped research practice that asks how **capability** persists or erodes when materials, institutions, people, and ecosystems are under pressure. It traces three linked movements: **use** (capability is spent), **residuality** (what remains after spending — depleted stocks, waste streams, latent skill, institutional memory, damaged habitat), and **regeneration** (how capability is restored, recomposed, or substituted).

The public tagline *“Understanding how life buys time under pressure”* is treated here as a research orientation, not a slogan: pressure shortens the window in which systems can still act; the work is to make that window and its mechanics visible before anyone claims a right to intervene.

## Working claim (falsifiable, private)

Sri Lankan socio-material systems lose and recover capability through patterned cycles that can be evidenced without inventing readiness. If we cannot name units of analysis, evidence types, and kill criteria for questions, we stay in Sensemaking and do not move stage.
