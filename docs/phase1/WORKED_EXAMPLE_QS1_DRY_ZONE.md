## Generative socio-ecological form (named first)

**Name of the form:**  
**Dry Zone cascade–paddy seasonal composition** — a living socio-ecological form in which ephemeral-rivulet hydrology, linked village tanks, shared command cultivation, seasonal water-allocation planning, and seed/skill/labor cultures **compose** usable water–soil–crop order across Maha and Yala cycles.

This is the object Frame C requires before underside pressure typing. It is not a slogan and not a project brand. It is the patterned increase of usable order that public secondary sources already describe when they are read for *what the season is trying to enact*, not only for what interrupts it.

### Units of aliveness (composition, not brochure)

1. **Cascade hydrology timing**  
   The National Drought Plan (NDP 2020) defines a cascade as a “connected series of tanks organized within a micro-catchment of the Dry Zone landscape storing, conveying and utilizing water from an ephemeral rivulet,” each cascade a distinct small watershed on the order of 13–26 km² (citing Panabokke et al.). Village tanks (on the order of **13,141** updated systems under the Department of Agrarian Development; NDP also cites ~12,000–15,000 minor reservoirs) are not isolated ponds: they are a **linked storage–conveyance pattern**. That linkage is syntropic form — usable order composed across a micro-catchment so that rainfall timing, tank-to-tank conveyance, and command release can still make a cultivation season possible when rainfall alone would not. The World Bank PAD (2025) restates the same anatomy in investment language: ‘cascade systems’ as “small community-managed watersheds that include water storage tanks (artificial lakes), paddy command areas, upland farming zones, and associated watersheds.” UNDP Dry Zone framing (CRIWMP / GCF project page) likewise treats the cascade / sub-basin and interconnected village irrigation systems as the unit that must be upgraded if water security is to hold — institutional framing that the *form* is cascade-level composition, not single-tank relief.

2. **Shared cultivation as composition (*Bethma*)**  
   Under Frame C, *Bethma* is read first as **adaptive composition of the command**, not only as shortage coping. The NDP records that when allocated water falls short of demand, communities practice *Bethma*: only a portion of the irrigable extent is cultivated (citing Goonasekere and Gamage, 1999), alongside crop-pattern change and agreed extension of irrigation intervals. The generative reading: the community recomposes who cultivates which water-reachable land so that a season remains *possible* under constrained storage — usable order is renegotiated rather than abandoned. (Public secondary sources also describe *Bethma* historically as temporary proportional redistribution of head-end fields toward tail-enders under scarcity; that detail is used here only as form-description from open literature already aligned with the NDP’s naming of the practice — not as Anavaratha field observation.)

3. **Seasonal Water Management planning**  
   The NDP describes a Water Management Panel (WMP, established 1985 at MASL) assisted by a Water Management Secretariat that prepares a **System / Seasonal Operation Plan (SOP)** at the beginning of each season — hydrology, crop water requirement, energy, system status, and maintenance folded into an allocation programme discussed at a pre-seasonal WMP meeting with agencies and farmer representatives. At scheme level, *Kanna* meetings and Project Management Committees further adjust cultivation plans when water is short. This is institutional **composition of order before the monsoon settles**: usable water timing is planned into the season rather than discovered only after failure. Syntropy here is not “good governance” rhetoric; it is the secondary record of a seasonal planning form that tries to keep generative capacity (irrigated area, cropping intensity, shared burden) coherent across actors.

4. **Seed / skill / labor cultures that make a season possible**  
   CFSAM (FAO/WFP 2022) documents the ordinary generative metabolism of the season when it still works: Maha (roughly Sep–Mar) and Yala (roughly Apr–Sep) as the twin cropping rhythm; smallholder paddy holdings (about **70 percent** under 0.8 ha); seed sourced largely from **retained production, farmer-to-farmer exchange, or certified seed** via Provincial Department of Agriculture / registered local seed farmers; long-run yield gains historically tied to improved varieties, irrigation extension, and input supply that brought the country near rice self-sufficiency by around 2010. Those are not “success stories” pasted from a flyer — they are evidence that a season is a **composed capability**: seed stocks, labor (including households’ capacity to plant, transplant, harvest), irrigation timing, and soil-nutrient use must cohere inside one window. When CFSAM later reports that poor Maha/Yala yields raised concerns about **quantity and quality of retained paddy seed** for the next season, that sentence only makes sense because retained seed *is* part of the generative form — a living stock that carries capability across cycles.

**What is alive in the form (one-line):**  
A Dry Zone season is alive when cascade storage timing, shared command composition (*Bethma* and allied adjustments), seasonal allocation plans, and seed/skill/labor cultures successfully **increase usable order** — water that can still irrigate, land that can still be planted fairly under constraint, and seed/labor that can still open the next cycle — rather than merely delaying collapse.

## Source analyses (original; generative reading first)

Same four public sources as `WORKED_EXAMPLE_Q1.md`. Analyses below are original and **syntropy-primary**; they are not brochure paraphrase and not a second pressure catalog.

### 1. FAO & WFP (2022). *Special Report — FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) to the Democratic Socialist Republic of Sri Lanka* (September 2022). Rome.

- **Publisher/authors:** Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations; World Food Programme  
- **Year:** 2022  
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.4060/cc1886en  
- **PDF:** https://www.fao.org/3/cc1886en/cc1886en.pdf  

**Generative reading (capability / composition / aliveness):**  
CFSAM’s production narrative implies a prior living composition: monsoon-timed Maha/Yala plantings, irrigation-networked paddy, retained and exchanged seed, and household labor–input metabolism that had, in recent favorable years (2018–2021 as the report frames them), supported rising paddy output under adequate fertilizer, pesticide, and irrigation-water availability. The mission’s attention to **seed availability** (retained stocks, exchanges, certified channels) treats seed culture as a cross-season generative stock — the unit that must remain coherent if the *next* Maha is to open. Yield and area accounts are therefore not only damage meters; they are traces of whether the season’s composition held.

**Honesty gap:** CFSAM is a rapid assessment under crisis conditions. It does not map cascade-level hydrology or name *Bethma*; generative form must be inferred from seasonal production, seed, and input metabolism, then paired with NDP/PAD cascade language. Do not invent district cascade case studies from CFSAM.

### 2. Ministry of Environment, Sri Lanka (2020). *National Drought Plan for Sri Lanka* (September 2020). Hosted via UNCCD.

- **Publisher/author:** Ministry of Environment, Government of Sri Lanka (UNCCD country drought-plan series)  
- **Year:** 2020  
- **URL:** https://www.unccd.int/sites/default/files/country_profile_documents/NDP%20of%20Sri%20Lanka-Final%20Report-Sept%202020.pdf  

**Generative reading:**  
This is the richest public secondary source in the Q1 set for **naming the form**. Cascade definition and micro-catchment scale; ~13,000+ village tanks under Agrarian Development; minor irrigation as roughly one-quarter of paddy land and production; WMP / WMS / SOP seasonal planning; farmer organizations and *Kanna* platforms; *Bethma*, crop diversification, and extended intervals as collective adjustments; historical framing of Dry Zone irrigated rice as a “hydraulic civilization” pattern that stored Dry Zone rain *and* Wet Zone runoff. Read under C: these are descriptions of **how usable order is composed** — storage, conveyance, allocation, shared burden — not merely of drought pathology. Even the plan’s candid line that mandates are “sectoral and diffused” and mostly reactive is underside relative to the generative objects it has already named (cascade, SOP, *Bethma*, MIS).

**Honesty gap:** The NDP mixes description with normative “ideal” coordination. Cascade rehabilitation mentions and “ongoing projects” annex material are institutional claims, not Anavaratha impact evaluation. Historical drought-frequency citations inside the plan are secondary and not re-verified here.

### 3. World Bank (2025). *Project Appraisal Document: Integrated Rurban Development and Climate Resilience Project* (Report No. PADHI00638; 14 March 2025).

- **Publisher/author:** International Development Association / World Bank (Agriculture and Food Practice, South Asia)  
- **Year:** 2025  
- **URL:** https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099032425093021027/pdf/BOSIB-36bb07ec-d1fc-476b-9456-2e3f45db23f2.pdf  

**Generative reading:**  
The PAD’s cascade paragraph is Frame-C-usable: community-managed watersheds knitting tanks, paddy command, upland zones, and associated catchments — i.e., a **socio-ecological composition unit**. Proposed Cascade Management Plans / Committees and rehabilitation language presume that restoring storage and governance can recover generative capacity (reliable supply, water-use efficiency, landscape management). Dry zones holding roughly **two-thirds** of farmland and the bulk of irrigation infrastructure locate where that form matters nationally. Historical irrigation-plus-subsidy investment that raised irrigated area to around **0.6 million ha** (~70 percent of paddy area) is secondary evidence that material–institutional composition *has* increased usable order over decades — before the PAD turns to siltation, deferred rehab, and policy lock-in as underside.

**Honesty gap:** A PAD aggregates citations to justify investment. Treat cascade-management proposals as planned institutional instruments, not as proven Anavaratha outcomes. Do not invent recovery-season statistics or CMC performance numbers not demonstrated in the PAD text used here.
