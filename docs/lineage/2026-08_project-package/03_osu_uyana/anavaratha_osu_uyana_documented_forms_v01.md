# Anavaratha

## Osu Uyana Living-System Inquiry

**Documented Sri Lankan garden forms — Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This register maps documented contemporary forms relevant to the Osu Uyana inquiry. It does not assume that every medicinal garden is an Osu Uyana, nor that the term describes a single national institutional model. Each form is recorded according to its source, function, scale, stewardship, ecological condition, and evidence limits.

## 3. Ganewatta as a full living-system example

The official Department of National Botanic Gardens page presents Ganewatta as more than a plant collection. It describes a garden with ex situ conservation, a medicinal-plant gene pool, commercial cultivation, education, research, systematic cultivation, propagation nurseries, seed preservation, a mini herbarium, a library, and bee-keeping to promote pollinator conservation [1].

This makes Ganewatta a strong candidate for a living-system case study because several layers are visible in one institutional record:

| Layer | Ganewatta evidence or question |
|---|---|
| **Landscape** | Intermediate Zone, approximately 25 km from Kurunegala, 21.04 hectares |
| **Habitat and conditions** | Tropical climate with a distinct dry season; average temperature reported as 25–30°C; soil and water management require further verification |
| **Living collection** | More than 300 medicinal-plant species, mother plants, water plants, traditional fruit sections, and other collections |
| **Propagation** | Three nurseries reported to produce more than 30,000 plants annually |
| **Knowledge** | Education and research for traditional-medicine students, Ayurvedic doctors, botanists, school students, scientists, and the public |
| **Seed continuity** | Seed room and medicinal-plant seed-preservation activity reported |
| **Relationships** | Bee-keeping is described as promoting pollinator conservation; the full ecological relationship requires further evidence |
| **Stewardship** | Department of National Botanic Gardens institutional responsibility; operational details remain open |

The figures and functions above are claims from the official page and should not be generalised to every medicinal garden or Osu Uyana. They also do not establish that Ganewatta is formally called Osu Uyana.

## 4. What “Osu Uyana” currently appears to cover

The evidence supports treating Osu Uyana as a **plural contemporary field of practice**, not a single fixed institution. It may refer to herbal or medicinal gardens connected to production, ex situ conservation, demonstration, education, Ayurveda, community activity, or local cultivation. The same phrase may carry different institutional and cultural meanings in different locations.

| Possible meaning | Evidence position |
|---|---|
| **Medicinal or herbal garden** | Supported by contemporary Sinhala usage and official SLDAC material [2] |
| **Production garden** | Supported for some SLDAC and Ganewatta functions, but not every Osu Uyana [1] [2] |
| **Demonstration or education garden** | Supported by community/school source leads and Ganewatta’s education function [1] [3] |
| **Conservation garden or seed resource** | Supported for Ganewatta’s ex situ and seed-preservation functions [1] |
| **Community or household garden** | Plausible and supported as a research pathway; exact term usage requires primary verification |
| **Ancient named institution** | Not established |

## 5. Immediate research questions

The next source work should compare at least three contemporary forms in detail: Ganewatta as an official medicinal-plant and ex situ garden, an SLDAC herbal-garden site using Osu Uyana terminology, and a village or school medicinal-plant plot. The comparison should ask who governs each garden, what is cultivated, what is conserved, how seeds and knowledge move, which non-plant organisms are present, how water and soil are managed, what seasonal rhythms matter, and what forms of care or restraint are practised.

No species recommendation follows from this register. The purpose is to understand the garden as a living and knowledge system before any future cultivation decision.
