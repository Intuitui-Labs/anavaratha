# Anavaratha

## Institutional and spatial source verification

**Cultivation-branch source pathway notes — Version 0.1**

## 1. Department of Forest Conservation

**Official page:** [Department of Forest Conservation](https://www.env.gov.lk/web/index.php/en/department-of-forest-conservation)

The Ministry of Environment page states that the Department’s objectives include conserving forests for posterity with regard to biodiversity, soils, water, and historical, cultural, religious, and aesthetic values; increasing forest tree cover and productivity; and strengthening contributions to rural welfare and the national economy. The page identifies a technical division for **Geographical Information Systems & Forest Inventory**, alongside biodiversity and watershed management, environment management and georesources, planning and monitoring, social forestry and extension, and silviculture and forest plantation management.

The page also describes a regional structure of five forest regions, 23 forest divisions, 81 forest ranges, and further field subdivisions. This is relevant to Anavaratha because future requests for inventory, GIS, forest management, or local verification should be addressed through the appropriate institutional and geographic level rather than treating the Forest Department as a single undifferentiated source.

The official page provides the Department contact address, telephone numbers, email, and a link to the Department’s own website. It does not itself provide a downloadable current GIS catalogue or a complete inventory dataset. The next verification step is therefore to inspect the Department’s own publications, downloads, GIS/inventory pages, and any access conditions before treating a map or inventory product as available for atlas reuse.

**Access status:** Institutional pathway verified; current downloadable GIS/inventory products not yet verified.

**Source date note:** The page displayed a last-modified date of 21 August 2026 when accessed on 22 August 2026.

## 2. Department of Wildlife Conservation

**Official page:** [Protected Areas – Department of Wildlife Conservation](https://www.dwc.gov.lk/?page_id=72)

The DWC page states that protected areas are administered by the Department of Wildlife Conservation and the Forest Department, and that the Fauna & Flora Protection Ordinance provides the legal framework for fauna, flora, and habitat protection. It describes protected-area categories including Strict Natural Reserve, National Park, Nature Reserve, Jungle Corridor, Marine National Park, Marine Reserve, Buffer Zone, and Sanctuary or Managed Elephant Reserve.

The page reports two declared jungle corridors—Kavuulla–Minneriya and Nelugala—and explains their purpose as facilitating animal movement between protected areas and helping reduce habitat-fragmentation pressures. It also states that access to certain protected areas, including strict natural reserves, requires permission from the Director General for authorised scientific research or official duties.

The page provides current category counts and a reported protected-area coverage figure, but the page also contains an internal wording inconsistency in the National Park count (“Twenty Eight (25)”). These figures should therefore be treated as page-reported values requiring date- and definition-aware checking, not copied as unquestioned national totals.

The page does not itself provide downloadable GIS layers or a clear spatial-data licence. The DWC institutional pathway and protected-area/corridor concepts are verified; current map layers, metadata, access permissions, and sensitive-location rules remain to be confirmed through DWC or an authorised data gateway.

**Access status:** Protected-area and corridor institutional pathway verified; downloadable GIS layers and reuse conditions not yet verified.

## 3. National Wetland Directory

**Official gateway:** [National Wetland Directory of Sri Lanka – Sri Lanka Biodiversity Clearing-House](https://lk.chm-cbd.net/documents/national-wetland-directory-sri-lanka)

The Sri Lanka Biodiversity Clearing-House identifies the National Wetland Directory as a national report and provides a downloadable 9.72 MB PDF. The page describes the directory as an updated national information resource developed in the context of Sri Lanka’s National Wetlands Policy, the National Wetlands Steering Committee, the need for better wetland information, and the Central Environmental Authority’s wetland-management work.

The page links onward to the IUCN Library System record and provides the PDF as a direct file. The directory is therefore a verified source pathway for wetland research, but the directory’s age, underlying survey methods, spatial precision, update status, metadata, and reuse conditions must be examined in the PDF and any associated database or institutional records before atlas integration.

**Access status:** Directory gateway and downloadable PDF verified; full directory extraction, spatial metadata, update status, and reuse conditions not yet verified.

## 4. National Herbarium

**Official page:** [National Herbarium – Department of National Botanic Gardens](https://botanicgardens.gov.lk/service/national-herbarium/)

The National Herbarium page describes the Herbarium as the national institution responsible for authentication of Sri Lankan plants, plant exploration, taxonomic investigation, and documentation of the country’s flora. It states that the institution provides facilities to local and foreign researchers for taxonomy-related research and assists with correct plant identification. It also describes involvement in the preparation of Sri Lanka’s National Red List and in surveys targeting threatened plant species.

The page lists public services including plant identification and information, plant authentication and voucher-specimen preservation, plant exploration and specimen collection, documentation and publication, specialist support for plant groups, education, exchange of information and material, and library facilities. It also names publications such as national and district floras, field guides, checklists, and materials on medicinal and threatened plants.

The page provides a general institutional contact route and multilingual access, but it does not provide a public searchable specimen catalogue or a detailed online request procedure. The Herbarium pathway is therefore verified for taxonomic authentication, voucher evidence, and researcher contact; specific specimen access, digitised records, fees, permissions, collection restrictions, and data-reuse conditions remain to be confirmed directly.

**Access status:** Institutional role and services verified; catalogue, specimen-access procedure, and digitised-data pathway not yet verified.

## 5. Central Environmental Authority

**Official home page:** [Central Environment Authority](https://www.cea.lk/web/en)

The current CEA home page exposes links or entries for the National Wetland Directory, Red List of Threatened Fauna and Flora of Sri Lanka, National Wetland Database, National Environmental Information Centre, GIS/remote-sensing support, Natural Resources Management & Monitoring, Water Quality Monitoring, Solid Waste Management, and related environmental information services.

This verifies that the CEA is an important institutional gateway for the cultivation branch’s wetland, biodiversity, environmental-information, and spatial-research needs. The home page does not by itself establish that the National Wetland Database is publicly queryable or downloadable; the database entry and any linked system must be inspected separately. The same caution applies to GIS/remote-sensing products and Red List materials: the existence of a link is not evidence that the underlying dataset is current, open, spatially precise, or reusable.

**Access status:** CEA information gateways verified; National Wetland Database contents, public queryability, metadata, update frequency, and reuse conditions not yet verified.

## 7. DWC research permissions

**Official page:** [Research Permissions – Department of Wildlife Conservation](https://www.dwc.gov.lk/?page_id=433)

The DWC states that activities regulated by the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance require a permit, including research involving protected species or conducted in protected areas. It provides a downloadable application route and an online research portal. The page says applications are generally processed within six weeks, but proposals should reach the Director’s office at least two months before proposed activities; long-term intensive proposals may require at least four months for scrutiny.

The page requires the principal investigator to work in or collaborate with a recognised scientific institution, and it requires foreign researchers to collaborate with a local institution or DWC. Proposals must include researcher information, specimens proposed for collection, and a work schedule. The page states that collection of fauna and flora specimens in protected areas should be avoided unless extremely necessary, and that collection of rare, endangered, threatened, status-unknown, or undescribed species is restricted. It also requires monitoring, notification to protected-area authorities, reporting, acknowledgment of DWC collaboration, and separate permission for filming, animal capture, night work, temporary structures, or specimen import/export.

This is an important field-entry constraint for Anavaratha. The current desk phase does not itself require field permits, but any later verification involving protected areas, protected species, collection, capture, filming, or sensitive locations must be designed around DWC permission, local institutional collaboration, non-extractive methods, and the possibility that observation or existing data is preferable to collection.

**Access status:** Permit requirements and online application pathway verified; no permit action is being initiated at this desk stage.

## 8. Protected Planet comparative spatial layer

**Country profile:** [Protected Planet – Sri Lanka](https://www.protectedplanet.net/country/LKA)

The August 2026 country profile identifies 685 total protected areas for Sri Lanka, 10 with management-effectiveness evaluations, and a 99% polygon/1% point ratio. It cites the UNEP-WCMC World Database on Protected Areas (WDPA) and provides links to WDPA, resources, legal information, and the wider Protected Planet system.

This is a useful comparative spatial-data pathway for checking names, boundaries, and global reporting, but it is not a substitute for DWC or Forest Department authority. The atlas should record the WDPA version/date, compare it with national legal and institutional sources, and avoid assuming that global coverage, boundaries, management status, or category definitions are fully equivalent to Sri Lankan official records.

**Access status:** Current comparative country profile verified; detailed layer download, record-level metadata, and reconciliation with national sources remain to be completed.
