# Anavaratha

## Source-map protocol for Sri Lankan regenerative cultivation

**Living-sector research — Working protocol, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

The Anavaratha source map is a discovery and orientation tool. Its purpose is to locate existing catalogues, datasets, repositories, reports, maps, monitoring systems, species records, ecosystem studies, restoration cases, and knowledge resources before creating new research products.

The source map should answer:

> **Who already knows what, where is that knowledge held, how can it be accessed, what does it cover, and how can Anavaratha use it responsibly?**

It is not a claim that every source is complete, current, open, or equally reliable. It is a way to make the existing knowledge landscape visible.

## 2. Source-map categories

| Category | Typical source holders | Relevant material |
|---|---|---|
| **Environmental monitoring** | Central Environmental Authority and related public bodies | Water, air, soil, environmental quality, compliance, monitoring, and technical reports |
| **Wildlife and protected areas** | Department of Wildlife Conservation, IUCN, conservation partners | Species, habitats, protected areas, wildlife surveys, recovery plans, corridors, and management plans |
| **Forests and trees** | Forest Department, universities, arboreta, botanical institutions | Forest inventories, tree catalogues, flora, natural regeneration, restoration, seed, and silviculture |
| **Biodiversity clearing-house** | Sri Lanka Biodiversity / CBD national mechanisms | National reports, projects, biodiversity indicators, protected areas, species, ecosystems, and policy |
| **Universities and repositories** | University of Colombo, Peradeniya, Sri Jayewardenepura, Jaffna, Ruhuna, Rajarata, and other repositories | Theses, journals, field studies, species records, ecological surveys, and local research |
| **Medicinal and ethnobotanical knowledge** | Department of Ayurveda, universities, medicinal gardens, community and knowledge holders | Medicinal-plant lists, ethnobotany, cultivation, traditional knowledge, conservation, and biocultural use |
| **Spatial and ecological data** | Survey Department, GIS repositories, CBD, IWMI, FAO, World Bank, remote-sensing platforms | Ecological zones, soils, watersheds, land cover, elevation, climate, habitats, and social-spatial layers |
| **Agriculture and food systems** | Department of Agriculture, FAO, universities, farmer networks | Crop diversity, homegardens, agroforestry, seed systems, soil, water, nutrition, and farmer knowledge |
| **Wetlands, coasts, and blue systems** | Coast Conservation, CEA, universities, IUCN, UN agencies, marine institutions | Mangroves, lagoons, wetlands, seagrass, coral, fisheries, coastal habitat, and restoration |
| **Restoration and stewardship projects** | UN Decade, NGOs, community organisations, private initiatives, research programmes | Restoration cases, methods, outcomes, participation, financing, monitoring, and lessons learned |
| **Biocultural and historical knowledge** | Communities, archives, museums, religious institutions, oral-history projects, scholars | Local names, stories, ritual, cultural landscape, historical use, and memory |

## 3. Minimum source record

Each source should receive a record containing the following fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Source ID** | Unique reference for cross-linking |
| **Title** | Exact title of the resource |
| **Institution or holder** | Organisation, community, researcher, or repository |
| **Link or access path** | Public URL, repository path, catalogue reference, or contact route |
| **Source type** | Catalogue, dataset, map, report, article, thesis, project, archive, testimony, or other |
| **Domain** | Forest, tree, soil, water, biodiversity, wetland, agriculture, urban, medicinal, stewardship, or other |
| **Geography** | Site, district, ecological zone, watershed, coastal area, national, or other |
| **Time coverage** | Publication date, observation period, historical range, or update cycle |
| **Content summary** | What the source actually contains |
| **Data granularity** | Species, plot, site, district, basin, national, or other |
| **Authority** | Why the holder or method is credible for this subject |
| **Accessibility** | Open, partial, restricted, offline, broken, or contact-required |
| **Reuse conditions** | Licence, attribution, consent, privacy, sensitive-location, or other restrictions |
| **Currency** | Current, dated, irregularly updated, or unknown |
| **Completeness** | Broad, targeted, representative, partial, or unknown |
| **Relationship to atlas** | Directly reusable, reference only, requires extraction, or gap lead |
| **Verification status** | Unchecked, inspected, cross-checked, or verified |
| **Next action** | Read, download, request access, compare, cite, or exclude |

## 4. Assessment dimensions

Sources should be evaluated across five dimensions rather than assigned one undifferentiated quality score.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | Who produced or holds the material, and what is their relationship to the subject? |
| **Relevance** | Does it address the living being, relationship, habitat, or condition under study? |
| **Resolution** | Is its spatial, temporal, taxonomic, and functional detail suitable for the question? |
| **Currency** | How old is the evidence, and is the system likely to have changed? |
| **Access and ethics** | Can it be accessed and reused without violating law, privacy, consent, or species protection? |

## 5. Source relationship types

A source may play different roles in the research process:

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Anchor** | Establishes an authoritative baseline or official definition |
| **Catalogue** | Provides records of species, habitats, places, projects, or observations |
| **Dataset** | Provides structured data suitable for comparison or analysis |
| **Context** | Explains history, policy, method, or institutional setting |
| **Case** | Documents a particular restoration, stewardship, or ecological relationship |
| **Validation** | Helps cross-check another source or claim |
| **Gap lead** | Shows that important information exists but is incomplete or inaccessible |
| **Knowledge relationship** | Holds local, traditional, cultural, or community knowledge requiring ethical engagement |

## 6. Anti-duplication rule

Before creating a new catalogue or dataset, Anavaratha should ask whether the desired information already exists in another form. If it does, the project should prefer one of five actions: link to it, cite it, clean or structure it with permission, build a synthesis layer, or document the gap that prevents reuse.

A new resource is justified only when it adds a clearly stated value, such as connecting previously separate catalogues, translating between ecological and community scales, making a source findable, documenting a missing relationship, or creating a responsibly governed synthesis.

## 7. Source-map outputs

The source-map phase should produce:

1. an institutional directory;
2. a catalogue and dataset register;
3. a subject-to-source matrix;
4. a Sri Lankan geography and scale matrix;
5. an access and reuse register;
6. a source-age and currency view;
7. a duplication and synthesis plan;
8. an uncertainty and missing-knowledge register; and
9. a priority reading path for the living-sector atlas.

## 8. Working principle

> **Do not begin by asking what Anavaratha should create. Begin by asking what already exists, who sustains it, what it makes possible, and what remains disconnected or missing.**

This principle makes Anavaratha a connector and sense-making practice rather than another isolated repository.
