## 3. Source status labels

Every source-map entry should carry one of the following labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Anchor** | Use as a primary institutional or authoritative basis for a specific claim |
| **Catalogue** | Use for structured records, lists, names, or statuses |
| **Context** | Use to understand history, policy, method, or landscape |
| **Case** | Use to learn from a place-specific project or study |
| **Cross-check** | Use to compare and validate another source |
| **Lead** | Use to locate a source, person, dataset, or unresolved question |
| **Restricted** | Do not reuse or publish without permission, consent, or legal review |

## 4. Evaluation rule

A source becomes **atlas-ready** only when its original page or document has been inspected; its title, holder, date, method, geography, and scope are recorded; its claims are extracted accurately; its licence or access conditions are known; and its limitations are attached to the resulting atlas record.

> **A link is not yet knowledge. A catalogue is not yet context. A source becomes useful when its scope, authority, limits, and relationships are understood.**
