# Anavaratha

## Tree, wetland, ecological-zone, and stewardship crosswalk

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working crosswalk, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This crosswalk connects structural trees, wetlands, ecological orientation layers, and the institutions and communities responsible for care. It is designed to prevent spatial or administrative records from being mistaken for complete ecological understanding.

## 6. Ecological-zone rule

The FAO GAEZ Sri Lanka layer identifies Dry, Intermediate, and Wet zones based on rainfall, soil, and terrain [6]. It is useful as an initial orientation layer, but its source is attributed to a 1979 Department of Agriculture source, its current dataset contains three records, and its metadata states that no licence is provided and permission should be requested for use [6].

The crosswalk must therefore treat ecological zones as a starting frame, not a final ecological diagnosis. Every tree, wetland, medicinal plant, or relationship record should be further connected to local soil, water, elevation, habitat, seasonality, land use, and community context.

## 7. Stewardship responsibility map

| Research or care question | First institutional route | Complementary knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| Plant identity or threatened flora | National Herbarium | University botanists and local names |
| Forest, tree, or watershed management | Forest Department | Communities, farmers, researchers, and local institutions |
| Protected species or protected area | DWC | Local communities, conservation partners, and species specialists |
| Wetland catalogue or management | CEA and CBD/Clearing-House | Wetland users, local studies, IWMI, and community observation |
| Ecological-zone orientation | FAO GAEZ and Department of Agriculture pathway | Soil, watershed, climate, and local ecological studies |
| Medicinal-plant knowledge | National Herbarium, Ayurveda and practitioner pathways | Knowledge holders, ethical community research, and conservation studies |
| Restoration method | Existing IUCN, UN, IWMI, university, and community cases | Long-term monitoring and local stewardship evidence |

## 8. What the crosswalk prevents

The crosswalk prevents several common errors: treating a tree catalogue as a habitat model; treating a protected-area boundary as proof of habitat integrity; treating a wetland directory as a current condition assessment; treating an ecological-zone layer as a species-selection guide; treating an institutional mandate as evidence of implementation; and treating a restoration project’s stated objective as a verified outcome.

## 9. Next actions

The next extraction should obtain current versions of Forest Department GIS and inventory resources, verify DWC protected-area and corridor layers, inspect the full National Wetland Directory, identify CEA wetland-database access, and connect tree and wetland records to specific atlas relationships. All access, licensing, permit, consent, and sensitive-location conditions should be recorded with the resulting crosswalk.

> **A responsible crosswalk connects place, life, knowledge, and authority without pretending that any one of them is the whole landscape.**
