# Anavaratha

## Regenerative ecological cultivation branch

**Branch index within the larger project home — Sri Lanka, desk-first, Version 0.1**

## 1. Branch identity

The regenerative ecological cultivation branch is one of Anavaratha’s two principal directions. It is responsible for understanding and eventually strengthening the conditions through which living systems and the communities connected to them can flourish, regenerate, and continue.

This branch is currently in its **desk research, sense-making, and knowledge-building phase**. Its documents are not a field plan, planting prescription, species catalogue, or medical guide. They form an evolving research infrastructure that should support responsible decisions later, if and when the evidence and relationships justify action.

## 2. Branch question

> **What conditions allow Sri Lankan living systems and their communities of care to regenerate, and where could proportionate cultivation strengthen those conditions without reducing life to a checklist?**

The branch studies relationships among landscapes, habitats, watersheds, organisms, communities, institutions, knowledge, time, and care. It treats cultivation as more than adding plants. Cultivation means helping conditions become more capable of sustaining life.

## 4. How the branch fits Anavaratha

The branch is nested under the larger project home rather than standing beside it as an independent project. Its relationship to the wider architecture is:

```text
Anavaratha project home
└── Shared desk-first foundation
    ├── Regenerative ecological cultivation branch
    │   ├── Current active research stream
    │   ├── Sri Lanka eagle-eye and domain studies
    │   ├── Living-sector and relationship atlas
    │   └── Future proportionate cultivation pathways
    │
    └── Waste and material responsibility branch
        ├── Separate future desk-research architecture
        ├── Material-flow and responsibility studies
        └── Future proportionate waste-related pathways
```

The waste direction may later be linked to cultivation where a genuine relationship exists—for example, when material conditions affect soil, water, habitats, or communities. Such links should be explicit cross-links, not an uncontrolled merging of documents.

## 5. Current branch maturity

The branch is best described as **connected but not yet testable across all possible sites**. It has moved beyond a general idea because it has a national context, domain taxonomy, source infrastructure, relational descent, prototype atlas, and explicit limitations. It has not yet earned a specific field action because key source verification, local knowledge, seasonal understanding, permissions, stewardship relationships, and site-level conditions remain incomplete.

| Maturity level | Branch position |
|---|---|
| **Mapped** | Achieved at a broad Sri Lankan and domain level |
| **Characterised** | Substantially developed, but uneven across domains and source types |
| **Connected** | Active through the relationship atlas, crosswalks, and living-sector model |
| **Testable** | Not yet established for a particular field opportunity |
| **Actionable** | Not yet appropriate to claim |

## 6. Active desk priorities

The next work should strengthen the evidence system rather than expand into premature intervention. Priority areas are the row-level extraction and visual validation of the 2025 medicinal-plant checklist; current National Herbarium catalogue and specimen access; Forest Department GIS and inventory products; DWC protected-area and corridor layers; the National Wetland Directory and database metadata; primary or local-language clarification of “Osu Uyana”; a fully supported Sri Lankan fruiting-plant and seed-disperser relationship; and seasonal records.

Every expansion should retain source, date, method, geography, confidence, licence or permission, sensitive-location protection, consent and attribution, and the distinction between direct evidence and Anavaratha interpretation.

## 7. Branch principle

> **The cultivation branch is not a planting programme looking for a site. It is a disciplined process for learning where and how cultivation might strengthen living continuity.**

Its present task is therefore to make relationships more legible without pretending that legibility equals control. The branch should remain open to revision, local authority, ecological complexity, and the possibility that the appropriate action may be protection, non-intervention, natural regeneration, knowledge-sharing, or care rather than planting.
