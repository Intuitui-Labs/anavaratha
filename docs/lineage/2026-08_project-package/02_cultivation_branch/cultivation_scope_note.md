# Anavaratha regenerative cultivation workstream

## Scope clarification

This workstream focuses exclusively on **regenerative cultivation in Sri Lanka**. Its subject is the creation and renewal of living capacity through ecological restoration, trees, forests, soil, water, biodiversity, habitats, seed and food systems, community stewardship, and long-term care.

The workstream begins with an eagle-eye, desk-based understanding of Sri Lanka’s living systems before any field intervention is chosen. It studies ecological regions, watersheds, forests, wetlands, mangroves, coasts, agricultural landscapes, urban ecosystems, climate and seasonal change, biodiversity, communities, livelihoods, institutions, knowledge traditions, and existing restoration practices.

The workstream does **not** analyse waste management, pollution flows, disposal systems, collection systems, or waste-sector governance. Those may belong to a separate Anavaratha dimension in the future, but they are not part of the current research question.

## Central research question

> **What conditions allow living systems and the communities that care for them to regenerate in Sri Lanka, and where might careful cultivation create durable ecological capacity?**

## Working principle

> **First understand the living landscape; then identify the conditions and leverage points for regeneration.**

## Meaning of cultivation

Cultivation is used broadly. It includes planting and tending trees, restoring soil and water relationships, protecting and increasing biodiversity, supporting native and locally appropriate species, renewing seed and food knowledge, creating habitat, building ecological literacy, and enabling communities to sustain care over time.

Cultivation is not limited to producing visible greenery. Its success depends on whether living systems become healthier, more diverse, more resilient, more connected, and more capable of continuing beyond a single project or organisation.

## Relationship to the wider Anavaratha concept

The broader Anavaratha concept may eventually contain multiple dimensions. The present workstream intentionally isolates the regenerative cultivation dimension so it can be understood on its own terms, without being interpreted as a consequence of or response to waste management. Its intellectual and practical centre is **the positive creation of ecological capacity**.
