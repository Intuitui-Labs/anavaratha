# Anavaratha

## Cultivation-branch verification protocol

**Desk-first evidence standard for the Sri Lankan living-systems atlas — Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This protocol governs the next stage of Anavaratha’s regenerative ecological cultivation research. It is designed to prevent the living-sector atlas from becoming an unverified catalogue, a planting list, or a collection of attractive but disconnected claims.

Every new record must preserve the difference between what a source directly states, what can reasonably be inferred, what remains uncertain, and what Anavaratha proposes as a research connection. The protocol applies to medicinal plants, food plants, trees, animals, insects, fungi, soil organisms, habitats, ecological relationships, garden traditions, and stewardship practices.

## 2. Record identity

Each record receives a stable identifier and a declared primary record type.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| **Record ID** | Stable identifier that does not change when the record is revised |
| **Primary type** | Organism, habitat, relationship, condition, institution, knowledge practice, source, or intervention hypothesis |
| **Primary direction** | Regenerative ecological cultivation; cross-links to waste remain explicit and secondary |
| **Working title** | Plain-language title that does not overstate certainty |
| **Version** | Revision number and date of the current record |
| **Geographic scope** | Island, ecological region, district, watershed, site, or unknown |
| **Temporal scope** | Historical, seasonal, current, projected, or unknown |

## 3. Evidence provenance

A source is not merely a URL. The record should make it possible to understand what the source is, how it was produced, what geography it covers, and what access or reuse conditions apply.

| Field | Required question |
|---|---|
| **Source ID** | Which source supports the claim? |
| **Source title and institution** | Who produced or maintains it? |
| **Source type** | Official catalogue, dataset, map, peer-reviewed study, thesis, report, local-language source, oral or community knowledge, or secondary interpretation |
| **Publication or update date** | When was the source produced or last updated? |
| **Access date** | When did Anavaratha consult it? |
| **Exact location** | Page, table, figure, record number, section, or URL fragment |
| **Method** | How did the source establish the information? |
| **Licence or permission** | Can it be reused, quoted, mapped, or redistributed? |
| **Access restriction** | Is the information sensitive, restricted, unpublished, or location-protected? |
| **Attribution requirement** | How should the source or knowledge holder be credited? |

## 4. Claim classification

Every important statement must be classified so that interpretation does not silently become fact.

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Direct evidence** | The source explicitly documents the claim |
| **Corroborated** | Multiple independent sources support substantially the same claim |
| **Qualified inference** | The claim follows reasonably from the evidence but is not directly stated |
| **Anavaratha interpretation** | A project-level synthesis, categorisation, or proposed relationship |
| **Hypothesis** | A question or possible relationship requiring further testing |
| **Disputed** | Credible sources or knowledge holders conflict |
| **Unknown** | The relevant evidence has not yet been found or is insufficient |

The atlas should never present a hypothesis or interpretation in the same visual or textual manner as direct evidence without a visible status label.

## 5. Confidence and uncertainty

Confidence describes the strength of the evidence for a particular claim. It does not describe how important, beautiful, useful, rare, or charismatic a being may be.

| Confidence | Minimum basis |
|---|---|
| **High** | Direct, appropriate, recent or historically suitable evidence with clear geography and method; preferably corroborated where the claim is consequential |
| **Medium** | Credible but incomplete evidence, limited geography, older evidence, or one important unresolved qualification |
| **Low** | Plausible indication, weak source fit, substantial missing context, or unresolved conflict |
| **Unassessed** | Confidence has not yet been evaluated |

Each record should include an uncertainty statement that identifies what is missing, what could change the interpretation, and what must not be claimed yet.

## 6. Organism records

For a tree, plant, animal, insect, fungus, or soil organism, the minimum record should include:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Taxonomic identity** | What name or names are used, and are synonyms or identification uncertainties present? |
| **Local names** | Which languages or communities use which names, with attribution and spelling preserved? |
| **Distribution** | Where is the organism documented, at what scale, and with what sampling limitations? |
| **Habitat and conditions** | What soil, water, shade, elevation, salinity, host, or landscape conditions are documented? |
| **Ecological role** | What function or relationship is directly supported? |
| **Status** | Is conservation, population, legal, or knowledge status documented by an appropriate source? |
| **Seasonality** | What flowering, fruiting, breeding, dormancy, migration, or other seasonal pattern is documented? |
| **Food or medicinal relation** | Is use documented, by whom, and with what safety and knowledge-ethics limits? |
| **Cultural or historical relation** | What meaning or history is documented, and by whom? |
| **Stewardship** | Who manages, protects, cultivates, observes, or has authority over the organism or knowledge? |
| **Non-action caution** | What would make planting, collection, relocation, or intervention inappropriate? |

## 7. Relationship records

A relationship must be recorded as a relation, not inferred merely because two organisms occur in the same broad landscape.

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | What being, condition, or actor is initiating the relationship? |
| **Object** | What being, condition, or actor is affected or connected? |
| **Relationship verb** | Pollinates, disperses, shelters, shades, feeds, decomposes, competes, depends on, protects, cultivates, teaches, or another precise verb |
| **Mechanism** | How does the relationship operate? |
| **Evidence** | Which source documents the mechanism? |
| **Geographic boundary** | Where has it been observed or documented? |
| **Temporal boundary** | In which season, period, or life stage does it apply? |
| **Population qualification** | Is this individual, local, regional, species-wide, or unknown? |
| **Confidence** | What is the evidence strength? |
| **Uncertainty** | What remains unresolved? |
| **Stewardship implication** | What should be protected, observed, or avoided? |

## 8. Medicinal-plant records

Medicinal status and medicinal use must be recorded as knowledge claims, not automatically as health advice. A medicinal-plant record should identify the source and knowledge tradition, preserve local names, distinguish documented use from demonstrated efficacy, and avoid recommending dosage, treatment, collection, or self-medication.

The 2025 checklist should be treated as an existing source to cross-reference and validate. Anavaratha should not reproduce it as a new independent catalogue without preserving its fields, page provenance, taxonomy, status definitions, and limitations.

## 9. Knowledge and ethics

Where knowledge is local, Indigenous, community-held, unpublished, sacred, commercially sensitive, or linked to vulnerable species, the record must not assume that public visibility equals permission to reproduce or map it.

The atlas must preserve attribution and consent conditions, avoid exposing sensitive locations, distinguish public documentation from community authority, and record when a relationship is intentionally withheld. A gap may be an ethical protection rather than a research failure.

## 10. Verification workflow

Every record should move through the following workflow:

> **Identify → locate source → extract exact evidence → preserve provenance → classify claim → assess confidence → record uncertainty → check ethical status → cross-link → review → publish internally or hold**

A record should be held back from the public-facing atlas when its identity, source, permission, geography, or ethical status is unresolved.

## 11. Readiness gate

A record may support a cultivation hypothesis only when its evidence and relationships are sufficient for the scale of the question. A documented organism is not automatically a planting recommendation. A medicinal use is not automatically a health recommendation. A species relationship is not automatically a reason to intervene.

> **No record becomes an action recommendation merely because it is interesting, useful, native, rare, medicinal, or ecologically attractive.**

The verification protocol therefore protects Anavaratha’s central stance: first understand the living landscape, then decide whether any proportionate form of care, cultivation, observation, or non-intervention is justified.
