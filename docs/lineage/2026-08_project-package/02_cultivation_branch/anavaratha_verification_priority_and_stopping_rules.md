# Anavaratha

## Verification priority and stopping rules

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working protocol, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This protocol decides which existing sources deserve deeper extraction first and when a record has enough support for the next research stage. It prevents Anavaratha from accumulating links without resolving the most important uncertainties.

## 2. Priority score

Each source or gap should be assessed across five dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Impact** | Would resolving this change the interpretation of a living relationship or care hypothesis? |
| **Authority** | Is the source institutionally, scientifically, or locally authoritative for the question? |
| **Transferability** | Can the evidence inform more than one atlas record or ecological context? |
| **Urgency** | Could delay increase ecological, cultural, legal, or ethical risk? |
| **Feasibility** | Can the source be accessed, extracted, and verified without disproportionate effort or harm? |

A high-priority item is one that is important, authoritative, reusable, urgent, and reasonably feasible. A difficult or inaccessible source may still be high priority, but the required request or permission should be recorded rather than assumed away.

## 3. Priority tiers

| Tier | Meaning | Initial examples |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Foundational** | Resolves identity, legal responsibility, status, or a cross-cutting source gap | National Herbarium pathway; 2025 medicinal checklist tables; DWC and Forest Department data routes |
| **P2 — Relational** | Connects organisms to habitat, season, function, or stewardship | Pollinator pairs; fruiting plant–disperser record; wetland–species links |
| **P3 — Contextual** | Improves interpretation but does not yet determine a care hypothesis | Historical cases, general restoration narratives, broad ecological orientation |
| **P4 — Exploratory** | Interesting lead with weak current evidence or unclear relevance | Unverified species claims, unsupported common-name associations, unlocated catalogues |

## 4. Evidence status

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Located** | Source or lead has been found |
| **Inspected** | Original page or document has been read |
| **Extracted** | Specific claims and fields have been recorded |
| **Cross-checked** | A second relevant source or method has been compared |
| **Contextualised** | Place, time, scale, method, limits, and relationships are attached |
| **Atlas-ready** | The record can be responsibly linked into the atlas |
| **Field-relevant** | The record may inform a place-specific research or care hypothesis |

## 5. Stopping rules

Research on a record should pause when one of the following is true:

| Stopping condition | Required action |
|---|---|
| **The source does not contain the expected information** | Mark the source as inspected and record the absence; do not keep searching the same page indefinitely |
| **The claim is already supported at the required level** | Mark it cross-checked or contextualised and move to the next gap |
| **Further detail requires permission or consent** | Record the access pathway and stop extraction until permission is available |
| **A species or location is sensitive** | Generalise, restrict, or withhold details and document the reason |
| **Sources conflict** | Preserve both records and create a conflict item; do not force a normalised value |
| **The evidence is too coarse for the intended use** | Retain it as orientation only and seek a finer source |
| **The evidence cannot support an intervention claim** | Keep it as context or a research lead, not as a recommendation |
| **The search begins producing repetition without new evidence** | Stop the pass and synthesise what is known and unknown |

## 6. Verification order

The current order is:

1. Verify identity, taxonomy, names, synonyms, and conservation fields through the National Herbarium and the updated medicinal checklist.
2. Verify the institutional and spatial routes for forests, protected areas, corridors, wetlands, and ecological zones.
3. Verify Osu Uyana references, local terminology, species, knowledge holders, and governance.
4. Verify seasonal relationship evidence for flowering, pollination, fruiting, dispersal, breeding, and care.
5. Verify at least one complete species–habitat–relationship record before expanding the atlas broadly.

## 7. Field-entry gate

No record should be treated as field-relevant until it has:

| Requirement | Minimum condition |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Correct organism or relationship is sufficiently verified |
| **Place** | Geography and ecological scale are explicit |
| **Condition** | Necessary soil, water, habitat, seasonal, or social conditions are described |
| **Stewardship** | Responsible authorities, communities, or knowledge holders are identified |
| **Ethics** | Consent, permits, sensitive locations, and knowledge protection are addressed |
| **Uncertainty** | Remaining gaps are visible and proportionate to the proposed activity |
| **Learning value** | The purpose of further work is clear |

## 8. Working rule

> **Do not seek completeness everywhere. Seek sufficient clarity at the points where a wrong assumption would matter most.**
