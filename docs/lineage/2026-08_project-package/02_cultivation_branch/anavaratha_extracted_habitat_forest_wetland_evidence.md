## Extracted habitat, forest, corridor, and wetland evidence layer

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working extraction, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This document extracts existing institutional evidence relevant to habitat continuity, protected areas, forest and watershed responsibility, wetlands, corridors, and spatial context. It does not create new habitat boundaries or replace official management plans.

## 3. What is directly reusable

The Forest Department page can support institutional descriptions, mandate mapping, and discovery of divisions responsible for forest inventory, GIS, biodiversity, watershed management, social forestry, and research [1]. The DWC page can support protected-area categories, corridor logic, reference-site reasoning, and permit awareness [2]. The National Wetland Directory page can support catalogue discovery and historical policy context [3]. The CEA page can support discovery of wetland, Red List, and environmental-information gateways [4].

None of these pages alone establishes current species distribution, habitat quality, ecological recovery, or field-level intervention suitability.

## 5. Source limits

Institutional mandates should not be described as evidence that an objective has been achieved. A protected-area category does not guarantee habitat integrity. A corridor designation does not demonstrate that animals use the corridor successfully. A wetland directory entry does not establish current ecological condition. An administrative boundary does not necessarily correspond to an ecological boundary.

## 6. Next extraction priorities

The next pass should locate the actual Forest Department inventory and GIS products; obtain current protected-area and corridor layers; inspect the National Wetland Directory and any National Wetland Database access; identify current Red List versions; and connect institutional responsibility to specific habitats and species without exposing sensitive information.

> **Institutional location is the beginning of accountability, not evidence that ecological continuity already exists.**
