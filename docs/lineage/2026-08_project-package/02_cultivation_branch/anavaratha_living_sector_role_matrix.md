# Anavaratha

## Living-sector role and relationship matrix

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working analytical tool, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This matrix supports the descent from landscape and habitat into organisms and relationships. It is designed to prevent Anavaratha from treating living beings as a list of planting options or as isolated “useful” species.

Each organism or organism-group should be studied through three linked questions:

> **What is it? What relationships does it participate in? What conditions allow those relationships to continue?**

## 2. Primary role dimensions

| Dimension | Categories to investigate | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural** | Canopy, understory, ground layer, aquatic layer, root, soil, nesting structure | Structure is habitat, not only biomass |
| **Nourishing** | Food, nectar, fruit, leaf, prey, host, medicine, soil organic matter | “Useful” to humans is only one form of nourishment |
| **Connecting** | Pollination, seed dispersal, movement, corridor, fungal association, water connection | Relationships may be seasonal or difficult to observe |
| **Protective** | Shade, cooling, erosion control, water retention, shelter, wind protection, flood buffering | Functions depend on place and scale |
| **Regenerative** | Seed source, recruitment, natural regeneration, soil building, decomposition, succession | A species may support regeneration without being a restoration target |
| **Indicative** | Indicator of habitat quality, water condition, soil condition, disturbance, recovery, or climate change | Indicator status requires evidence and a defined baseline |
| **Cultural** | Story, ritual, medicine, food, livelihood, memory, identity, customary care | Document knowledge with consent and attribution |
| **Conservation** | Endemic, endangered, threatened, rare, restricted, protected, locally declining, recovering | Status must include source, date, and geographic context |
| **Relational** | Attraction, dependence, mutualism, competition, predation, host relationship, conflict, stewardship | Avoid assuming that relationships are always beneficial or harmonious |

## 3. Relationship matrix

| From | Relationship | To | Condition required | Possible observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flowering plant | Pollination | Bee, butterfly, moth, bird, bat, or other pollinator | Flowers, season, habitat, pesticide-free or suitable conditions | Visits, flowering calendar, fruit or seed set |
| Fruiting tree | Seed dispersal | Bird, bat, mammal, or other animal | Fruit, shelter, movement route, suitable germination site | Seed movement, seedlings, animal presence |
| Canopy tree | Shade and microclimate | Soil, seedlings, people, animals, insects | Canopy structure, water, space, appropriate density | Temperature, moisture, survival, behaviour |
| Rooted vegetation | Soil protection and retention | Soil, water, slope, downstream system | Root structure, ground cover, slope fit, rainfall | Infiltration, erosion, moisture, sediment |
| Wetland plant | Habitat and water relation | Fish, amphibian, insect, bird, water system | Appropriate hydrology, water quality, seasonal inundation | Recruitment, nesting, water level, species presence |
| Fungus or microorganism | Decomposition or symbiosis | Root, soil, plant, animal, organic matter | Suitable substrate, moisture, chemical conditions | Soil structure, decomposition, plant association |
| Medicinal plant | Health and knowledge | People, healers, households, cultural system | Correct identity, sustainable harvest, propagation, knowledge continuity | Use, cultivation, seed or plant sharing, regeneration |
| Native habitat | Refuge and movement | Endemic or threatened organism | Continuity, food, shelter, low disturbance, corridor | Occupancy, movement, breeding, seasonal use |
| Community | Stewardship | Habitat or organism | Rights, access, knowledge, time, tools, institutional support | Care practice, monitoring, decisions, continuity |

## 4. Priority assessment dimensions

A priority organism or relationship should be assessed across the following dimensions rather than selected by charisma alone.

| Dimension | Guiding question |
|---|---|
| **Irreplaceability** | Is the organism endemic, restricted, threatened, or difficult to replace? |
| **Ecological leverage** | Does supporting it strengthen several other relationships? |
| **Vulnerability** | How sensitive is it to habitat loss, climate, disturbance, disease, or isolation? |
| **Recoverability** | Can the population or relationship recover naturally if conditions improve? |
| **Cultural significance** | Does it carry medicinal, food, historical, spiritual, or place-based meaning? |
| **Cultivation feasibility** | Can people support it without causing new harm or reducing genetic diversity? |
| **Observability** | Can change be monitored with appropriate methods and time? |
| **Social accountability** | Who benefits, who bears labour, and who may be excluded or burdened? |
| **Uncertainty** | What is not known about its identity, status, relationships, or response? |

## 5. Suggested priority classes

These classes are not a ranking of worth. They indicate why a being or relationship may deserve deeper study.

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Creates or maintains habitat, soil, water, shade, or structural conditions |
| **Connector** | Links organisms, habitats, seasons, or landscapes through movement or exchange |
| **Attractor** | Supports the presence of pollinators, seed dispersers, birds, insects, or other beings |
| **Indicator** | Helps reveal ecological condition or change |
| **Guardian** | Represents a threatened, endemic, rare, or irreplaceable living relationship |
| **Provider** | Contributes food, medicine, material, livelihood, or other human wellbeing |
| **Memory-bearer** | Holds historical, cultural, medicinal, linguistic, or place-based knowledge |
| **Recoverer** | Demonstrates natural or assisted regeneration and can reveal what conditions support recovery |
| **Steward** | Represents a person, group, institution, or practice that sustains ecological care |

## 6. Selection rules

Anavaratha should not select species solely because they are fast-growing, visually attractive, marketable, familiar, or easy to plant. Selection should follow ecological diagnosis and consider genetic diversity, local provenance, habitat, associated organisms, water and soil conditions, maintenance, and long-term consequences.

An endangered organism should not automatically become a cultivation target if propagation or translocation is inappropriate. A medicinal plant should not be harvested more intensively because it has cultural value. A tree that attracts wildlife should be assessed for both benefits and possible human–wildlife conflict. A “friendly” insect or animal should be understood through ecological relationships rather than a simplified good–bad classification.

## 7. Data confidence

Each claim should carry a confidence field:

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | Supported by authoritative, current, geographically relevant evidence or repeated observation |
| **Moderate** | Supported by credible evidence but with limitations in date, scale, or coverage |
| **Low** | Plausible or locally reported but requires verification |
| **Unknown** | The relationship or status is not yet sufficiently documented |

A living-sector atlas should show uncertainty instead of hiding it. Unknown relationships are research priorities, not invitations to invent certainty.

## 8. The first atlas outputs

The initial atlas should contain an organism–habitat table, a relationship network, a functional-role matrix, a conservation-priority layer, a biocultural-knowledge register, a seasonal calendar, and a regenerative-condition map. These outputs should be linked to sources, locations, dates, and confidence levels.

The final aim is not to produce a definitive catalogue of Sri Lankan life. It is to produce a useful and revisable map of relationships that can guide proportionate cultivation and future field learning.

> **The unit of Anavaratha is not the species alone. It is the species-in-relationship-with-habitat, season, other beings, and people.**
