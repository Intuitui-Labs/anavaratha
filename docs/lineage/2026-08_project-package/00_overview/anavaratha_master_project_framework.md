# Anavaratha

## Master project-home framework

**Two ecological directions, one desk-first knowledge architecture — Sri Lanka-focused working framework, Version 1.0**

## 1. Anavaratha as the project home

**Anavaratha is the larger project home.** It is not limited to one intervention, one species, one garden, one waste technology, or one field site. It is a long-term ecological inquiry and practice that begins with disciplined understanding and may eventually develop several proportionate forms of action.

The project home has two principal directions:

1. **Regenerative ecological cultivation**
2. **Waste and material responsibility**

These directions are connected because both shape ecological and human conditions. They remain distinct because they ask different primary questions, use different evidence, involve different responsibilities, and may lead to different kinds of action.

> **One project home. Two ecological directions. One shared discipline: understand before acting.**

## 2. The shared foundation

Each direction begins with the same initial sequence:

> **Desk research → sense-making → knowledge-building → relationship mapping → uncertainty review → proportionate decision about action**

This sequence is not a rigid linear pipeline. Research may return to an earlier stage when a new source, relationship, seasonal observation, community perspective, or ethical concern changes the picture.

The shared foundation asks Anavaratha to understand Sri Lanka through ecological, spatial, temporal, social, institutional, cultural, material, and future-facing lenses. It also requires the project to distinguish documented evidence from interpretation, and an emerging possibility from an established conclusion.

| Shared discipline | Meaning for the whole project |
|---|---|
| **Desk-first** | Build understanding before choosing a field intervention |
| **Sense-making** | Connect information into systems, patterns, relationships, and hypotheses |
| **Source-aware** | Use existing catalogues and institutions while preserving provenance and access conditions |
| **Multi-scale** | Move between national, regional, watershed, institutional, community, and site levels |
| **Time-aware** | Record history, seasonality, duration, succession, accumulation, and future change |
| **Relational** | Study dependencies, flows, capacities, burdens, and feedbacks |
| **Proportionate** | Match claims and action to evidence, authority, capacity, and risk |
| **Adaptive** | Keep categories, maps, interpretations, and strategies revisable |
| **Accountable** | Respect consent, attribution, local authority, sensitive knowledge, and uneven burdens |

## 4. Direction A: regenerative ecological cultivation

The cultivation direction studies and supports the conditions through which living systems and the communities connected to them can flourish, regenerate, and continue in Sri Lanka.

Its inquiry descends through the following sequence:

> **Landscape → habitat → living community → organism → relationship → condition → proportionate care**

The branch includes forests, trees, natural regeneration, soil, water, watersheds, biodiversity, habitat connectivity, wetlands, mangroves, coasts, agroecology, agroforestry, seeds, food, medicinal plants, urban cultivation, animals, birds, insects, fungi, soil organisms, cultural relationships, ecological history, and stewardship.

Its central question is:

> **What conditions allow Sri Lankan living systems and their communities of care to regenerate, and where could proportionate cultivation strengthen those conditions?**

Cultivation is therefore broader than planting. It may eventually involve protection, natural regeneration, habitat repair, seed work, garden-making, agroecology, ecological education, community stewardship, or deliberate non-intervention. The appropriate form must emerge from the conditions rather than being imposed in advance.

The branch is currently in its desk research, sense-making, and knowledge-building phase. It is not yet a field plan, planting prescription, species list, medical guide, or claim of action readiness.

## 5. Direction B: waste and material responsibility

The waste direction studies the material conditions that shape ecological and human life. It asks how materials are produced, used, circulated, discarded, accumulated, exposed, governed, and made someone’s responsibility.

Its inquiry may include prevention, reduction, reuse, repair, recovery, recycling, treatment, disposal, plastics, hazardous materials, wastewater, illegal dumping, burning, contamination, service inequality, worker conditions, producer responsibility, municipal capacity, and circular possibilities.

Its central question is:

> **What materials and harms move through Sri Lankan systems, where do responsibilities and burdens accumulate, and what conditions could reduce harm and improve material accountability?**

This branch must not be treated as merely a cleanliness campaign or a disposal problem. Its desk research should examine upstream production and consumption, infrastructure, labour, governance, exposure, inequality, ecological effects, and the difference between moving waste elsewhere and actually reducing harm.

The waste direction belongs fully within Anavaratha, but its detailed research architecture should be developed separately from the cultivation atlas. It should not be inserted into cultivation documents merely because pollution can affect living systems.

## 6. How the directions connect

The two directions should be **separate in primary question and connected in consequence**. A wetland-cultivation record may link to a waste record about contamination affecting that wetland. A soil-regeneration record may link to a material record about harmful inputs. A waste-flow record may link to a cultivation record about soil organisms, water, food systems, or habitat.

Such links must be explicit, evidence-based, geographically bounded, time-aware, and labelled with confidence. They must preserve the identity of both records rather than merge them into a general environmental category.

| Connection rule | Application |
|---|---|
| **Primary direction** | Every record begins by stating whether its main question belongs to cultivation or waste |
| **Explicit cross-link** | A relationship to the other direction is recorded separately |
| **Evidence preserved** | Each direction retains its own sources, methods, uncertainties, and responsibilities |
| **No premature synthesis** | A connection is not assumed merely because both directions concern the environment |
| **Shared consequences** | Real interactions can later inform a cross-direction systems view |

> **Separate the questions, connect the consequences, and let evidence determine the relationship.**

## 7. The current location of the project

The active work is currently located in **Direction A: regenerative ecological cultivation**, within the shared desk-first foundation. The cultivation branch has already developed a substantial research infrastructure:

| Existing layer | Current contribution |
|---|---|
| **Sri Lanka eagle-eye** | Establishes the national, multi-scale, cultivation-only research frame |
| **Eight domain studies** | Explores ecological regions, forests, soil and water, biodiversity, wetlands, agroecology, urban cultivation, and stewardship |
| **Living-sector descent** | Moves from landscape to organism, relationship, condition, and care |
| **Source map** | Connects existing catalogues, datasets, institutions, and knowledge resources |
| **Evidence crosswalk** | Links catalogue, habitat, institutional, relationship, and stewardship evidence |
| **Relationship atlas** | Creates a revisable, source-linked representation of living relationships |
| **Medicinal-garden inquiry** | Explores Osu Uyana and comparative medicinal-garden traditions cautiously |
| **Adaptation. method** | Requires representational humility, provisional categories, and transparent revision |
| **Cultivation Atlas Release 01** | Demonstrates a small, governed, source-linked relationship atlas without claiming completeness or action readiness |

The cultivation work should continue deepening this infrastructure before it moves toward a particular site or action. Cultivation Atlas Release 01 is the first controlled milestone: it makes a limited number of records legible while preserving uncertainty, source authority, and ethical boundaries. The waste direction can be opened later through its own equivalent desk-research architecture.

## 8. Berlin as a bounded analogy

The earlier analogy to Isaiah Berlin’s distinction between negative and positive freedom helps describe the broad directional shape of Anavaratha, but it is not the project’s literal political theory.

The waste direction may often involve **freedom from harmful conditions** such as contamination, exposure, unmanaged accumulation, and ecological obstruction. The cultivation direction may often involve **freedom for flourishing**: the capacity of living systems and communities to grow, participate, renew, and continue.

The analogy is not absolute. Waste work also requires constructive capacity, and cultivation can require protection from harmful conditions. The distinction indicates a direction of attention rather than a hierarchy between the two branches.

These references are not all evidence of the same kind. Some are artistic works, some are lived examples, some are organisations, and some are institutional sources. Their roles must remain distinct.

## 10. Master maturity model

Both directions can use a shared maturity model while filling it with different evidence.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mapped** | The relevant system, geography, actors, and broad conditions are identified |
| **Characterised** | Important components, histories, relationships, capacities, and pressures are reasonably described |
| **Connected** | Dependencies, feedbacks, benefits, burdens, responsibilities, and unintended effects are understood across relevant scales |
| **Testable** | A proportionate hypothesis and learning design can be stated responsibly |
| **Actionable** | Authority, capacity, safeguards, partners, resources, and monitoring are adequate for a defined next step |

“Actionable” does not mean merely urgent. It means that the knowledge, relationships, authority, capacity, and safeguards are sufficient for a responsible action of a defined scale.

## 11. Immediate cultivation priorities

The cultivation branch has now reached its first controlled atlas milestone. Cultivation Atlas Release 01 contains a small set of visually checked medicinal-checklist examples, a Horton Plains bird–fruiting-plant relationship, a seasonal fruiting-community record, medicinal-garden context, and institutional evidence pathways.

The next work remains within the desk-first phase. Its highest-value tasks are to continue semi-manual checklist review only where it materially strengthens a relationship; clarify National Herbarium catalogue and specimen access; identify current Forest Department GIS and inventory products; verify DWC protected-area and corridor layers; inspect the National Wetland Directory and database metadata; deepen primary and local-language clarification of “Osu Uyana”; and add only relationships with clear methods, geography, season, and stewardship context.

These tasks should not become a new ungoverned catalogue. Every addition should retain source, date, method, geography, confidence, licence or permission, sensitive-location protection, consent and attribution, and the distinction between direct evidence and Anavaratha interpretation. No atlas record should silently become a planting prescription, medicinal guide, collection mission, site selection, or field intervention.

## 12. Master working definition

> **Anavaratha is a Sri Lankan project home for two desk-first ecological directions: regenerative ecological cultivation, which studies and strengthens the conditions for living continuity; and waste and material responsibility, which studies and reduces the material burdens and harms that shape ecological life. Both directions begin with rigorous research, sense-making, and knowledge-building before moving toward proportionate, accountable action.**

## 13. Final operating principle

> **Understand before acting. Cultivate capacity where life can continue. Reduce harm where material systems obstruct it. Keep the directions distinct, and connect them where evidence shows that they meet.**
