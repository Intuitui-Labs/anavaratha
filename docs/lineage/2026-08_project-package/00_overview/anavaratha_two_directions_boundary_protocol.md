# Anavaratha

## Two-direction boundary and alignment protocol

**Shared project home, distinct ecological research directions — Working framework, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

Anavaratha contains two principal directions: **regenerative ecological cultivation** and **waste and material responsibility**. This protocol keeps their research streams distinct while ensuring that they can later communicate where their relationships are real and useful.

The purpose is not to isolate the directions from one another. Ecological cultivation can be affected by waste, pollution, contaminated soil, and damaged water systems. Waste systems can affect forests, farms, wetlands, animals, people, and the conditions for regeneration. The purpose is to avoid collapsing these relationships too early into one undifferentiated environmental catalogue.

## 2. The boundary rule

Each record, research question, source, map, and proposed action should have a clear **primary direction**. A record may be cross-linked to the other direction, but it should not lose its original object, evidence, responsibility, or method.

| If the primary question is about… | It belongs primarily to… |
|---|---|
| How living systems flourish, regenerate, relate, and continue | **Regenerative ecological cultivation** |
| How materials, waste, pollution, exposure, and responsibility move through systems | **Waste and material responsibility** |
| How waste conditions affect living systems | A primary waste or cultivation record with an explicit cross-link, depending on the question being investigated |
| How cultivation creates or changes material flows | A primary cultivation or waste record with an explicit cross-link, depending on the question being investigated |

## 3. Shared foundation

Both directions use the same foundational research disciplines:

> **Desk research → source evaluation → sense-making → relationship mapping → uncertainty recording → ethical review → proportionate decision about action**

The shared foundation includes the following requirements:

| Requirement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Desk-first** | Do not begin with a symbolic intervention before understanding the relevant system |
| **Source-linked** | Preserve the source, date, geography, method, access conditions, and interpretation status |
| **Multi-scale** | Move between national, regional, institutional, community, and site scales |
| **Time-aware** | Record history, seasonality, duration, accumulation, succession, and future pressures |
| **Relational** | Study dependencies, exchanges, burdens, capacities, and feedbacks |
| **Proportionate** | Match claims and actions to evidence, authority, capacity, and risk |
| **Adaptive** | Allow records, categories, and hypotheses to change when knowledge changes |
| **Accountable** | Respect local authority, consent, attribution, sensitive information, and uneven burdens |

## 4. Direction A: regenerative ecological cultivation

The cultivation direction studies and supports the conditions through which living systems can flourish, regenerate, and continue in Sri Lanka. Its units of inquiry range from ecological regions and watersheds to habitats, living communities, organisms, relationships, conditions, and forms of care.

Its research includes forests, trees, natural regeneration, soil, water, biodiversity, habitat connectivity, wetlands, mangroves, coasts, agroecology, seeds, food, medicinal plants, urban cultivation, animals, insects, fungi, soil organisms, knowledge systems, and stewardship.

Its central question is:

> **What conditions allow living systems and the communities that care for them to regenerate, and where could proportionate cultivation strengthen those conditions?**

The cultivation branch must not be reduced to tree counts, planting targets, species lists, or generic greening. It must maintain attention to ecological fit, natural regeneration, population and habitat relationships, seasonality, community authority, and long-term stewardship.

## 5. Direction B: waste and material responsibility

The waste direction studies the material conditions that shape ecological and human life. Its units of inquiry range from products, materials, households, institutions, and services to collection systems, labour, disposal sites, pollution pathways, exposure, and governance.

Its research may include prevention, reduction, reuse, repair, recovery, recycling, treatment, disposal, contamination, illegal dumping, burning, wastewater, plastics, hazardous materials, service inequalities, worker conditions, producer responsibility, municipal capacity, and circular possibilities.

Its central question is:

> **What materials and harms move through Sri Lankan systems, where do responsibilities and burdens accumulate, and what conditions could reduce harm and improve material accountability?**

The waste branch must not be treated only as a cleanliness campaign or a technical disposal problem. It must account for production, consumption, labour, infrastructure, governance, exposure, inequality, ecological effects, and the possibility that a material problem begins far upstream of its visible waste form.

## 6. Cross-linking without collapse

Cross-links are permitted when they are explicit and evidence-based. For example, a cultivation record concerning a wetland may link to a waste record concerning a pollution pathway affecting that wetland. Neither record should be rewritten as the other. The cross-link should identify the relationship, its evidence, its direction, and its uncertainty.

A cross-link should contain at least:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Source record** | Which cultivation or waste record is initiating the link? |
| **Target record** | Which record in the other direction is connected? |
| **Relationship** | What affects, depends on, constrains, enables, or signals what? |
| **Evidence** | Which source supports the relationship? |
| **Geography** | Where does the relationship hold? |
| **Time** | Is it historical, seasonal, current, projected, or unknown? |
| **Confidence** | How strong is the evidence? |
| **Ethical status** | Are there restrictions, consent requirements, or sensitive details? |
| **Interpretation** | What is directly documented and what is Anavaratha’s synthesis? |

## 7. Shared questions, different answers

The directions can ask parallel questions without assuming that they have identical evidence or outcomes.

| Shared question | Cultivation interpretation | Waste interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **What is moving?** | Water, nutrients, seeds, pollen, organisms, shade, energy, knowledge | Materials, products, chemicals, waste, labour, costs, risks |
| **What is accumulating?** | Biomass, soil organic matter, habitat complexity, ecological knowledge | Waste, contamination, infrastructure deficits, exposure, unpaid burdens |
| **What is vulnerable?** | Species, habitats, watersheds, food systems, medicinal knowledge, communities | Workers, residents, children, ecosystems, informal systems, future generations |
| **What creates capacity?** | Natural regeneration, diversity, soil life, stewardship, connectivity, time | Prevention, safe systems, accountability, repair, access, service capacity |
| **What should be observed?** | Regeneration, relationships, seasonal change, survival, ecological function | Flows, quantities, leakage, exposure, service performance, behaviour, harms |
| **What must not be assumed?** | That planting equals regeneration | That disposal equals resolution |

## 8. Research maturity for both directions

Both directions can use the same maturity language while filling it with different evidence:

| Level | Shared meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mapped** | The relevant system, geography, actors, and broad conditions are identified |
| **Characterised** | Important components, histories, relationships, capacities, and pressures are reasonably described |
| **Connected** | Dependencies, feedbacks, benefits, burdens, responsibilities, and unintended effects are understood across relevant scales |
| **Testable** | A proportionate hypothesis and learning design can be stated responsibly |
| **Actionable** | Authority, capacity, partners, safeguards, resources, and monitoring are sufficient for a defined next step |

The word **actionable** should not mean “urgent enough to act.” It should mean that the knowledge and conditions are adequate for a responsible action of a specified scale.

## 9. Present status

The cultivation direction is substantially further developed. It already has an eagle-eye framework, domain papers, living-sector taxonomy, relationship atlas, source map, evidence register, medicinal-garden inquiry, and Adaptation.-influenced method of provisional representation.

The waste direction remains part of the larger Anavaratha home and its earlier conceptual history, but it should receive its own desk-research architecture rather than being inserted into the cultivation documents. When opened, it should begin with a separate scope, source map, material-flow taxonomy, evidence register, and ethical review.

## 10. Operating principle

> **Separate the questions, connect the consequences, and let evidence determine the relationship.**

Anavaratha can therefore hold both directions without confusion. Cultivation asks how living capacity can be enabled. Waste asks how material burdens and harms can be reduced and made accountable. Both begin by learning how the system actually lives.
