# Anavaratha

## Project-home architecture

**Two ecological directions, one desk-first foundation — Working framework, Version 0.1**

## 1. Core clarification

Anavaratha is the larger project home. It contains two principal ecological directions:

1. **Regenerative ecological cultivation**
2. **Waste and material responsibility**

These are not two unrelated projects and they are not two immediate field programmes. Each direction begins with the same initial discipline: **desk research, sense-making, and knowledge-building**. Only after a direction has built sufficient understanding should it move toward design, partnership, experimentation, or field action.

The current body of work belongs to the first direction. The Sri Lanka eagle-eye framework, living-sector taxonomy, species relationship atlas, source map, crosswalks, and medicinal-garden research are all part of Anavaratha’s **regenerative ecological cultivation branch**.

## 3. What the two directions share

Both directions begin from the same intellectual and ethical foundation. They must understand Sri Lanka as a place with ecological variation, social difference, institutional complexity, histories, livelihoods, uneven burdens, and changing futures. Both must identify what is known, what is uncertain, who holds knowledge, who carries responsibility, and what scale of action is proportionate.

Both also follow an adaptive research cycle:

> **Observe → Gather → Cross-link → Interpret → Question → Revise → Decide whether to act**

This is not a linear pipeline. New evidence may reopen earlier assumptions. A source may expose a gap. A local relationship may alter the categories. A possible intervention may be rejected because the knowledge, authority, capacity, or ecological fit is insufficient.

## 4. What remains distinct

The two directions should not be collapsed into one generic environmental programme. They have different objects, vocabularies, evidence, actors, risks, and possible interventions.

| Dimension | Regenerative ecological cultivation | Waste and material responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Primary object | Living systems and their capacity to regenerate | Materials, waste flows, pollution, and service systems |
| Central question | What conditions allow life to flourish and continue? | What materials and harms move through the system, and who is responsible? |
| Main relations | Species, habitat, soil, water, season, community, stewardship | Production, consumption, collection, labour, disposal, exposure, governance |
| Time horizon | Succession, growth, seasonality, inheritance, intergenerational care | Flow, accumulation, service cycles, historic contamination, future liability |
| Typical evidence | Species, habitats, ecological processes, seed, soil, water, local knowledge | Quantities, flows, facilities, regulations, service coverage, labour, exposure |
| Possible later action | Cultivation, restoration, conservation, garden, seed, habitat, stewardship | Reduction, reuse, recovery, service redesign, material substitution, accountability |
| Main caution | Planting without ecological fit or social responsibility | Treating waste as an isolated technical problem without tracing responsibility |

## 5. The current position

Anavaratha is currently at the shared foundation, with substantial development in Direction A. It is not yet at the field-intervention stage. The cultivation branch has moved through the following knowledge-building sequence:

| Stage | Current work |
|---|---|
| **Eagle-eye view** | Sri Lankan ecological regions, habitats, institutions, and regenerative conditions |
| **Domain exploration** | Forests, trees, soil, water, biodiversity, wetlands, agroecology, urban cultivation, and stewardship |
| **Living-sector descent** | Organisms, ecological functions, biocultural meaning, status, and relationships |
| **Source awareness** | Existing catalogues, institutional resources, source evaluation, and crosswalks |
| **Medicinal-garden inquiry** | Osu Uyana and comparative medicinal-garden traditions |
| **Adaptive representation** | _Adaptation._ as a methodological reminder that the atlas must remain provisional |

The cultivation direction is therefore not merely “tree planting.” It is the study and cultivation of **conditions for living continuity**.

## 6. The shared research method

Each direction should maintain its own domain-specific research programme, but use common research disciplines:

| Research discipline | Application |
|---|---|
| **Desk-first** | Build enough understanding before selecting field actions |
| **Sense-making** | Connect facts into systems, relationships, patterns, and hypotheses |
| **Source-aware** | Reuse existing catalogues and preserve provenance rather than duplicating them |
| **Multi-scale** | Move between national, regional, watershed, institutional, community, and site scales |
| **Temporal** | Record history, seasonality, change, duration, and intergenerational effects |
| **Proportionate** | Match claims, risks, resources, and interventions to the evidence and capacity available |
| **Relational** | Study beings, materials, institutions, and people through their relationships |
| **Adaptive** | Keep categories and strategies open to revision |
| **Accountable** | Record authority, consent, uncertainty, burdens, benefits, and unintended effects |

## 7. Berlin analogy within the larger home

Berlin’s negative and positive freedom remain a bounded analogy for the shape of Anavaratha’s two ecological directions, not a literal political theory of the project.

At the broadest level, waste and material responsibility may often concern **freedom from harmful conditions**: contamination, exposure, unmanaged accumulation, and ecological obstruction. Regenerative ecological cultivation may often concern **freedom for flourishing**: the capacity of living systems and communities to grow, participate, renew, and continue.

This analogy does not imply that the waste direction is only negative or that cultivation is only positive. Both require constructive research, design, labour, governance, and care. The analogy describes a directional relationship, not a hierarchy.

## 9. Working definition

> **Anavaratha is a Sri Lankan project home for two desk-first ecological directions: cultivating the conditions for living systems to regenerate, and understanding the material and waste conditions that shape ecological life. Both begin through rigorous sense-making and knowledge-building before moving toward proportionate, accountable action.**

## 10. Present research status

The immediate work should remain within the cultivation branch’s knowledge-building phase. Its next tasks include strengthening the linked living-sector atlas, verifying medicinal and ecological records, understanding Osu Uyana and related garden traditions, and allowing the research to reveal where a future cultivation pathway may be appropriate.

The waste direction should have its own separate desk-research architecture when it is opened. It should not be forced into the cultivation atlas, and the cultivation atlas should not be reduced to waste or pollution categories.

> **One project home. Two directions. One shared discipline: understand before acting.**
