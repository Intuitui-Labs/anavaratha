# Anavaratha Project Package

**Consolidated project archive — Version 1.0**

## 1. What Anavaratha is

Anavaratha is a Sri Lanka-focused ecological project home for long-term research, sense-making, knowledge-building, and proportionate future action. It is not currently a single intervention, garden, marketplace, waste solution, species list, or field plan.

The project is built around a common discipline:

> **Desk research → sense-making → knowledge-building → relationship mapping → uncertainty review → proportionate decision about action**

The work completed so far has developed two established ecological directions and one emerging domain. The **regenerative ecological-cultivation direction** is the active research stream in this package. The **waste and material-responsibility direction** remains a separate future stream and is not developed in the cultivation or Osu Uyana work. The newly identified **circulation and latent-value domain** is a provisional emerging area concerned with useful things finding new relationships before they become waste.

## 3. Active branch: regenerative ecological cultivation

The cultivation branch studies the conditions through which living systems and their communities of care can regenerate and continue in Sri Lanka. Its descent is:

> **Landscape → habitat → living community → organism → relationship → condition → proportionate care**

It includes ecological regions, forests, trees, natural regeneration, soil, water, watersheds, biodiversity, wetlands, mangroves, coasts, agroecology, agroforestry, seeds, food, medicinal plants, urban cultivation, animals, birds, insects, fungi, soil organisms, cultural relationships, ecological history, institutions, and stewardship.

The branch is currently **mapped, characterised, and partially connected**, but it is not yet testable or action-ready for a specific field site.

## 4. Osu Uyana pathway

The Osu Uyana inquiry began from the user’s clarification that *Osu Uyana* means medicinal garden in Sinhala. The research now treats it as a provisional contemporary category for medicinal cultivation and knowledge spaces, not as a single fixed institution and not as an automatically proven ancient continuity.

The inquiry compares official medicinal-plant gardens, herbal-garden networks, community and school plots, household gardens, and historical managed landscapes. Its living-system model connects:

| Layer | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Landscape** | Ecological region, cultural landscape, settlement, forest, wetland, coast, farm, or homegarden |
| **Habitat conditions** | Soil, water, shade, climate, drainage, moisture, disturbance, and elevation |
| **Living community** | Medicinal plants, trees, pollinators, birds, fungi, soil organisms, animals, and people |
| **Medicinal knowledge** | Sinhala names, practitioner knowledge, household practice, Ayurveda, and teaching |
| **Continuity systems** | Seeds, mother plants, nurseries, vouchers, herbarium records, and propagation |
| **Stewardship** | Labour, institutions, households, schools, communities, authority, access, and protection |
| **Time and adaptation** | Seasons, flowering, fruiting, harvest, succession, climate variability, and revision |

Ganewatta is the most fully documented official case in the current inquiry. Its official description presents a 21.04-hectare Intermediate-Zone garden with medicinal-plant collections, ex situ conservation, commercial cultivation, education, research, nurseries, seed preservation, a mini herbarium, and bee-keeping. These features are treated as a case to study, not as a universal template.

## 5. Cultivation Atlas Release 01

The first controlled atlas release demonstrates how existing sources can be connected without recreating them as an uncontrolled catalogue. It contains 11 controlled records: six visually checked medicinal-checklist examples; the Yellow-eared Bulbul and Horton Plains fruiting-community relationship; Ganewatta; Osu Uyana; and institutional evidence pathways.

The medicinal checklist remains governed by two distinct numbers: **1,476**, the article’s reported narrative total, and **1,349**, the incomplete conservative working extraction. The discrepancy is deliberately preserved as unresolved. The extraction is not a final national catalogue and does not support medicinal, planting, or propagation recommendations.

The strongest relationship record concerns Yellow-eared Bulbul (*Pycnonotus penicillatus*) frugivory in Horton Plains. The study documents feeding on multiple plants, seeds of nine plant species in faecal samples, and seasonal variation in fruiting and feeding relationships. The atlas records seed ingestion evidence but does not automatically claim effective seed dispersal or recruitment.

## 6. Methodological influence of *Adaptation.*

Charlie Kaufman’s *Adaptation.* (2002) is treated as an artistic and methodological influence, not as scientific ecological evidence. Its role is to encourage representational humility, provisional categories, reflexive mapping, uncertainty, revision, and awareness that a map changes what becomes visible.

> **The map is not the living world, but the map changes the conditions under which the living world is seen.**

## 7. Emerging domain: circulation and latent value

The circulation domain emerged from the idea that a thing no longer needed by its current user may still be needed, repairable, shareable, exchangeable, adaptable, or valuable to someone else.

Its central proposition is:

> **Before something becomes waste, it may still be waiting for another relationship.**

It studies resale, gifting, sharing, lending, renting, repair, refurbishment, adaptation, redistribution, and component recovery. It is not simply a marketplace idea: trust, condition, safety, fit, access, transport, storage, repair labour, reciprocity, and a responsible endpoint all matter.

Its provisional position is:

> **Circulation and latent value is an emerging domain under Anavaratha, adjacent to waste but not contained by it, and capable of supporting cultivation without becoming ecological cultivation itself.**

This domain is conceptually developed in this archive but remains desk-research-only and has not been turned into an application, marketplace, or intervention.

## 10. Current unresolved questions

The archive intentionally leaves important questions open. These include the complete row-level status of the 2025 medicinal checklist; current access pathways for institutional spatial and specimen data; primary and local-language clarification of Osu Uyana; site-level ecological conditions of medicinal gardens; the relationship between formal gardens and household or community practices; the long-term governance of seeds and knowledge; and the evidence needed before any atlas record can support a defined care action.

The emerging circulation domain has its own unresolved questions: which Sri Lankan practices already circulate unused things; how informal repair and exchange work; who performs the labour; how trust and safety are built; who is excluded; and where a useful object becomes a waste burden.

## 11. What this package does not claim

This package does not claim to provide a complete Sri Lankan medicinal-plant catalogue, medical advice, species-selection guidance, garden-design prescription, restoration plan, waste solution, marketplace design, field-site recommendation, or proof that any intervention is ready to begin.

Its purpose is to preserve a careful foundation from which future research can proceed.

## 12. Core working definition

> **Anavaratha is a Sri Lankan ecological project home for disciplined desk-first inquiry into living continuity, material responsibility, and the continuation of useful relationships. It builds knowledge through evidence, sense-making, relationship mapping, uncertainty, ethics, and proportionate care before deciding what action—if any—is responsible.**
