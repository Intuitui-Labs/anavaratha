# Anavaratha

## Species-level research selection criteria

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working protocol, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

The next descent should move from broad organism categories into carefully selected species and relationship cases. The aim is not to produce a definitive list of Sri Lankan life. It is to choose representative cases that reveal how living systems function, how relationships continue, and where responsible cultivation or protection might later be possible.

No species should be treated as a recommendation merely because it is attractive, fast-growing, famous, medicinal, endangered, or easy to propagate.

## 2. Selection dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Ecological representation** | Does the case represent an important Sri Lankan habitat or ecological region? |
| **Relational richness** | Does it reveal relationships with soil, water, plants, animals, insects, fungi, or people? |
| **Regenerative relevance** | Does it help explain natural recovery, recruitment, habitat creation, soil, water, or continuity? |
| **Conservation significance** | Is it endemic, endangered, threatened, rare, locally declining, or ecologically irreplaceable? |
| **Biocultural significance** | Does it connect with food, medicine, history, language, ritual, livelihood, or place? |
| **Functional relevance** | Does it have a documented role in shade, water, soil, pollination, seed dispersal, habitat, or food webs? |
| **Evidence availability** | Can identity, habitat, status, and relationships be supported by credible sources? |
| **Learning value** | Would studying it reveal something that a generic species list would not? |
| **Ethical manageability** | Can it be studied without exposing vulnerable locations, extracting knowledge, or creating new harm? |
| **Comparability** | Can it be compared with cases from other habitats or regions using common fields? |

## 3. Selection balance

The first research set should maintain balance across the following contrasts:

| Contrast | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Plant and animal** | Regeneration is not only a plant process |
| **Visible and less-visible** | Soil life, fungi, and microorganisms are part of living capacity |
| **Common and threatened** | Everyday ecological function matters alongside conservation urgency |
| **Forest and non-forest** | Sri Lankan living systems include farms, wetlands, coasts, gardens, and cities |
| **Wild and cultivated** | Human care and ecological autonomy interact differently |
| **Scientific and biocultural** | Different knowledge systems reveal different relationships |
| **Individual and network** | A species may matter because of the community and relationships around it |

## 4. Research order

The first species-level pass should proceed from relationships that are relatively observable toward relationships that require deeper specialist evidence.

1. **Structural trees:** identify candidates through habitat, shade, canopy, soil, water, food, and natural-regeneration relationships.
2. **Pollinator relations:** identify flowering plants and associated pollinator groups through seasonal observation and ecological literature.
3. **Seed-dispersal relations:** identify fruiting plants and animal dispersers through documented movement and recruitment relationships.
4. **Soil-life relations:** identify soil organisms, fungi, roots, and decomposition processes through ecological and soil evidence.
5. **Medicinal and food plants:** investigate species, names, uses, cultivation, seed, knowledge, safety, and conservation, including the Osu Uyana line of inquiry.
6. **Endemic or endangered beings:** investigate species status together with habitat, ecological relationships, and social responsibility.

This order is not a hierarchy of value. It is a practical movement from visible structure and relationships toward increasingly detailed and sensitive records.

## 5. Minimum evidence threshold

A species or relationship may enter the preliminary atlas when its identity and relevance are plausible and documented. It may guide a future cultivation hypothesis only when habitat, conditions, stewardship, and potential unintended effects are sufficiently understood.

| Stage | Allowed claim |
|---|---|
| **Lead** | A possible species or relationship worth investigating |
| **Documented** | Identity and basic relevance supported by a credible source |
| **Contextualised** | Habitat, status, function, season, and human relationship described |
| **Connected** | Associated beings, conditions, stewardship, and wider landscape relationships mapped |
| **Actionable** | A proportionate care or cultivation hypothesis can be stated responsibly |

The atlas should preserve the distinction between these stages.

## 6. Ethical filters

The research should not disclose precise locations of vulnerable populations when doing so could increase harm. Medicinal knowledge should not be treated as an unregulated source of remedies. Local names and knowledge should be recorded with attribution and consent. Endangered organisms should not be propagated or moved without appropriate specialist and institutional guidance. Restoration should not create new burdens for communities or wildlife.

## 7. Working rule

> **Choose cases for the relationships they reveal, not only for the names they carry.**

The first species-level atlas is successful if it teaches Anavaratha how to recognise ecological capacity, biocultural meaning, uncertainty, and responsible care.
