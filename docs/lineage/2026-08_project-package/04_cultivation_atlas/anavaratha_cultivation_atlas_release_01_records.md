# Anavaratha

## Cultivation Atlas Release 01

**Controlled record set — Version 0.1**

## 1. Reading this release

These records are deliberately compact. Each one preserves the difference between a source fact, an Anavaratha cross-link, and a future question. A record’s inclusion means that it is useful for demonstrating the atlas method; it does not mean that the subject is recommended for planting, medicinal use, propagation, collection, or intervention.

The medicinal-plant entries draw their identity fields from Appendix 1 of the 2025 checklist. The article reports 1,476 medicinal plant species, while the current machine-assisted extraction remains incomplete at 1,349 conservative working candidates. The complete checklist is therefore treated as a governed source layer rather than reproduced here as a second catalogue [1] [2].

### CA01-R01 — Yellow-eared Bulbul, *Pycnonotus penicillatus*

A peer-reviewed study investigated this endemic and threatened bird in Horton Plains National Park from September 2015 to November 2017. It used focal-animal sampling, faecal analysis, field-guide identification, monthly observations of tagged plants, and ripe-fruit-cover estimates. The study documents 16 feeding plants and seeds of nine plant species in faecal samples [3].

The record’s relationship is labelled **frugivory with seed-ingestion evidence**. It is not labelled confirmed effective seed dispersal because the available evidence does not by itself establish post-defecation germination or recruitment for every plant. The geography, study period, methods, and seasonal frame remain attached to the record.

### CA01-R02 — Horton Plains fruiting-plant community

The same study reports that *Rubus ellipticus* was the major fruit source and that ripe-fruit maxima varied by plant and season. *Solanum mauritianum* had the highest ripe-fruit cover during the northeast monsoon, first inter-monsoon, and second inter-monsoon seasons, while *Berberis ceylanica* had the highest ripe-fruit cover during the southwest monsoon. Associations between feeding attempts and fruit cover or numbers of fruiting trees were species-specific [3].

This record is intentionally a **community and seasonal record**, not a planting list. It demonstrates that a cultivation atlas must represent temporal food availability, multiple plant species, bird status, habitat, and ecological risk together. The study also reports feeding on introduced *Passiflora tripartita*; food use by a bird does not automatically make an introduced plant suitable for restoration.

### CA01-H01 — Ganewatta Medicinal Plant Garden

The official Department of National Botanic Gardens page verifies the project identity “Ganewatta Medicinal Plant Garden.” The accessible page does not itself use or define “Osu Uyana,” so the record remains separate from the terminology record. Its management model, plant holdings, educational role, and ecological design require further evidence [4].

### CA01-H02 — Osu Uyana

Contemporary Sri Lankan herbal-garden material uses “Osu Uyana” in Sinhala-language medicinal-garden contexts, including gardens associated with cultivation, production, demonstration, and retained ecological features [5]. Broader scholarly evidence supports a history of managed royal, monastic, and home gardens in Sri Lanka, but that evidence does not prove that an ancient formal institution was named “Osu Uyana” or that modern gardens are uninterrupted continuations under that exact name [10].

The record therefore treats Osu Uyana as a **contemporary category and research doorway**, with local meanings and institutional uses still requiring source-by-source clarification.

### CA01-G01 — Institutional evidence pathways

The atlas connects the National Herbarium to taxonomic authentication and voucher evidence; the Forest Department to forest administration, GIS, and inventory pathways; DWC to protected areas, corridors, permits, and species governance; and CEA/CBD gateways to wetland and biodiversity information [6] [7] [8] [9]. These are not interchangeable data sources. Each retains its own authority, access conditions, spatial scale, and update status.

## 4. Osu Uyana integration

The Osu Uyana inquiry adds a living-system layer to Release 01. CA01-H01 Ganewatta and CA01-H02 Osu Uyana are no longer treated as simple garden entries; they are connected to the seven-layer model of landscape, habitat conditions, living community, medicinal knowledge, continuity systems, stewardship and governance, and time/adaptation.

| Atlas connection | Release 01 interpretation | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Garden form ↔ landscape** | Ganewatta is documented in Sri Lanka’s Intermediate Zone; other Osu Uyana forms must be situated in their own ecological and cultural landscapes | Do not generalise Ganewatta conditions to all Sri Lankan gardens |
| **Garden ↔ soil, water, shade** | Garden viability depends on local physical and biological conditions | Soil, water, and microclimate fields remain to be verified for individual sites |
| **Medicinal plants ↔ living community** | Plants may connect to pollinators, birds, fungi, soil organisms, and people | Each relationship needs its own source and method |
| **Garden ↔ knowledge** | Sinhala names, practitioner knowledge, household practice, institutional science, and teaching may meet | Translation and reuse must preserve authority, consent, and attribution |
| **Garden ↔ continuity** | Seeds, mother plants, nurseries, herbarium records, and propagation support continuity | Provenance, genetic diversity, and exchange rules remain open |
| **Garden ↔ stewardship** | Garden managers, institutions, households, schools, communities, and regulators may share responsibility | Ownership does not by itself reveal all care relationships |
| **Garden ↔ time** | Flowering, fruiting, harvest, labour, succession, and climate variability shape the garden | Seasonal and longitudinal records remain limited |

The expanded Osu Uyana model is recorded in [`anavaratha_osu_uyana_living_system_model_v01.md`](anavaratha_osu_uyana_living_system_model_v01.md), with documented garden forms, ecological conditions, and stewardship controls in the related inquiry files. These documents deepen the atlas but do not convert any entry into a species recommendation or action plan.
