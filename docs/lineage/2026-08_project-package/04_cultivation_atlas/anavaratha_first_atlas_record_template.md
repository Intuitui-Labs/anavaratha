# Anavaratha

## First living-sector atlas record template

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working template, Version 0.1**

## 1. Use of this template

This template is for recording a living being or relationship without reducing it to a planting recommendation. Each record should connect identity, habitat, ecological role, biocultural meaning, seasonality, stewardship, evidence, and uncertainty.

The first atlas will use six clusters:

| Cluster | Initial unit of inquiry |
|---|---|
| **Structural trees** | A native or locally appropriate tree and the habitat functions it supports |
| **Pollinator relations** | A flowering plant, its pollinators, and seasonal continuity |
| **Seed-dispersal relations** | A fruiting plant, its dispersers, and recruitment conditions |
| **Soil-life relations** | A soil organism, root association, or decomposition relationship |
| **Medicinal and food plants** | A plant connected to nutrition, medicine, cultivation, and knowledge |
| **Endemic or endangered beings** | A priority organism and the habitat and social relationships required for its continuity |

## 2. Record identification

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Record ID** | Unique identifier |
| **Cluster** | One of the six initial clusters, or a justified additional category |
| **Record type** | Organism, habitat, relationship, condition, stewardship, or change |
| **Working title** | Plain-language title |
| **Date created or updated** | Date of record |
| **Researcher or contributor** | Person or group responsible for the record |
| **Status** | Draft, under review, verified, contested, or archived |

## 3. Identity and place

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Common name** | Name or names used in the relevant context |
| **Sinhala name** | To be verified and attributed where relevant |
| **Tamil name** | To be verified and attributed where relevant |
| **Scientific name** | Taxonomic name and authority where available |
| **Taxonomic confidence** | High, moderate, low, or unknown |
| **Place** | Ecological region, district, watershed, settlement, or site |
| **Habitat** | Forest, wetland, farm, garden, coast, stream, tank, urban patch, or other |
| **Spatial precision** | Exact, approximate, landscape-level, or intentionally obscured |
| **Source location** | Map, coordinates, administrative reference, or community description |

## 4. Ecological profile

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Life form** | Tree, shrub, herb, grass, insect, bird, mammal, fish, fungus, microorganism, or other |
| **Status** | Native, endemic, introduced, threatened, endangered, rare, common, recovering, or uncertain |
| **Life requirements** | Soil, water, shade, food, shelter, breeding, nesting, temperature, and seasonal needs |
| **Ecological roles** | Habitat, shade, water, soil, pollination, seed dispersal, decomposition, predation, food-web, or other |
| **Associated beings** | Plants, animals, insects, fungi, microorganisms, and communities |
| **Seasonal pattern** | Flowering, fruiting, breeding, migration, dormancy, harvest, or care period |
| **Regenerative process** | Natural regeneration, recruitment, succession, propagation, recovery, or restoration |

## 5. Human and biocultural profile

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Food relation** | Nutrition, edible part, household use, seasonal use, or food-system role |
| **Medicinal relation** | Use, preparation, knowledge holder, evidence, cultivation, and sustainability |
| **Historical relation** | Place, memory, story, lineage, land-use history, or historical change |
| **Cultural relation** | Ritual, language, identity, art, religion, or customary practice |
| **Livelihood relation** | Income, labour, material, farming, fishing, craft, tourism, or other economic relation |
| **Knowledge holders** | People, communities, healers, farmers, elders, institutions, or researchers |
| **Consent and attribution** | Permission, attribution, reciprocity, and restrictions on use |

## 6. Relationship statement

Every record should include a concise relationship statement in the following form:

> **In [place or habitat], [being A] is related to [being or system B] through [relationship]. This relationship depends on [conditions], occurs during [season or time], and is supported or constrained by [ecological and social factors].**

Examples of relationship types include pollination, seed dispersal, shade, soil formation, water retention, shelter, predation, decomposition, food, medicine, cultural meaning, and stewardship.

## 7. Evidence record

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Source type** | Peer-reviewed study, government source, institutional report, community knowledge, field observation, map, image, or other |
| **Citation or link** | Full reference or URL |
| **Date of source** | Publication or observation date |
| **Geographic coverage** | Site, district, region, national, or other |
| **Method** | Survey, interview, remote sensing, experiment, review, observation, or other |
| **Claim supported** | Specific claim, not a general source label |
| **Confidence** | High, moderate, low, or unknown |
| **Limitations** | Missing data, old source, uncertain identity, limited sample, or scale mismatch |
| **Verification needed** | What should be checked next |

## 8. Regenerative-condition assessment

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| **Existing capacity** | What is already regenerating, functioning, or being cared for? |
| **Limiting condition** | What restricts continuity, diversity, movement, or reproduction? |
| **Minimum useful care** | Protection, observation, assisted regeneration, propagation, habitat creation, or other |
| **Potential unintended effects** | Access, livelihood, wildlife, water, genetic, cultural, or institutional risks |
| **Stewardship requirement** | Who must care, observe, decide, or maintain? |
| **Time horizon** | Seasonal, annual, multi-year, or intergenerational |
| **Success signal** | What change would indicate improved relationship or condition? |
| **Non-success signal** | What would indicate harm, mismatch, or need to stop and revise? |

## 9. Review protocol

A record should pass four reviews before guiding cultivation:

| Review | Question |
|---|---|
| **Identity review** | Is the organism or relationship correctly identified? |
| **Ecological review** | Is the habitat, function, season, and condition supported by evidence? |
| **Biocultural review** | Are local names, knowledge, use, consent, and attribution handled responsibly? |
| **Stewardship review** | Is there a realistic and accountable way to support continuity? |

A record may remain useful even when it fails to support immediate action. Unknown or contested records should remain visible as research priorities, not be converted into confident interventions.

## 10. Output format

The first atlas should produce one record per selected organism or relationship, a network diagram showing associations, a seasonal calendar, a map of habitats and conditions, and an uncertainty register. The atlas should support comparison without forcing all beings into one measure.

> **The record is complete only when it shows not just what a being is, but what allows it to remain in relationship.**
