# Anavaratha

## Living-sector atlas prototype scope

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Prototype definition, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

The first atlas prototype is a controlled test of Anavaratha’s source-linked relationship model. It is intentionally small. It does not attempt to represent all Sri Lankan species, habitats, medicinal knowledge, or restoration work.

The prototype asks whether existing records can be connected across:

> **organism → habitat → relationship → condition → stewardship → evidence → uncertainty**

The prototype should demonstrate the structure before Anavaratha invests in a larger database, map, platform, or field programme.

## 2. Prototype record set

| Record group | Initial records or cases | Why included |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural tree** | Kumbuk (*Terminalia arjuna*) as a documented identity lead; Hora (*Dipterocarpus zeylanicus*) as a research lead | Tests tree identity, habitat fit, shade, water, forest structure, and regeneration |
| **Pollination** | Mango–bee relationship in Jaffna homegardens; dry-zone flower-visitor study as a network lead | Tests plant–insect interaction, seasonality, and cultivated habitat |
| **Seed dispersal** | One fruiting-plant–disperser record to be selected only after species-level verification | Tests movement, seed deposition, recruitment, and habitat connectivity |
| **Soil life** | AMF–rice-root relationship | Tests less-visible life, growth stage, soil condition, and evidence limits |
| **Medicinal plants** | Checklist-to-Herbarium crosswalk; Osu Uyana as a contextual lead | Tests taxonomy, conservation, knowledge, cultivation, and ethical handling |
| **Food and homegardens** | Jaffna and Kandyan homegarden systems | Tests food, trees, shade, biodiversity, seed, household, and culture relations |
| **Endangered being** | Red Fin Labeo (*Labeo lankae*) and Malwathu Oya Basin | Tests species–habitat–basin–governance relationships |
| **Stewardship** | National Herbarium, Forest Department, DWC, CEA/CBD, universities, communities, and knowledge holders | Tests responsibility, authority, access, and care links |

## 3. Inclusion rules

A record enters the prototype when it meets four conditions. Its identity or relationship is supported by a source or clearly labelled as a research lead. Its geographic and temporal scope is recorded. Its relationship to the living-sector atlas is explicit. Its uncertainty and next verification step are visible.

A record does not enter as an actionable recommendation merely because it is included in the prototype.

## 4. Exclusion rules

The prototype excludes a national planting list, unsupported claims of ecosystem benefit, unverified local names, exact sensitive locations, clinical claims about medicinal plants, universal claims based on one regional study, and project objectives presented as measured outcomes.

The present prototype is focused on regenerative cultivation and living relationships. Waste-management and pollution-flow materials are not part of this prototype.

## 5. Prototype fields

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Record ID** | Stable identifier |
| **Record group** | Tree, pollination, dispersal, soil, medicinal, food, endangered, or stewardship |
| **Entity** | Organism, habitat, relationship, institution, or condition |
| **Place** | Sri Lankan region, basin, habitat, or study area |
| **Relationship** | Ecological, biocultural, institutional, or stewardship relation |
| **Condition** | What allows the relationship to continue |
| **Source** | Original page, article, catalogue, or project record |
| **Evidence statement** | Narrow claim supported by source |
| **Status** | Lead, documented, contextualised, cross-linked, or actionable |
| **Confidence** | High, moderate, low, or unknown |
| **Limitation** | Scope, age, method, access, taxonomy, or ethics |
| **Stewardship** | Who observes, cares, decides, or holds responsibility |
| **Next action** | Verify, extract, cross-check, request, map, or interview |

## 6. Prototype success criteria

The prototype is successful if it demonstrates that a researcher can trace every claim to an original source, distinguish identity from function, connect an organism to its habitat and relationships, see the conditions required for continuity, identify responsible stewards, recognise uncertainty, and determine what additional research is needed.

It is not successful merely because it contains many records or produces an attractive visual map.

## 7. Working principle

> **Small enough to inspect, rich enough to reveal relationships, disciplined enough to remain trustworthy.**
