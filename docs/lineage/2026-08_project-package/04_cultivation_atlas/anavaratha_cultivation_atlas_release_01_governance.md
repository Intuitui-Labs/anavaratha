# Anavaratha

## Cultivation Atlas Release 01

**Review states and governance — Version 0.1**

## 1. Why review states matter

An atlas that gathers names and relationships can easily create a false impression of certainty. Release 01 therefore treats review status as part of the knowledge itself. The status should tell the reader what has been checked, what kind of claim is supported, what remains uncertain, and what would be required before the record could guide care.

> **Uncertainty is not a defect to hide. It is a condition to govern.**

## 2. Review-state vocabulary

| State | Meaning | What the atlas may do |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovered** | A source, institution, term, or possible relationship has been located | Preserve the lead and identify the source to verify |
| **Inspected** | The original page, document, or record has been opened and its scope observed | Describe only the inspected scope |
| **Extracted** | Specific fields or wording have been transcribed with page or section provenance | Use the extracted fields with their original limits |
| **Visually validated** | A representative or selected record has been checked against the source image or layout | Use the validated fields while keeping the review scope explicit |
| **Cross-linked** | A relationship between records has a documented match rationale | Show the connection and its evidence type |
| **Contextualised** | Place, time, method, institution, access, and limitations are attached | Support a richer research interpretation |
| **Accepted** | The record is sufficiently reliable for the limited claim stated | Include in the controlled release |
| **Conditional** | The record is useful but requires a stated condition before wider use | Include with a visible caveat |
| **Merged** | Two records refer to the same entity or event and the match is verified | Keep provenance from both sources; do not erase differences |
| **Rejected** | The proposed record or connection is contradicted, spurious, duplicated, or outside scope | Keep the audit reason; do not include in the active release |
| **Unresolved** | Evidence is insufficient or conflicting | Preserve the question without forcing a conclusion |
| **Source-ambiguous** | The source, date, authority, translation, or record identity cannot be securely established | Withhold from substantive synthesis |
| **Field-required** | Desk research identifies a question that cannot responsibly be answered without future observation or local verification | Do not treat as action-ready |

## 3. Claim levels

A record may have different review states for different claims. For example, *Aponogeton jacobsenii* may be visually validated for its Appendix 1 name and status fields while remaining field-required for current habitat and sensitive-location information. The atlas must not allow a strong identity claim to silently upgrade a weak ecological or medicinal claim.

| Claim level | Example in Release 01 | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | Name, family, synonym, local name | Source row, taxonomic authority, page provenance |
| **Status** | 2012 or 2020 conservation field | Dated source assessment; no timeless substitution |
| **Occurrence** | Presence in a named habitat or site | Survey, specimen, institutional, or spatial source |
| **Relationship** | Bird consumes a fruit; fungus associates with a root | Named parties, method, place, and time |
| **Condition** | Fruiting changes across monsoon seasons | Repeated observation or seasonal study |
| **Care** | Protection, observation, propagation, or cultivation | Ecological, horticultural, community, and governance evidence |
| **Action** | Field visit, collection, planting, or intervention | Permission, risk review, consent, method, and accountable stewardship |

## 4. Ethical controls

### 4.1 Medicinal knowledge

A medicinal-plant checklist is not a medical treatment guide. Release 01 does not infer efficacy, dosage, safety, preparation, contraindications, or clinical suitability from checklist inclusion. Traditional and practitioner knowledge must be attributed in its own language and context, with consent, reciprocity, and protection against extractive publication.

### 4.2 Sensitive species and places

Threatened, endemic, rare, commercially valuable, or culturally sensitive organisms may require generalised locations or withheld coordinates. The atlas should not publish exact localities, collection sites, nesting sites, or medicinal-plant populations when disclosure could increase pressure or harm. A location field must therefore include a sensitivity decision, not merely a coordinate.

### 4.3 Institutional authority

The National Herbarium, Forest Department, DWC, CEA, universities, communities, practitioners, and garden managers do not have interchangeable authority. The atlas records which institution governs which claim or access pathway. DWC permission requirements remain relevant to protected-area research, protected species, collection, monitoring, and future field validation [1].

### 4.4 Local language and translation

“Osu Uyana” should remain in Sinhala and transliteration together where possible. Translation into “medicinal garden” is a useful orientation, but it must not erase local institutional or cultural meanings. Sinhala names in the medicinal checklist should be retained as printed, with normalisation treated as a separate operation.

### 4.5 Attribution and source integrity

Every record preserves the source, author or institution, date, method, page or section, and licence or access condition. The atlas distinguishes direct evidence from Anavaratha interpretation. A cross-link should be reversible: a reader must be able to return from the interpretation to the source record.

### 4.6 No silent action transition

A desk record cannot silently become a planting list, collecting mission, restoration prescription, or medicinal recommendation. Any transition toward action requires a new review gate covering ecological fit, harm, uncertainty, permissions, consent, resources, monitoring, and the possibility that protection or non-intervention is the more appropriate care.

## 5. Governance fields for every record

| Governance field | Required question |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | Who produced or holds the source? |
| **Permission** | Is access, reuse, collection, or field validation restricted? |
| **Sensitivity** | Could publication create ecological, cultural, commercial, or personal risk? |
| **Consent** | Does local or practitioner knowledge require consent and attribution? |
| **Language** | What language carries the original meaning? |
| **Temporal validity** | What date and assessment period does the claim belong to? |
| **Interpretation boundary** | What does Anavaratha add, and what does it not claim? |
| **Next responsible step** | What is the smallest ethical action needed to improve confidence? |

## 6. Release 01 governance position

Release 01 is **accepted as a desk-research demonstration**, conditional for ecological generalisation, unresolved for complete medicinal-checklist coverage, and field-required for any site-specific care or intervention. The release is therefore useful but deliberately non-prescriptive.
