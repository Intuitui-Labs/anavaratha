# Anavaratha

## First living-sector atlas

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Preliminary atlas, Version 0.1**

> **This atlas begins with relationships, not a planting list.**

## 1. Purpose

The first atlas translates Anavaratha’s living-sector direction into a small, researchable set of organism–relationship clusters. It is deliberately preliminary. Its purpose is to show how the research can descend from landscape to habitat, organism, relationship, condition, and proportionate care without pretending that the first records are complete.

The six records represent different forms of living capacity: structure, attraction, movement, soil, biocultural knowledge, and conservation responsibility.

## 2. Initial atlas records

### Record L-01 — Structural tree and shade–habitat relation

| Field | Preliminary entry |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | Structural and shade-bearing native trees |
| **Organism** | A site-appropriate native tree; exact species to be selected through ecological-region research |
| **Relationship** | Tree → shade, microclimate, soil protection, habitat, food, and future regeneration |
| **Conditions to investigate** | Soil, water, elevation, canopy need, associated species, natural regeneration, and maintenance |
| **Why selected** | Trees are visible entry points into less-visible relationships among soil, water, insects, birds, fungi, and people |
| **Evidence status** | Conceptual; species-specific record not yet verified |
| **Next research** | Compare candidate trees by habitat fit, ecological function, cultural relation, seed continuity, and long-term care |

This record prevents the project from beginning with a generic tree list. The tree becomes an entry point into a living community and a question of what kind of structure a place requires.

### Record L-02 — Flowering plant and pollinator relation

| Field | Preliminary entry |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | Flowering plants and pollinators |
| **Organisms** | Flowering plant–bee, butterfly, moth, fly, beetle, bird, or other pollinator relationships |
| **Relationship** | Flowering plant → nectar, pollen, shelter, and seasonal resource; pollinator → reproduction and ecological connection |
| **Conditions to investigate** | Flowering calendar, habitat, water, shelter, pesticide exposure, nesting, and surrounding plant diversity |
| **Why selected** | Pollination makes interdependence visible and can connect food, biodiversity, garden, and learning systems |
| **Evidence status** | Supported as a general ecological relationship; local species pairings require verification |
| **Next research** | Document Sri Lankan flowering calendars and pollinator associations across contrasting habitats |

The Metta Garden case in Colombo provides a local example of an urban garden intentionally creating space for butterflies, bees, other insects, food, and learning [1]. The atlas should use it as a case of relationship-oriented cultivation, not as proof that one design works everywhere.

### Record L-03 — Fruiting plant and seed-disperser relation

| Field | Preliminary entry |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | Fruiting plants and seed dispersers |
| **Organisms** | Fruiting tree or plant–bird, bat, mammal, or other disperser relationships |
| **Relationship** | Fruiting plant → food; disperser → seed movement and regeneration |
| **Conditions to investigate** | Fruiting season, movement, shelter, germination sites, habitat continuity, and human–wildlife relations |
| **Why selected** | Seed dispersal connects individual plants to forest recovery, habitat connectivity, and animal movement |
| **Evidence status** | General relationship; Sri Lankan species-specific pairings require verification |
| **Next research** | Identify documented fruiting plants and dispersers by ecological region and season |

This record asks whether cultivation can support the conditions for natural recruitment rather than merely placing mature plants in the ground.

### Record L-04 — Soil-life and root relation

| Field | Preliminary entry |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | Soil organisms, roots, fungi, and decomposition |
| **Organisms** | Soil organisms, fungi, roots, decomposers, and plants; exact organisms to be selected through soil research |
| **Relationship** | Soil life ↔ roots, organic matter, decomposition, nutrient cycling, structure, and moisture |
| **Conditions to investigate** | Soil texture, organic matter, moisture, disturbance, roots, litter, temperature, and biological activity |
| **Why selected** | Regeneration depends on less-visible living conditions that a tree or garden survey may overlook |
| **Evidence status** | Ecological framework established; local organism relationships require site-specific evidence |
| **Next research** | Develop practical soil-life indicators and compare methods suitable for Sri Lankan contexts |

This record protects Anavaratha from equating regenerative cultivation with above-ground appearance. Soil is not only a medium in which plants are placed; it is a living system whose condition determines future capacity.

### Record L-05 — Medicinal and food plant relation: Osu Uyana line of inquiry

| Field | Preliminary entry |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | Medicinal and food plants, including the Osu Uyana line of inquiry |
| **Organisms** | Medicinal, edible, nutritional, household, and culturally significant plants; exact set to be verified |
| **Relationship** | Plant ↔ health, food, cultivation, seed, knowledge, household, livelihood, and culture |
| **Conditions to investigate** | Identity, local names, propagation, sustainable harvest, knowledge holders, evidence, access, and consent |
| **Why selected** | Connects biodiversity with human wellbeing, living knowledge, food, medicine, and biocultural continuity |
| **Evidence status** | The concept is relevant; “Osu Uyana” and its institutional or local meanings require careful verification |
| **Next research** | Identify Sri Lankan medicinal-garden contexts, Sinhala and Tamil terminology, species, knowledge protocols, and conservation needs |

This record must not turn medicinal plants into commodities or present traditional use as a medical guarantee. It should prioritise knowledge reciprocity, conservation, safe identity, propagation, and responsible access.

### Record L-06 — Endemic or endangered being and habitat relation

| Field | Preliminary entry |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | Endemic or endangered beings |
| **Organism** | Red Fin Labeo (*Labeo lankae*) as an initial anchor case |
| **Relationship** | Endemic fish ↔ freshwater habitat, basin, water conditions, food web, movement, and conservation responsibility |
| **Conditions to investigate** | Distribution, habitat, water quality, flow, breeding, threats, climate effects, local knowledge, and governance |
| **Why selected** | IUCN Sri Lanka identifies it as an endemic, critically endangered fish whose current evidence is restricted to the mid and lower Malwathu Oya Basin [2] |
| **Evidence status** | Initial species and conservation context verified through IUCN; detailed ecological record still required |
| **Next research** | Obtain species-level studies and basin evidence before making any cultivation or habitat recommendation |

This record demonstrates that the living sector includes animals and aquatic systems, not only plants. The responsible response may be habitat protection, monitoring, basin coordination, or another form of care rather than direct propagation.

## 3. Cross-cluster relationship map

| Cluster | Primary relationship | Secondary relationships | Regenerative condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structural tree** | Shade and habitat | Soil, water, food, nesting, seed | Ecological fit and natural recruitment |
| **Pollinator plant** | Attraction and pollination | Food, seasonal continuity, seed | Flowering diversity and habitat |
| **Fruiting plant** | Food and seed dispersal | Movement, regeneration, habitat | Fruiting continuity and disperser access |
| **Soil life** | Root and decomposition relation | Nutrients, moisture, structure | Living soil and organic matter |
| **Medicinal/food plant** | Human nourishment and knowledge | Seed, livelihood, culture, pollination | Responsible cultivation and knowledge continuity |
| **Endangered being** | Habitat dependence | Food web, water, movement, conservation | Protected habitat and accountable governance |

## 4. What the first atlas is designed to reveal

The first atlas should reveal that ecological cultivation is not a set of unrelated activities. Shade may alter soil and water. Flowering plants may support insects and food production. Fruiting plants may support animals and forest recruitment. Soil life may determine whether trees and food plants can continue. Medicinal plants may connect biodiversity with cultural knowledge and household wellbeing. An endangered animal may reveal the condition of an entire habitat or basin.

These relationships form a network of possible leverage, but not every relationship should be acted upon immediately. The research must establish whether an intervention strengthens a living system, whether the effect is place-specific, and whether the people responsible for care have the authority and capacity to continue it.

## 5. Provisional atlas rule

> **Select a living being because it helps reveal or strengthen a relationship, not because it is merely attractive, famous, endangered, medicinal, fast-growing, or easy to plant.**

The atlas should include both celebrated and ordinary beings, visible and less-visible life, and relationships that are culturally meaningful as well as scientifically measurable.
