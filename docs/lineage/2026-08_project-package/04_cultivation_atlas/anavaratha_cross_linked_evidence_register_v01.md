# Anavaratha

## Cross-linked evidence register and gap map

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Working synthesis, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This register connects existing sources to the living-sector atlas without treating any source as more complete than it is. Each row identifies a supported evidence area, the atlas relation it can populate, and the gap that remains.

## 4. Gap map

| Gap class | Current condition | Why it matters | Responsible next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Identity gap** | Names and taxonomies may differ across sources | Incorrect identification can invalidate all later records | Crosswalk with National Herbarium and current taxonomic sources |
| **Spatial gap** | National layers may be too coarse for habitat decisions | A three-zone map cannot determine local ecological fit | Add watershed, soil, habitat, elevation, and local studies |
| **Temporal gap** | Sources range from historical reports to current articles | Ecological status and land use change over time | Record date and compare historical/current evidence |
| **Relationship gap** | Catalogues often list organisms without interactions | Regeneration depends on relationships, not lists | Connect species to shade, pollination, dispersal, soil, water, and care |
| **Seasonal gap** | Flowering, fruiting, breeding, rainfall, and care calendars are incomplete | Relationships may exist only during specific periods | Build seasonal records from studies and local observation |
| **Status gap** | Threat categories and distribution may change | Historical status can mislead present action | Cross-check current national and global assessments |
| **Access gap** | Some resources are public gateways without underlying data | Discovery does not equal extractability | Record contacts, permissions, licences, and formats |
| **Knowledge gap** | Community and medicinal knowledge may be under-recorded or sensitive | Extraction can harm knowledge holders and living systems | Establish consent, attribution, reciprocity, and protection protocols |
| **Evaluation gap** | Project pages may describe intent or success without long-term outcomes | Visible activity can be mistaken for regeneration | Seek monitoring, failures, unintended effects, and maintenance records |
| **Governance gap** | Institutional mandates overlap across forests, wildlife, wetlands, and environment | Responsibility may be unclear at a place | Build a responsibility and permit map before field entry |
| **Synthesis gap** | Existing sources are distributed across institutions and scales | Important relationships remain disconnected | Create a linked evidence layer, not another isolated catalogue |

## 5. Provisional source-to-action logic

The cross-linked register should guide four types of action:

| Evidence condition | Anavaratha response |
|---|---|
| Existing authoritative record and usable access | Link and cite; do not recreate |
| Existing record with missing relationship or context | Add a synthesis layer with clear provenance |
| Existing record with unclear currency or scale | Cross-check and mark limitations |
| Important gap or inaccessible source | Document the gap and define a responsible request or research pathway |

## 6. Current conclusion

The source landscape confirms the value of the anti-duplication approach. Sri Lanka already has institutional knowledge, plant authentication, medicinal-plant catalogues, protected-area information, wetland directories, spatial layers, university studies, conservation projects, and local cases. The central gap is not necessarily the absence of information. It is the fragmentation of information across institutions, scales, languages, methods, dates, and access conditions.

Anavaratha’s distinctive contribution can therefore be a **relationship-aware synthesis layer** that makes existing knowledge findable and comparable while showing uncertainty and respecting rights.

> **Connect before collecting. Cross-check before claiming. Understand before intervening.**
