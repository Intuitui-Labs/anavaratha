# Anavaratha

## Atlas prototype limitations and research gaps

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Review, Version 0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This review prevents the prototype from being mistaken for a complete ecological database or a field-ready intervention plan. It identifies what the prototype demonstrates, what it does not yet establish, and what must be resolved before a relationship can guide action.

## 2. What the prototype demonstrates

The prototype demonstrates that existing sources can be connected across organisms, habitats, relationships, conditions, stewardship, and evidence. It also shows that the same living system can be seen through several scales: a tree, a homegarden, a wetland, a river basin, a protected area, an institution, or a community.

The prototype is strongest as a **research architecture** and a **source-linked sense-making tool**. It is not yet a complete dataset.

## 3. Current limitations

| Limitation | Effect on interpretation | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| **Small record set** | The prototype cannot represent Sri Lanka’s full biological or ecological diversity | Expand through balanced ecological-region and relationship selection |
| **Uneven source resolution** | Some records are species-level while others are national, project, or institutional | Preserve scale and do not compare unlike records as if equivalent |
| **Incomplete local detail** | Many records lack exact habitat, soil, water, season, or stewardship context | Add local studies and later place-based verification |
| **Taxonomic uncertainty** | Names, synonyms, and common names may differ | Cross-check with National Herbarium and current taxonomic sources |
| **Temporal mismatch** | Sources range across historical and recent periods | Record dates and treat change as evidence, not automatic error |
| **Relationship gaps** | Catalogues often identify beings without documenting interactions | Add pollination, dispersal, soil, water, shade, and care evidence |
| **Seasonal gaps** | Flowering, fruiting, breeding, rainfall, and care periods are incomplete | Build seasonal calendars from studies and appropriate observation |
| **Access constraints** | Some underlying datasets, permits, or specimens may require request | Keep access and reuse conditions visible |
| **Knowledge ethics** | Medicinal and local knowledge cannot be extracted casually | Establish consent, attribution, reciprocity, and protection |
| **Outcome uncertainty** | Project descriptions may document intentions more than long-term outcomes | Separate activities, outputs, outcomes, and ecological recovery |
| **No field validation** | Source connections have not yet been tested in a specific living place | Treat records as research hypotheses until responsibly verified |

## 5. Readiness assessment

| Use | Current readiness | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Conceptual discussion** | Ready | Framework and relationships are clear |
| **Source discovery** | Ready | Institutional and catalogue pathways are mapped |
| **Literature review** | Ready with caution | Sources and gaps are identified; full extraction remains incomplete |
| **Species recommendation** | Not ready | Habitat, provenance, season, and consequences remain insufficiently verified |
| **Planting design** | Not ready | No site-specific ecological or stewardship diagnosis exists |
| **Field intervention** | Not ready | No place, consent, authority, baseline, or learning design has been established |
| **Atlas prototype expansion** | Ready | Common fields and research gaps are defined |

## 6. Decision rule for future action

A record may guide a field-facing cultivation hypothesis only when identity, habitat, relationship, condition, stewardship, potential burden, and evidence are sufficiently understood for the relevant place and scale. A record may still guide further desk research even when it is not field-ready.

> **Not ready for intervention does not mean not useful. It means the record is currently most valuable as a better question.**

## 7. Next research priorities

The next desk-based pass should extract actual catalogue fields from the 2025 medicinal-plant checklist, verify National Herbarium pathways, locate current forest and protected-area layers, inspect the National Wetland Directory, verify Osu Uyana contexts, and build multilingual and seasonal fields. It should then select a small number of records for deeper, source-complete relationship profiles.

## 8. Working conclusion

The prototype has reached the point at which its architecture is useful, but its records should remain provisional. Its central achievement is not numerical completeness. It is the disciplined separation of evidence, interpretation, relationship, uncertainty, and future care.

> **The atlas should become more specific before it becomes larger.**
