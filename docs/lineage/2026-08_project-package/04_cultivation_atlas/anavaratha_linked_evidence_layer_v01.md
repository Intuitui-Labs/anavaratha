# Anavaratha

## Linked evidence layer

**Sri Lanka regenerative-cultivation dimension — Crosswalk synthesis, Version 0.2**

## 1. Purpose

This linked evidence layer brings together the first Anavaratha crosswalks without collapsing different catalogues, institutions, or knowledge systems into a single false database. It shows where records can be connected and where differences, missing data, access limits, and ethical conditions must remain visible.

The layer connects:

> **organism ↔ habitat ↔ relationship ↔ place ↔ season ↔ institution ↔ knowledge ↔ stewardship ↔ evidence**

## 4. Crosswalk logic by research descent

### 4.1 Landscape to habitat

Ecological-zone and watershed layers provide orientation. Forest, wetland, homegarden, coastal, agricultural, and urban records provide habitat context. A national three-zone layer is never sufficient to determine local ecological fit; the local record must add soil, water, elevation, season, land use, and existing living relationships.

### 4.2 Habitat to organism

The habitat record should connect to trees, plants, animals, insects, fungi, microorganisms, and other living beings. Presence alone is not enough. Each organism record should state whether it is resident, seasonal, historical, recovering, threatened, or uncertain, and what evidence supports that classification.

### 4.3 Organism to relationship

The organism record should connect to shade, water, soil, pollination, seed dispersal, food, medicine, habitat, movement, predation, cultural meaning, and stewardship only when the source supports the specific relationship. Identity evidence should not be silently extended into function evidence.

### 4.4 Relationship to condition

Every relationship should connect to its conditions: soil, moisture, shade, flowering, fruiting, shelter, breeding, movement, connectivity, season, knowledge, access, and care. The condition is the most important bridge to regenerative cultivation.

### 4.5 Condition to proportionate care

Only after the relationship and condition are understood should the atlas describe possible care. Possible care may mean protection, observation, natural regeneration, assisted regeneration, propagation, habitat creation, connectivity work, knowledge preservation, or institutional coordination. A planting intervention is only one possible form.

## 5. Evidence maturity

| Level | Current meaning for Anavaratha |
|---|---|
| **Discovered** | Source or institution located |
| **Inspected** | Original page or document opened and scope recorded |
| **Extracted** | Specific claims or fields recorded with provenance |
| **Cross-linked** | Related records connected with match reasoning |
| **Contextualised** | Place, time, scale, method, and limitations attached |
| **Testable** | A responsible relationship or care hypothesis can be stated |

The current linked layer is a mixture of **inspected, extracted, and cross-linked** records. It is not yet a field-ready dataset.

## 7. Uncertainty and access register

| Issue | Affected records | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Current taxonomy** | Plants, medicinal species, trees | Cross-check with National Herbarium and current taxonomic sources |
| **Historical status** | Medicinal-plant counts, wetland directory, older studies | Preserve publication date and do not claim current totals |
| **Coarse spatial resolution** | FAO GAEZ, national directories, global ranges | Use for orientation only; add local ecological evidence |
| **Incomplete range** | IUCN spatial data and threatened species | Record range uncertainty and cross-check national sources |
| **Underlying data not public** | Forest GIS, CEA databases, some DWC materials | Record as access lead; request rather than assume access |
| **Sensitive locations** | Threatened species, medicinal plants, community knowledge | Generalise, restrict, or withhold location details as appropriate |
| **Knowledge governance** | Medicinal plants, Osu Uyana, local names and practices | Consent, attribution, reciprocity, and protection required |
| **Project outcome uncertainty** | Restoration cases and recovery plans | Separate objectives, activities, outputs, and measured outcomes |

## 8. What Anavaratha should create next

The next product should not be another national catalogue. It should be a **small, source-linked atlas prototype** containing a limited number of organism–relationship records with full provenance. Each record should include source links, original wording where needed, normalised fields, spatial and temporal scope, ecological function, biocultural context, stewardship, uncertainty, and next verification step.

The prototype should test whether records from different catalogues can be joined without losing their differences. It should also identify which fields cannot be responsibly filled from existing sources and therefore require later specialist, community, or field research.

## 9. Working conclusion

Sri Lanka’s existing knowledge landscape is not empty; it is distributed. The value of Anavaratha lies in making relationships among these distributed resources visible while preserving institutional authority, local knowledge, uncertainty, and access conditions.

> **Anavaratha should not compete with existing knowledge. It should help existing knowledge meet itself.**

## 11. Updated maturity assessment

The linked evidence layer is now stronger in four areas: source provenance, institutional access constraints, contemporary medicinal-garden terminology, and seasonal species relationships. It remains a **desk-research evidence layer**, not an action-ready ecological plan.

| Evidence dimension | Current position |
|---|---|
| **Identity and taxonomy** | Improved through the checklist crosswalk and National Herbarium pathway, but the checklist still needs targeted row-level resolution |
| **Habitat and spatial context** | Improved through DWC, Forest Department, wetland, CEA, and WDPA pathways; current authoritative downloadable layers remain incomplete |
| **Relationships and seasonality** | Strengthened by the Horton Plains Yellow-eared Bulbul study; wider species-network coverage remains limited |
| **Medicinal-garden context** | Contemporary use of Osu Uyana is supported; historical exact-name continuity remains unverified |
| **Governance and ethics** | DWC permit, collection, collaboration, monitoring, and reporting conditions are now explicit |
| **Action readiness** | Not established; no planting, collection, protected-area visit, medicinal use, or site intervention should be inferred |

The immediate atlas task is targeted review, not scale expansion: complete the semi-manual medicinal-checklist row review, verify record-level taxonomic and status fields, preserve access and sensitivity constraints, and add only relationships whose methods, geography, season, and evidence are clear.
