# Anavaratha Enforcement Institution and Capacity Map v0.1

## Purpose

Sri Lanka’s waste enforcement system is distributed. The same failure can involve a local authority that collects or disposes waste, a provincial authority responsible for coordination, a regulator responsible for licences and environmental compliance, a land or development authority, police, courts, and ecological agencies. Anavaratha therefore maps **responsibility chains**, not just named institutions.

## Institutional map

|---|---|---|---|

## Compliance-production chain

A legal system produces observable compliance only if the following chain functions:

1. **Rule clarity:** the responsible actors can identify the applicable prohibition or duty.
2. **Boundary clarity:** the site, material, period, and responsible party are defined.
3. **Detection:** a complaint, inspection, CCTV record, field observation, or environmental signal identifies possible non-compliance.
4. **Attribution:** waste is linked to a generator, transporter, contractor, council, or facility.
5. **Evidence integrity:** photographs, samples, records, witnesses, and chain of custody support the allegation.
6. **Authority action:** an authorized institution issues a notice, direction, licence action, or charge.
7. **Adjudication:** a court or competent authority determines liability.
8. **Sanction or remedy:** a fine, imprisonment, cost, corrective measure, restoration, or operational change is ordered.
9. **Implementation:** the responsible actor actually performs the order.
10. **Verification:** a later inspection or measurement confirms correction.
11. **Persistence:** the corrected condition remains over time.

The absence of evidence at one stage should not be coded as proof that the stage did not occur. It should be coded as **unknown**, followed by a specific retrieval request.

## Meethotamulla accountability pattern

The official Supreme Court judgment demonstrates why the chain must include multiple institutions. It found different forms of responsibility involving CMC, Kolonnawa Urban Council, the Urban Development Authority, Western Province Waste Management Authority, and CEA. The Court issued restoration and future-programme directions, while noting that the fundamental-rights jurisdiction and evidence did not support individual penal consequences.[1]

This is not a story of one absent law. It is a case of distributed responsibility, weak escalation, prolonged operation beyond safe limits, land-use decisions, and failed enforcement. Anavaratha should use it as a template for tracing where capacity and accountability separated.

## Capacity indicators to retrieve

The highest-value administrative records are not only total budgets or staff counts. They are operational records that show whether capacity was usable: vehicle availability by day; route completion; missed collections; disposal tonnage by destination; facility opening and downtime; maintenance and repair; staff vacancies and safety; weighbridge logs; environmental-protection licences; complaints and response times; notices and prosecutions; fines imposed and paid; restoration orders; and post-order inspections.

## Interpretation rule

> **An institution can have formal authority without operational capacity, and it can have operational activity without transparent evidence. Anavaratha must measure both.**
