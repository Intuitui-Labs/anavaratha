# Anavaratha Enforcement Verification Agenda v0.1

## Objective

The purpose of the next evidence round is not to prove that institutions are dishonest or that all open dumping is illegal. It is to determine which links in the compliance-production chain are observable, which are missing, and whether formal compliance signals correspond to material conditions.

## Ranked desk-data requests

| Priority | Evidence request | Why it matters | Preferred source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current consolidated National Environmental Act and regulations with effective dates | Prevents use of outdated fine ranges or repealed provisions | Parliament, Environment Ministry, official Gazette |
| 2 | CEA annual reports and enforcement statistics | Shows inspections, complaints, notices, prosecutions, licences, and staffing | CEA annual reports and RTI/public records |
| 3 | Magistrate and court records for waste offences | Separates alleged conduct, charges, convictions, fines, payment, and restoration | Court registry, judgments, legal databases |
| 4 | Local-authority complaint, notice, fine, and disposal registers | Reveals local enforcement throughput and missing links | Municipal/urban/pradeshiya sabha records |
| 5 | Local-authority vehicle, route, staffing, and maintenance records | Measures usable service capacity rather than nominal assets | Councils and provincial authorities |
| 6 | Facility licences, weighbridge logs, maintenance, leachate, and monitoring records | Tests whether facility status corresponds to operating reality | CEA, councils, facility operators |
| 7 | Current dumpsite inventory with coordinates and legal status | Tests claims about open dumps, forest proximity, wetland proximity, and administrative transfer | CEA, WMA, local authorities, conservation agencies |
| 8 | Parliamentary questions, Auditor General reports, procurement and project evaluations | Identifies cost, delays, non-completion, and accountability mechanisms | Parliament, Auditor General, project agencies |
| 9 | Remote-sensing and geospatial change detection | Tests expansion, new scars, burn sites, and land-use proximity | Sentinel/Landsat, official land-cover maps |
| 10 | Community, worker, and civil-society documentation | Captures unreported, seasonal, informal, and receiving-site burdens | Verified complaints, interviews, NGOs, local monitoring |

## Verification questions

### Legality and authority

- Which law or regulation applied on the date of the conduct?
- Which institution had the duty and which had the power to act?
- Was the site licensed, approved, tolerated, or simply used?
- Was the material ordinary municipal waste, plastic, hazardous waste, sewage, construction waste, or another category?

### Detection and attribution

- How was the issue detected?
- Can the waste be attributed to a generator, transporter, contractor, facility, or council?
- Is there a chain of custody for photographs, samples, vehicle records, or witness evidence?

### Enforcement and remedy

- Was there a notice, direction, licence action, charge, or court filing?
- Was there a conviction or only an allegation?
- Was a fine imposed and paid?
- Was restoration ordered, funded, completed, and independently verified?

### Reality and persistence

- What happened materially to the waste after the official action?
- Did the burden move to another location or actor?
- Did the problem recur after the inspection, project, media attention, or court order?
- What evidence exists after three, six, and twelve months?

## Nuwara Eliya controlled check

The Moonplains/JICA record should not be converted into an allegation of current illegal forest dumping. The later controlled check should verify: current site boundary; present legal and licence status; current daily inflows; source segregation; rainy-season access; leachate and perennial-stream protection; maintenance and machinery uptime; current staffing; receiving-site expansion; nearby forest and lake/wetland condition; complaints; and any enforcement or court history.

## Decision gate before fieldwork

Fieldwork is justified only when the desk phase has identified:

1. A clearly bounded site or pathway.
2. A specific legal, capacity, or material-reality question.
3. A provisional explanation that competes with at least one alternative explanation.
4. A list of records or measurements that can distinguish those explanations.
5. A safe and ethical method for gathering evidence.
6. A plan to avoid publishing unverified allegations against individuals or institutions.

## Interpretation rule

> **Absence of a public record is an evidence gap, not proof of absence. Repeated physical, administrative, and legal signals are stronger than any single source.**
