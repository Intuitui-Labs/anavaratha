# Anavaratha Pressure–Capacity–Action Analysis v0.1

## Scope

This analysis links the real national pressure and management indicators already assembled with Sri Lanka’s capacity and action evidence. It remains national and source-specific. It does not rank local authorities or calculate a composite capacity score.

## Evidence alignment

| Layer | Available real evidence | Reference period | What can be said |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Population pressure | DCS provisional population, households, occupied housing by GN, district, and province | 2024 | Broad denominator structure is available |
| MSW pressure | World Bank MSW generation series | 2021–2022; projections 2030–2050 | A source-specific national pressure series exists |
| Collection outcome | World Bank collection coverage | 2021 | A historical collection reference point exists |
| Disposal pathway | World Bank treatment/disposal shares | 2016 | A historical pathway profile exists |
| Uncollected pathways | World Bank management of uncollected waste | 2012 | A historical uncontrolled-pathway profile exists |
| Stream composition | World Bank MSW composition | 2018 | A source-specific composition reference exists |
| Physical/operational capacity | Studies and issue brief describe land, vehicles, equipment, maintenance, staffing, technical competence, OHS, and funding constraints | Various study periods | Mechanisms are documented; national current quantities are not yet available |
| Action architecture | National Action Plan goals, agencies, deadlines, and activities | 2021–2030 plan | Intended actions are documented; completion and persistence are not yet verified |

## What the current numbers imply—and do not imply

The 2021 collection-coverage value of 47.35% and the National Action Plan’s 2025 goal of 80% collection are useful as a baseline–target pair. Their difference is 32.65 percentage points, but this must not be called a measured 2025 shortfall because the baseline and target are from different dates and the current outcome has not yet been independently retrieved.

The 2016 pathway profile reports 52.65% uncollected, 24.81% open dumped, 13.88% composted, 8.60% incinerated, and 0.06% sanitary-landfilled. These shares sum to approximately 100% within that source’s definition. They should not be combined with the 2021 collection coverage or the 2012 uncollected-waste pathways as if they were one time series.

The 2012 uncollected-waste profile reports 59.402% open burned, 29.353% dumped on land, 9.713% recycled or composted, 1.11% entering the aquatic environment, and 0.425% other. This is a denominator nested within uncollected waste, not a share of all generated waste.

## Pressure–capacity interpretation

The available evidence supports a provisional diagnosis of **capacity heterogeneity and pathway weakness**, not a single national capacity deficit. Population and activity pressure are unevenly distributed, while usable capacity depends on land, vehicles, staff, finance, maintenance, technical skill, coordination, enforcement, markets, and ecological receiving limits. A national percentage can therefore conceal several different mechanisms.

The capacity/action evidence indicates that interventions exist at policy, institutional, infrastructure, community, and private-sector levels. The central analytical problem is connection and persistence: whether a target becomes a funded activity; whether an activity creates a functioning pathway; whether the pathway produces a measured outcome; and whether that outcome survives after the initial project, funding, or political attention changes.

## Action pathway model

| Stage | Data question | Current evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Problem recognition | Was the waste mechanism defined? | Strong for several national and policy problems |
| Design | Was an action, target, agency, and KPI specified? | Strong in the National Action Plan |
| Inputs | Were finance, land, equipment, staffing, and authority available? | Partial and mostly qualitative |
| Implementation | Was the action initiated and operated? | Mixed; requires project-level records |
| Immediate output | Was a facility, route, training, registry, or monitoring system delivered? | Partial |
| Outcome | Did collection, recovery, leakage, pollution, cost, or exposure change? | Insufficient national evidence |
| Persistence | Did the change remain after support or leadership changed? | Major evidence gap |
| Spillover | Did burden move to another place, actor, material, or time? | Recognized but insufficiently measured |

## Next real-data requirements

The next data search should prioritize a national or provincial operational dataset containing local-authority-level waste generated and collected, fleet and route data, facility names and coordinates, capacity and operating status, staff and budget variables, recovery destinations, and reporting periods. It should also search for post-2021 evidence on the National Action Plan’s goals and for records of project implementation and persistence.

Until those datasets are retrieved and audited, Anavaratha should use the present material as a **national evidence scaffold** rather than a performance dashboard. The correct next question is not “Which locality scores worst?” but “Which national system type and missing capacity variable should be measured next?”

## Current decision

The real-data work has now established three useful layers: a population/activity denominator layer, a source-specific MSW pressure/pathway layer, and a capacity/action evidence layer. It has not yet established a compatible national operational capacity inventory. That missing inventory should be treated as the next research target rather than hidden through imputation or scoring.
