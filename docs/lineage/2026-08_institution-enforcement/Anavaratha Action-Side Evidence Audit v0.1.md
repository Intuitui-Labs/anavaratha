# Anavaratha Action-Side Evidence Audit v0.1

## Purpose

This audit asks not merely whether Sri Lanka has policies or projects, but what kind of evidence exists for capacity, implementation, outcome, and persistence. The purpose is to prevent targets and interventions from being mistaken for achieved waste-system improvement.

## Evidence classification

| Class | Meaning | Current examples |
|---|---|---|
| A: Baseline measurement | A value describing a condition at a defined time | 47.35% collection coverage in the World Bank subset for 2021; 2016 treatment/disposal shares |
| B: Capacity input | An asset, resource, institution, or enabling condition | Staff counts in the Western Province study; facilities, equipment, land, and maintenance references |
| C: Planned action | A policy goal or proposed activity | National Action Plan inventory, monitoring, producer registration, facility audit, and LA profiling actions |
| D: Implementation evidence | Evidence an action was initiated or carried out | Case records, project reports, implementation studies, completed records |
| E: Outcome evidence | A measured change relative to baseline or comparison | Requires before/after, counterfactual, or repeated observations |
| F: Persistence evidence | Evidence that change remained after support, financing, or attention changed | Largely unresolved in the current corpus |
| G: Spillover evidence | Evidence that burden, cost, risk, or material moved outside the intervention boundary | Mentioned in studies and issue briefs, but requires structured measurement |

## Source audit

| Source | Capacity evidence | Action evidence | Outcome evidence | Persistence evidence | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What a Waste 3.0 Sri Lanka subset | Limited; collection and treatment/disposal indicators are pathway outcomes, not asset inventories | Categorical legal, institutional, plastic-policy, and EPR status rows | Source-specific and historical numeric indicators | Not provided as a complete longitudinal assessment | Different years, definitions, and source qualities |
| National Action Plan on Plastic Waste Management 2021–2030 | Identifies monitoring cells, facility audits, local-authority profiles, and community collection as enabling actions | Detailed goals, deadlines, responsible agencies, and activities | Targets are specified, but current achievement is not established here | Not established | Plan is not an independent implementation evaluation |
| Western Province implementation study | Reports staffing structure and identifies land, vehicles, instruments, technology, labor, finance, and political support as relevant | Examines implementation variables through interviews and a survey across selected local governments | Explains implementation failure factors, not a current operational measurement | Not established | Study period and sampling boundary limit current generalization |
| GRID-Arendal issue brief | Reports infrastructure, maintenance, technical competence, OHS, finance, market, ecological, and data conditions from desk and site observations | Recommends infrastructure, audits, EPR, databases, localized systems, finance, and PPP maintenance | Observational and recommendation-oriented; not a controlled impact evaluation | Not established | Mixed national and site observations; several figures are source-specific or estimated |

## What the action evidence currently shows

The action-side record is richer than a simple absence-of-policy narrative. Sri Lanka has national policies, action plans, local-government responsibilities, community and private-sector initiatives, facility investments, monitoring proposals, and enforcement instruments.[1] [2] [3]

However, the evidence is much stronger for **intended action and diagnosed constraints** than for **verified outcomes and persistence**. The current corpus can identify the action architecture and likely bottlenecks, but it cannot yet establish which interventions changed collection, recovery, leakage, exposure, costs, or accumulated stocks over time.

## Priority audit questions

The next evidence search should determine whether each major National Action Plan goal has a traceable implementation record, measurable indicator, responsible reporting body, post-deadline status, and independent verification. The same logic should be applied to facilities, equipment, local-government programs, community collection, EPR, and plastic-reduction initiatives.

## Data fields for the next action register

Each action should be recorded with: action ID; problem addressed; material stream; responsible institution; supporting actors; geographic boundary; target population; start date; planned end date; actual status; funding source; capital input; recurrent operating requirement; output; expected outcome; measured outcome; comparison basis; independent source; current operating status; persistence status; spillover; and uncertainty.

## Decision rule

No action should be labeled successful solely because it was announced, funded, constructed, launched, or reported as complete. Anavaratha should reserve “effective” for an action with a defined outcome supported by a credible comparison or repeated measurement, and reserve “durable” for an outcome that persists beyond the initial implementation window.
