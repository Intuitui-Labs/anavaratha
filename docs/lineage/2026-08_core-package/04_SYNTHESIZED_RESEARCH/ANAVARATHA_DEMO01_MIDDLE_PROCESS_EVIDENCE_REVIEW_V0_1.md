# Anavaratha Demonstrator 01: Middle-Process Evidence Review

## What can actually explain the apparent capacity–output difference?

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Scope:** Western Province composting demonstrator, supplemented by comparable Sri Lankan evidence.  
**Purpose:** Replace an apparent single “gap” with a structured account of observed, documented, inferred, and unknown processes.

## Executive conclusion

The forage produced an important correction. The strongest available sources do not support one universal explanation for the numerical difference between reported running capacity and reported finished compost output. They reveal several distinct measurement layers:

1. a 2025 Western Province study reports collected-organic-waste conversion of **1%–15%**;
2. our archived station table contains design capacity, running capacity, actual production, sales, area, employees, and operating days;
3. the ratio of reported production to reported running capacity is **6.57%**, but this is our derived comparison and is not automatically the study’s collected-organic-waste conversion;
4. a 2014 Bandarawela case provides a direct historical mass-balance example with 17.4 t/day incoming waste, approximately 80% diverted for composting, and only 40% feedstock mass recovered as compost, with most dry matter ending in dumpsites;
5. a 2017 Kirindiwela experiment shows that aeration, turning, moisture, process time, and feedstock preparation materially affect transformation, but it used pre-weighted and pre-sorted feedstock under experimental conditions;
6. a 2016 multi-site questionnaire and field survey documents missing source separation and process monitoring;
7. a 2026 semi-urban study models scenarios and explicitly acknowledges data limitations rather than providing a national measured balance.

These sources identify plausible mechanisms, but they cannot be added together as if they describe one common national or provincial denominator.

## 1. The four layers of evidence

| Layer | Evidence | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Published current-ish multi-station study | Manipura et al. 2025, 22 Western stations, 2022 data | Reported conversion range, process time, constraint prevalence, KPI design |
| Archived station summary table | 22 rows with capacity, production, sales, staffing, area | Numerical relationships among reported fields |
| Direct bounded mass balance | Bandarawela 2014 | One site’s input, diversion, mass reduction, leachate, compost recovery and dumping pathway |
| Controlled process experiment | Kirindiwela 2017 | Effects of aeration and process management on pre-sorted feedstock |
| Broad survey evidence | Madusanka et al. 2016/2017 | Recurrent missing separation and monitoring conditions |
| Scenario modelling | Fernando & Aramaki 2026 | Indicative climate implications under specified scenarios, not measured national operation |

## 2. The Western Province fields we actually possess

The available 22-station table contains the following fields:

- reported design capacity;
- reported running capacity;
- facility area;
- reported actual compost production;
- reported employees;
- reported compost sales;
- reported operating days, represented in the derived table as 25 days/month;
- field relationships calculated from those values.

It does **not** provide, for each station in the archived table:

- accepted organic input;
- total collected organic input;
- rejected loads;
- contamination mass;
- transfer to another plant or disposal site;
- batch stock;
- opening or closing inventory;
- moisture or dry-matter basis;
- process-stage losses;
- machine uptime or downtime;
- maintenance events;
- labour-hours or attendance;
- final residual quantity;
- residual destination;
- current operating status.

The supplementary questionnaire shows that these fields were part of the intended survey design, but their presence in the questionnaire does not prove that complete station-level observations were collected or supplied in the summary dataset.[1] [2]

## 3. What the 2,234.3 t/month number is—and is not

The number is calculated as:

\[
2,391.3\;t/month\;reported\;running\;capacity
-
157.0\;t/month\;reported\;actual\;compost\;output
=
2,234.3\;t/month
\]

It is a **field difference**, not a flow balance. It cannot be assigned to a specific process without data.

It is not currently established as:

- incoming material;
- accepted feedstock;
- rejected feedstock;
- unprocessed material;
- maturing compost;
- moisture loss;
- decomposition gas;
- material diverted elsewhere;
- residuals sent to disposal;
- measurement error;
- or pollution.

The number is still useful as a signal that the reported capacity and output fields are not self-explanatory. It tells us where to investigate, not what happened there.

## 4. The published 1%–15% conversion finding

Manipura et al. state that collected-organic-waste conversion into compost varied between 1% and 15% across the 22 stations.[3] This is a different denominator from our derived production/running-capacity ratio unless the study defines reported running capacity as collected organic input for the relevant observations.

The supplementary KPI document states that a 20% conversion factor was assumed as a current benchmark for all facilities, while acknowledging that the true factor may vary by station.[2] This confirms that “conversion” is an explicit analytical concern, but it also shows that the proposed benchmark is not an observed station-level balance.

The responsible reporting distinction is therefore:

| Figure | Denominator | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1%–15% | Collected organic waste, according to published abstract | Published study finding; detailed station denominators not in our archived table |
| 6.57% | Reported actual production / reported running capacity | Our derived field ratio; not automatically a conversion rate |
| 20% | Assumed conversion factor in proposed KPI framework | Benchmark/assumption; not an observed result |

## 5. What the additional Sri Lankan cases reveal

### 5.1 Bandarawela: a real bounded mass balance

Karunarathna et al. provide the closest located example of the missing middle being measured. The accessible record reports average incoming waste of 17.4 t/day, approximately 80% diverted for composting, 28% weight reduction and 34% volume reduction on a dry basis, and only 40% of feedstock mass recovered as compost. It also reports most dry matter ending in dumpsites and 0.67 tonnes of leachate, with evaporation accounting for a large part of water loss.[4]

This case demonstrates that the middle can contain several simultaneous transformations:

\[
Incoming\ waste
\rightarrow diverted\ feedstock
\rightarrow biological\ and\ moisture\ transformation
\rightarrow compost\ product
\rightarrow dry\ matter\ residuals\ and\ dumpsite\ pathway
\]

It does not provide a valid conversion factor for the Western Province stations. Its value is methodological: it shows the type of data required.

### 5.2 Kirindiwela: controlled process mechanism

De Silva and Yatawara used pre-weighted and pre-sorted market kitchen/food waste at the Kirindiwela sanitary-landfill premises in Gampaha. They monitored volatile solids and total nitrogen weekly, temperature daily, and compost quality against SLS 1246:2003. Aeration arrangement affected lead time and labour cost, while potassium and phosphorus were below SLS specifications.[5]

This demonstrates that process management can alter time, labour, heat, and product quality. It does not tell us what happened in the municipal stations receiving mixed or inconsistently separated waste.

### 5.3 The 2016 questionnaire and onsite survey

Madusanka et al. report no source separation at origin and no monitoring of temperature, moisture, stability, or maturity as recurrent problems. The proposed remedy was to separate at collection, monitor process parameters, and improve marketing.[6]

This helps explain why the middle may be difficult, but the survey is not a 2022 station-level mass balance.

### 5.4 2026 scenario study

Fernando and Aramaki model current and expanded composting scenarios in a semi-urban Sri Lankan context using IPCC Tier 1 GHG methods. The study reports emissions declining from 163.10 to 99.31 t CO2-eq/month across scenario conditions and explicitly notes data limitations and operational challenges.[7]

This shows how a defined boundary can support scenario analysis. It should not be used as evidence that the scenario capacity exists in practice or as a measured explanation of the Western Province field difference.

## 6. The best current decomposition of the unknown middle

The existing evidence permits a hierarchy rather than a false allocation.

### Established or directly supported mechanisms

- The 2025 study reports low collected-organic-waste conversion of 1%–15%.[3]
- The study reports 2.5–4.5 months for process completion.[3]
- The study reports no standard operating procedure and poor data management.[3]
- The study reports excess leachate and odour at 59% of stations.[3]
- The WMA and earlier surveys document contamination, weak separation, process monitoring deficits, space pressure, maintenance, and transfer problems.
- The Bandarawela case directly shows that composting can produce substantial dry-matter residuals and leachate rather than converting all feedstock to saleable compost.[4]

### Plausible but not allocated to the 2,234.3 t/month field difference

- rejected input;
- material still in active composting or maturation;
- moisture and dry-matter changes;
- machine downtime;
- labour limits;
- transfers outside the station boundary;
- residual disposal;
- informal recovery;
- stock carried across periods;
- measurement or period mismatch.

### Unknown in the available station table

- the actual input denominator per station;
- the quantity of rejects and residuals;
- the stock movement;
- the moisture basis;
- the time alignment of capacity, production, and sales;
- the degree to which running capacity means design availability, process feed, or actual daily intake;
- whether the station table was self-reported, independently weighed, or reconciled to records.

## 7. The exact next evidence request

To resolve the middle at one station, request one matched month or three matched months containing:

| Required field | Why it resolves the gap |
|---|---|
| Weighed incoming loads | Defines the input denominator |
| Accepted/rejected loads | Separates feedstock from contamination |
| Transfer/diversion records | Captures material outside the station boundary |
| Batch IDs and dates | Connects input to delayed output |
| Opening/closing stock | Separates production from inventory movement |
| Moisture/dry matter | Prevents wet/dry mass confusion |
| Operating days and downtime | Tests whether running capacity reflects actual availability |
| Labour-hours | Tests effective labour rather than headcount |
| Finished compost output | Defines product boundary |
| Reject/residual mass | Captures non-product material |
| Residual destination | Tests hidden disposal and burden transfer |
| Sales/use records | Tests product realization and stock drawdown |

Only after these are collected can the physical balance be written as:

\[
Accepted\ input + opening\ stock + external\ transfers
=
finished\ product + rejects + residuals + closing\ stock + measured\ process\ losses + error
\]

## 8. Revised interpretation for Anavaratha

The project should not claim to have discovered where the apparent gap went. It has discovered something more useful:

> **The available capacity and output fields are not sufficient to identify the middle process. The published study, its supplementary KPI design, and older Sri Lankan mass-balance and process studies show exactly which missing measurements are needed to resolve it.**

This is not a failure of the project. It is the correct forensic result. Anavaratha has moved from a potentially misleading performance ratio to a precise measurement programme.
