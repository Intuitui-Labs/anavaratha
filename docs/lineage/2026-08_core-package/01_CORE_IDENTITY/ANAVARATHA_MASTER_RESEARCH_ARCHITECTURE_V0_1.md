# Anavaratha: Master Research Architecture

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 25 August 2026  
**Geography:** Sri Lanka  
**Author:** Manus AI

## 1. Project identity

Anavaratha is a research, mapping, and practical-intelligence project about what happens to materials after and around human use. It examines how materials circulate, become unnecessary, remain useful, degrade, become residual, move through institutions and infrastructures, burden people and ecosystems, and sometimes return to productive or regenerative relations.

The project is organized into three domains:

1. **Freedom from Waste** — reducing unmanaged residual burdens and exposure.
2. **Material Afterlives** — preserving and circulating latent utility after first use.
3. **Ecological Flourishing** — understanding and supporting the regeneration and capability of living systems.

The three domains share a material-cycle grammar but retain separate research questions, evidence requirements, and ethical standards.

## 2. The central seam

The project’s central seam is the relation among **material flow, quality, capacity, information, power, place, time, and regeneration**.

A material does not become socially insignificant simply because its first user no longer needs it. An institutional system does not become capable simply because a facility exists. An ecosystem does not flourish simply because a restoration activity occurs. In each case, Anavaratha asks what is happening between formal classification and lived or material reality.

> **What is no longer useful here? What could be useful elsewhere? What is becoming residual? Who or what receives it? What information, capability, or power determines the transition?**

## 3. Ontology

Anavaratha treats each domain as an open, nested system containing:

- materials and energy;
- people, workers, households, institutions, and ecosystems;
- stocks and flows;
- transformations and exchanges;
- infrastructures and technologies;
- rules, markets, rights, and responsibilities;
- information and uncertainty;
- power and burden distribution;
- time, seasonality, memory, and persistence.

The same object can occupy different analytical states at different times. A chair may be active use, latent utility, repairable surplus, salvageable material, or residual waste. A compost plant may be a designed asset, a functioning process, an underused facility, a temporary storage point, or a hidden residual pathway. A wetland may be a receiver of burden, a transport system, a habitat, a restoration target, or a living source of ecological capability.

## 4. Three-domain architecture

### Domain I — Freedom from Waste

This domain studies involuntary waste burdens: exposure, accumulation, leakage, open dumping, open burning, unsafe work, odour, leachate, forced proximity, uncertain responsibility, and repeated system failure.

Its core method is the **residuals constitution**: no treatment, recovery, diversion, or compliance claim is accepted without asking what happens to rejects, leachate, ash, emissions, unsold products, stock, and other residuals.

Its central measure is not tonnes diverted alone. It is the combination of material containment, burden reduction, receiver condition, accountability, and persistence.

### Domain II — Material Afterlives

This domain studies objects and materials after their first use but before or beyond waste. It asks how repair, exchange, resale, donation, sharing, reuse, remanufacture, salvage, component recovery, and material recycling preserve or transform value.

Its core method is the **latent-utility ledger**, which records condition, location, ownership, repairability, compatibility, information, demand, trust, transaction cost, safety, labour, transport, price, duration of second use, and eventual residuals.

Its central measure is not circulation volume alone. It is the duration and quality of useful service preserved without creating hidden social or ecological burdens.

### Domain III — Ecological Flourishing

This domain studies the ability of ecosystems and more-than-human communities to regenerate, adapt, maintain function, and create future possibilities. It includes forests, wetlands, waterways, soils, habitats, ecological corridors, trees, biodiversity, restoration, and stewardship.

Its core method is the **ecological capability ledger**, which records ecological condition, function, connectivity, disturbance, material pressures, regeneration, protection, care, social access, and persistence.

Its central measure is not project activity alone. It is the sustained condition and capability of living systems.

## 5. Thermodynamic and aligned frames

Anavaratha uses thermodynamics as a discipline of boundaries, conservation, quality, irreversibility, useful potential, and unavoidable throughput. It does not turn social values into physical variables.

The project distinguishes:

| Concept | Anavaratha use |
|---|---|
| **Mass conservation** | Literal foundation for bounded material balances |
| **Energy and exergy** | Bounded comparison of process and material quality where data permit |
| **Entropy production** | Literal calculation only for defined systems with sufficient physical data |
| **Material dissipation** | Mixing, contamination, dispersion, degradation, burial, burning, leakage, and loss of recoverability |
| **Organizational dissipation** | Loss of effective capacity through missing data, maintenance failure, fragmented authority, and project discontinuity |
| **Negentropy** | Cautious metaphor for maintained differentiation, memory, feedback, repair, and organization |
| **Ecological metabolism** | Flows among settlements, economies, infrastructures, and living systems |
| **Political ecology** | Power and uneven distribution of access, extraction, risk, and burdens |
| **Capability approach** | Substantive ability to act, maintain, exchange, care, and flourish |
| **Environmental justice** | Distribution, recognition, participation, protection, remedy, and repair |

The project must not call all waste entropy, all social disorder entropy, or all good organization negentropy. Each claim must identify its boundary, unit, mechanism, and evidence status.

## 6. Research descent: from eagle view to local validation

Anavaratha follows a controlled descent. It does not start with an anecdote, a facility, or a named locality and then build a theory around it.

```text
1. Ontology and philosophical orientation
2. Three-domain material-cycle model
3. Thermodynamic, ecological, institutional, and justice frames
4. Sri Lankan national material and institutional architecture
5. Domain-specific streams, objects, ecosystems, and infrastructures
6. Capacity, constraints, actors, power, time, and spatial networks
7. Evidence audit and uncertainty structure
8. Integrated system typology
9. Controlled case selection by prior hypothesis
10. Documentary and remote verification
11. Field validation
12. Bounded intervention and persistence test
```

A descent gate is satisfied only when the higher layer has a clear definition, source base, boundary, uncertainty statement, and reason for descending. A locality is not selected because it is visually dramatic or personally remembered. It is selected because it tests a prior system type and can answer a falsifiable question.

## 7. Evidence architecture

All factual claims must be classified by source status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Observed/measured** | Directly measured under a stated method and period |
| **Audited** | Reported through an accountable audit or official verification process |
| **Reported** | Stated by a source but not independently verified by the project |
| **Estimated/modelled** | Derived through a stated estimation or model |
| **Planned/target** | Intended future activity or capacity |
| **Historical** | Correctly dated past condition |
| **Disputed** | Conflicting credible accounts exist |
| **Inaccessible** | A source or database is claimed or known to exist but cannot be inspected |
| **Unknown** | The project has insufficient evidence to classify the condition |

Every record should preserve source, URL or archive, publication date, data period, geographic boundary, unit, definition, page/table/figure where possible, evidence grade, currentness, uncertainty, and intended use.

## 8. Core ledgers

The project should not create a single national score. It should maintain connected but separate ledgers.

| Ledger | Main contents |
|---|---|
| **Material ledger** | Inputs, outputs, stocks, quality, transformations, residuals, and unknowns |
| **Capacity ledger** | Assets, usable capacity, throughput, downtime, labour, maintenance, finance, market, and ecological safeguards |
| **Afterlife ledger** | Holder, condition, repair, exchange, second use, duration, and final residual |
| **Ecological ledger** | Condition, function, pressures, regeneration, care, and persistence |
| **Burden ledger** | Exposure, nuisance, risk, receiving communities, workers, ecosystems, and remedy |
| **Information ledger** | Missingness, data quality, identifiers, feedback, record continuity, and accessibility |
| **Power ledger** | Decision rights, voice, visibility, responsibility, land, finance, and burden-transfer relations |
| **Time ledger** | Commissioning, operation, breakdown, reuse, closure, restoration, recurrence, and persistence |

The ledgers are connected through explicit relationships, not through assumed equivalence.

## 9. GIS role

GIS is an evidence and relation system, not a machine that discovers causality automatically. It should map:

- population and activity denominators;
- facilities and their evidence status;
- collection and transport pathways;
- temporary and hidden disposal leads;
- stocks and changes where spatially observable;
- roads, hydrology, catchments, wetlands, forests, protected areas, and land cover;
- reuse, repair, exchange, or material-bank nodes where reliable data become available;
- ecological condition and restoration sites;
- burden receivers and monitoring points.

Proximity is not contamination. A mapped point is not capacity. An OSM tag is not a verified facility. A cluster is not an illegal-dumping cluster. Every layer must include source, date, geometry, completeness, join key, uncertainty, and intended use.

## 10. Capacity and capability

Anavaratha distinguishes:

1. asset existence;
2. installed or designed capacity;
3. available capacity;
4. accepted-input capacity;
5. actual throughput;
6. quality-adjusted output;
7. residual containment;
8. recurrent and institutional capability;
9. ecological safety;
10. persistence after intervention or project support.

The same logic applies beyond waste. A repair centre may exist but lack parts. A reuse exchange may have listings but no trusted matching. A restoration project may plant trees but lack watering, protection, survival, or ecological connectivity. Capacity is therefore a conditional capability, not an object count.

## 11. Practical leverage

Anavaratha’s role is capacity augmentation, not capacity substitution. It can:

- make hidden flows and afterlives visible;
- preserve information and identifiers;
- identify the smallest binding constraint;
- connect actors who hold different parts of a cycle;
- improve measurement without creating an unmaintainable bureaucracy;
- protect workers and affected communities from erasure;
- strengthen repair, reuse, residual tracking, and ecological care;
- test modest interventions and check persistence.

The common sequence is:

> **make visible → define the boundary → identify the capability gap → trace the burden or value → locate leverage → test modestly → verify persistence.**

## 12. First project portfolio

The recommended portfolio is:

### Portfolio A — Freedom from Waste

Continue the Sri Lankan facility-capacity, hidden-disposal, material-pathway, law–capacity–reality, and GIS work. The first bounded demonstrator is composting, but later streams may include plastics, e-waste, wastewater, hazardous waste, construction waste, and residuals.

### Portfolio B — Material Afterlives

Begin desk research on used and surplus goods, repair economies, reuse channels, institutional asset disposal, construction-material reuse, electronics, textiles, furniture, tools, books, and community exchange. Start with one material family where sources and potential matching infrastructure are visible.

### Portfolio C — Ecological Flourishing

Develop a separate ecological evidence architecture covering forests, wetlands, waterways, soil, restoration, trees, biodiversity, and ecological stewardship. Connect it to the other portfolios only through explicit material or burden pathways.

## 13. Success criteria

Anavaratha does not need to solve all of Sri Lanka’s material problems to be successful. A strong first cycle should:

1. make one hidden material system legible;
2. explain one apparent capacity or circulation gap without inventing the middle;
3. show how value or burden moves across boundaries;
4. identify who has capability and who receives the consequences;
5. propose one modest, context-compatible leverage point;
6. test whether the change persists;
7. preserve enough evidence for another person to reproduce or challenge the analysis.

## 14. Final formulation

> **Anavaratha is a Sri Lanka–focused study of material afterlives, waste burdens, and ecological flourishing. It traces how matter, energy, value, information, institutions, people, and ecosystems preserve or lose capability through cycles of use, disuse, circulation, transformation, residuality, and regeneration.**
