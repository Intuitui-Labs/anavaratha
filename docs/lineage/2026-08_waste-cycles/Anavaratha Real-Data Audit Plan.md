# Anavaratha Real-Data Audit Plan

## Purpose

The first real-data phase will remain at the eagle-view level. It will not select a local authority or create a current national mass balance from incompatible estimates. It will establish which real datasets exist, what they actually measure, how far back they go, and what national or broad spatial patterns can be responsibly analyzed.

## National analysis questions

| Question | Data required | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| What population and activity pressures shape waste demand? | Population, households, urbanization, establishments, tourism, economic activity, land use | Pressure indicators and denominators |
| What waste quantities are reported, by stream and period? | Generation, collection, recovery, disposal, burning, leakage, composition | Harmonized source table with definitions |
| What management capacity is visible? | Local authorities, vehicles, staff, facilities, budgets, landfill and recycling nodes | Capacity inventory with evidence grade |
| Where do flows and burdens appear to move? | Administrative geography, facility locations, roads, waterways, land cover, drainage, reports | Broad spatial proxy map |
| What has changed over time? | Repeated annual, seasonal, project, policy, population, and facility data | Trend and persistence analysis |
| Which claims are compatible? | Metadata, units, reference periods, boundaries, methods | Definition and contradiction register |
| What remains unknown? | Missingness, non-reporting, incompatible categories, informal and uncollected flows | Uncertainty and validation priorities |

## Source hierarchy

Use official Sri Lankan data first for national and administrative variables, including the Department of Census and Statistics, data.gov.lk, the Central Environmental Authority, the Environment Ministry, provincial or local-government sources, and official plans. Use peer-reviewed studies and reputable development-agency datasets to fill methodological or historical gaps. Use international datasets for comparability, land cover, climate, and standardized methodological context. Every source retains its own definition and reference period.

## Real-data rule

No simulated or randomly generated data will be used. If a needed variable cannot be found, it will be labeled unknown or estimated only when a source provides an explicit estimate with method and uncertainty. A missing national dataset is itself a finding about informational capacity.

## First analytical products

The first products should be a source inventory, metadata audit, data dictionary, harmonized observations table, missingness profile, definition and contradiction register, broad national trend summaries where compatible, and a visual atlas of available spatial proxies. The outputs should distinguish measured data, modeled estimates, policy targets, and desk inference.

## Join rules

Datasets may be joined only when their geographic boundaries, time periods, units, and definitions are compatible. If a join requires an assumption, the assumption must be documented and the result labeled modeled or inferred. Facility maps, population data, and waste quantities should not be combined into a single score until the denominators and reference periods are aligned.

## National descent gate

The project may descend from national analysis to a broad system typology only after it has identified: which waste streams have usable data; which population/activity denominators are available; how waste quantities are defined; what capacity variables are observable; which broad spatial patterns are defensible; and which gaps require future targeted validation.
