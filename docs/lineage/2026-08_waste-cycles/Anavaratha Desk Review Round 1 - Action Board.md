# Anavaratha Desk Review Round 1: Action Board

## Objective

Move from a broad idea about waste cycles to a disciplined, evidence-based understanding of how Sri Lankan waste systems operate, fail, adapt, and transfer burdens across places and time.

## Workstream 1: Source extraction

Read the National Action Plan on Plastic Waste Management, the IWMI 20-city report, the Western Province database evaluation, the CEA policy documents, and selected local-authority studies. Extract page-level claims into the coded-claims matrix. Do not extract only headline numbers; record definitions, reference periods, boundaries, actors, mechanisms, and assumptions.

## Workstream 2: System reconstruction

For each studied place, reconstruct the pathway:

> **inflow → generator/activity → waste generation → separation or mixing → collection → transport → recovery or treatment → controlled/uncontrolled disposal → accumulation, leakage, or downstream destination.**

Mark each link as documented, partially documented, estimated, contested, or unknown.

## Workstream 3: Capacity and constraint analysis

For every major failure, identify the first binding constraint. Code whether it is physical, operational, financial, human, technical, institutional, political, social, market, informational, spatial, or temporal. Then record whether the proposed intervention addresses that binding constraint or only treats a visible symptom.

## Workstream 4: Proportional comparison

When compatible data exist, calculate waste and service measures relative to the correct population or activity denominator. Pair tonnes with per-person, per-household, per-establishment, per-route, per-vehicle, per-area, or per-operating-day measures as appropriate. Keep the original absolute value visible.

## Workstream 5: Time and persistence

Separate historical structure from current conditions. Identify whether an intervention was measured before, during, or after support. Record project duration, funding period, maintenance period, policy changes, seasonal context, and whether outcomes persisted.

## Workstream 6: Spatial transfer

Map not only where waste originates but also where it goes. Track transfer to other local authorities, landfills, waterways, informal workers, recycling markets, or future accumulation. A place should not be called successful if it reduces visible burden by exporting it without accounting for the receiving location.

## Workstream 7: Contradiction register

For every disagreement between sources, record the competing values or claims, periods, definitions, geographic scopes, methods, and potential reporting incentives. Classify the disagreement as a definitional conflict, temporal change, spatial difference, sampling difference, reporting gap, or unresolved contradiction.

## Workstream 8: Validation priority

Rank each unknown using two criteria: decision importance and validation feasibility. Prioritize unknowns that could materially change the choice of intervention and can be tested safely with a manageable future observation.

## Definition of completion

Desk Review Round 1 is complete when the project has a verified source library, coded claims, failure taxonomy, capacity-pressure model, proportional comparison rules, time-space comparison, contradiction register, provisional system profile, and ranked validation questions. It is not complete merely because many papers have been collected.

## Immediate decision gate

The project should not yet choose a final pilot or launch a public map. It should first complete the deeper extraction of the highest-priority sources and then decide whether the strongest next step is a pilot in Dompe/Gampaha, a comparison between Western and Eastern Province contexts, or a different case with better data access and institutional cooperation.
