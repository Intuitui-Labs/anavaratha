# Anavaratha Rigor Cycle: Desk to Field

## Purpose

The project should not move from idea directly to fieldwork. It should move through repeated cycles in which each concept becomes a claim, each claim is connected to evidence, and each evidence gap becomes a precise future observation.

## The cycle

| Stage | Question | Output | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Conceptualize | What does “Freedom from Waste” or “Freedom to Flourish” mean in this system? | Definition and boundary | Terms are operationally defined |
| 2. Hypothesize | What mechanism is expected to connect conditions and outcomes? | Causal-hypothesis log | Each claim includes an alternative explanation |
| 3. Inventory | What secondary data already exists? | Dataset register | Sources are catalogued with metadata |
| 4. Harmonize | Can the sources be compared? | Definition and compatibility matrix | Units, dates, boundaries, and methods are explicit |
| 5. Map | Where are pressures, flows, assets, and gaps? | Provisional atlas | Every layer carries evidence status |
| 6. Reason | What patterns are plausible, and what might be misleading? | Analytical memo | Competing explanations are written down |
| 7. Prioritize | Which unknowns matter most for decisions? | Field-priority list | Each future observation has a decision purpose |
| 8. Design field test | How can the claim be measured safely and repeatably? | Sampling and validation protocol | Method, sample, safety, and consent are approved |
| 9. Validate | Does observation support, qualify, or contradict the desk hypothesis? | Validation report | Discrepancies and uncertainty are documented |
| 10. Revise | What should change in the model, indicator, or intervention? | Versioned model and next-cycle plan | Revision is traceable to evidence |

## Hypothesis template

Use this format for every important claim:

> **Claim:** [What is believed to be happening.]  
> **Mechanism:** [Why it would happen.]  
> **Expected evidence:** [What should be observable in records, maps, or field measurements.]  
> **Alternative explanation:** [What else could produce the same pattern.]  
> **Decision relevance:** [What decision would change if the claim is true.]  
> **Minimum validation:** [Smallest safe and feasible observation needed.]

## Example: waste direction

> **Claim:** Collection gaps increase uncontrolled disposal in selected settlement segments.  
> **Mechanism:** Missed or irregular service shifts waste toward self-disposal, burning, or informal dumping.  
> **Expected evidence:** Route schedules and missed-service records overlap spatially with repeat hotspot observations.  
> **Alternative explanation:** Hotspots may be caused mainly by commercial activity, construction waste, flooding, or land-access conditions.  
> **Decision relevance:** Improve route design, frequency, access, enforcement, or source separation.  
> **Minimum validation:** Compare a sample of route/service records with repeat hotspot observations across a defined period.

## Example: ecological direction

> **Claim:** Organic-waste recovery could support ecological restoration in selected sites.  
> **Mechanism:** Separated organic material becomes a usable soil amendment or compost input, reducing residual disposal and improving soil-related restoration capacity.  
> **Expected evidence:** Adequate uncontaminated feedstock, processing capacity, suitable recipient sites, and measurable soil or vegetation objectives.  
> **Alternative explanation:** Contamination, transport costs, weak demand, or poor maintenance may prevent ecological benefit.  
> **Decision relevance:** Design a controlled recovery-and-restoration pilot rather than merely expanding collection.  
> **Minimum validation:** Desk screening of feedstock quality and recipient-site suitability, followed by a small supervised field test with safety and environmental controls.

## Pre-field decision gates

Fieldwork should begin only when five conditions are met. The research question is specific. The expected evidence is defined. A secondary-data review has already been completed. The field observation can change a decision. The team has permission, safety procedures, and a method for consent and data protection.

If a proposed field activity cannot satisfy these conditions, it belongs in the desk-analysis backlog rather than the immediate field plan.

## Reporting categories

Every conclusion in an Anavaratha report should be assigned one category:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Established | Supported by direct, relevant, and sufficiently complete evidence |
| Supported | Multiple credible sources agree, but direct validation is limited |
| Plausible | Mechanistically reasonable but not yet adequately evidenced |
| Contested | Sources or observations disagree materially |
| Unknown | Evidence is insufficient to make a responsible inference |

The project should treat “unknown” as a useful finding. It identifies where better data or a better question is needed.
