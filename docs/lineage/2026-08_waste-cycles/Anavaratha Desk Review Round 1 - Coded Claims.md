## Reading rule

These are coded analytical claims, not final truths. Each claim is linked to a source, context, mechanism, limitation, and future validation need. Historical or source-specific estimates must not be presented as current national facts without rechecking their reference period and definitions.

## Cross-source synthesis

The most consistent pattern is not one isolated deficiency but a **linked failure chain**. Pressure from population and economic activity meets uneven local capacity. Policy models may fail to differentiate scale. Collection and separation may be unreliable. Recovery may be weakened by contamination or missing markets. Facilities may be insufficient or poorly operated. Burdens may move spatially to disposal sites, waterways, informal workers, or neighboring authorities. Weak data systems then make the chain difficult to see and difficult to govern.

The strongest working hypothesis is therefore:

> **Waste-system performance depends on the fit between pressure, usable capacity, institutional coordination, and pathway reliability across time and space.**

This hypothesis is more useful than saying simply that “people do not segregate” or “the country lacks technology.”

## Claims requiring special caution

The national plastic plan contains important historical baseline estimates and targets. Those values are valuable for reconstructing the policy knowledge base but should not be treated as current conditions without checking the plan’s definitions and period. The IWMI 20-city report and the 2024 Western Province database paper are high-priority sources for deeper extraction before using their detailed findings. Historical studies should be used primarily to identify mechanisms and changes over time, not to substitute for current measurement.
