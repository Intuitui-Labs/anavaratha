# Anavaratha Demonstrator 01: Forensic Gap Analysis

## What exactly is the apparent capacity–output difference?

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Subject:** 22 Western Province compost stations reported by Manipura et al. (2025), using January–December 2022 study data.  
**Purpose:** Correctly identify what is known, what is derived, and what remains unknown between reported capacity and compost output.

## Executive conclusion

The previous phrase “capacity–output gap” was too easy to misunderstand. The available data do **not** show that running capacity entered the facility and failed to emerge as compost. They show that the study reports three different fields:

- design capacity;
- running capacity;
- actual compost production.

The arithmetic difference between running capacity and actual production is real as a difference between reported fields. It is **not yet a material balance**.

For the 22 stations:

| Reported field | Aggregate value | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| Design capacity | 2,775.0 t/month | Nominal design field |
| Running capacity | 2,391.3 t/month | Reported running-capacity field |
| Actual compost production | 157.0 t/month | Reported finished-compost output field |
| Running minus production | 2,234.3 t/month | Numerical difference between two reported fields only |
| Design minus running | 383.7 t/month | Numerical difference between two reported capacity fields only |
| Accepted input | Not supplied in available station table | Unknown |
| Rejected input | Not supplied | Unknown |
| In-process/maturation stock | Not supplied | Unknown |
| Moisture/dry-matter adjustment | Not supplied | Unknown |
| Other destinations/diversions | Not supplied | Unknown |
| Rejects/residuals | Not supplied | Unknown |
| Measurement/period reconciliation | Not supplied | Unknown |

The correct question is therefore not “Where did the 2,234.3 tonnes go?” We do not know that it was 2,234.3 tonnes of material. The correct question is:

> **What definitions, inputs, stocks, process states, outputs, destinations, and measurement practices lie between the reported running-capacity field and the reported finished-compost field?**

## 1. Three different statements that must not be conflated

### Statement A: Published study finding

The published abstract reports that collected-organic-waste conversion into compost varied between **1% and 15%**, that the complete process took **2.5–4.5 months**, that no standard operating procedure was observed, that 59% of stations reported excess leachate and odour, and that poor data management hindered benchmarking.[1]

This is the study’s reported operational conversion finding. It is not the same as the ratio we derived from the archived station fields below unless the denominator is shown to be identical.

### Statement B: Our derived field ratio

Using the available station table, we calculated:

\[
Reported\ output\text{-}to\text{-}running\ field\ ratio =
\frac{reported\ actual\ compost\ production}{reported\ running\ capacity}
\times 100
\]

This aggregate ratio is **6.57%**. It is a relationship between two reported fields. It must not be called the study’s organic-waste conversion factor without verifying that running capacity equals collected organic input, which the available data do not establish.

### Statement C: The numerical difference

The aggregate difference is:

\[
2,391.3 - 157.0 = 2,234.3\ t/month
\]

This is a difference between reported fields. It is not automatically:

- unprocessed incoming waste;
- rejected feedstock;
- process loss;
- water loss;
- compost still maturing;
- dumped material;
- missing material;
- thermodynamic entropy;
- or ecological pollution.

## 2. What the supplementary material reveals

The study’s own questionnaire shows that the middle process is much richer than the summary table. It requests daily organic waste intake, segregated and mixed waste, the share directed to the plant, ward-level tonnage, equipment hours, operating days, feedstock quantities, process monitoring, cycle times, quality tests, failure factors, finances, worker safety, sales, market demand, and digital records.[2]

The KPI document separately identifies the data needed to explain performance: daily intake, monthly production, monthly sales, residuals directed to final disposal, mean time between failures, mean time to repair, leachate, odour, methane, buffer zones, legal status, OHS, contamination, temperature, turning, moisture, throughput, cycle time, downtime, labour productivity, electricity, fuel, water, quality, and cost.[3]

Most importantly, the KPI document states that a **20% conversion factor was assumed** for all compost facilities as a current benchmark, while acknowledging that actual conversion may vary by station and operating conditions.[3] This is an assumption or benchmark in the proposed KPI framework. It must not be used to reconstruct missing inputs or to overwrite the published observed range of 1%–15%.

## 3. The unknown middle process

The current evidence supports twelve competing explanations. None should be selected without additional records.

| Possibility | What would have to be true | Evidence currently available | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running capacity is nominal or administrative | Running capacity does not mean equivalent physical input | Running field exists; accepted input absent | Open hypothesis |
| Input was rejected before composting | Contaminated or unsuitable loads were excluded | Contamination is documented generally | Plausible documented process |
| Material remained in stock or maturation | Output period does not match input period | Process time is 2.5–4.5 months | Open hypothesis |
| Wet mass changed through decomposition/evaporation | Input and output bases differ | Full moisture/dry-matter data absent | Open hypothesis |
| Material went to another station or use | Station boundary excludes diversions | External route data absent | Open hypothesis |
| Downtime reduced actual processing | Reported running field overstates uptime | Study documents operational difficulty; raw uptime absent | Plausible documented process |
| Definitions differ | Capacity may mean feed, process, theoretical, or saleable output | Definitions not fully harmonized in available table | Open hypothesis |
| Measurement or aggregation error | Values are rounded, estimated, self-reported, or mismatched | Seven stations report running capacity above design; four report sales above production | Observed warning |
| Rejects/residuals left unrecorded | Low output coexists with disposal or leakage | Residual fields absent | Open hypothesis |
| Sales came from prior stock | Sales exceed current production | Four station records show sales above production | Observed boundary warning |
| The process was genuinely low-output | Input was received but little saleable compost was produced | Low output-to-running ratios are widespread | Open hypothesis |
| Informal recovery occurred outside formal output | Some material left through non-standard channels | Broader system context supports possibility; station evidence absent | Open hypothesis |

## 4. What can be quantified now

Only the following diagnostics are defensible from the archived 22-station table:

| Diagnostic | Value | Correct interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Sum of reported design capacity | 2,775.0 t/month | Sum of nominal fields |
| Sum of reported running capacity | 2,391.3 t/month | Sum of reported running fields |
| Sum of reported actual production | 157.0 t/month | Sum of reported output fields |
| Output/running field ratio | 6.57% | Descriptive relationship, not a process yield unless denominators are harmonized |
| Running/design field ratio | 86.17% | Descriptive relationship, not verified uptime |
| Running minus output | 2,234.3 t/month | Field difference only |
| Design minus running | 383.7 t/month | Field difference only |
| Stations with output below 10% of running field | 17 of 22 | Descriptive pattern in reported fields |
| Stations with sales above production | 4 of 22 | Boundary or stock warning |
| Stations with accepted input recorded in available table | 0 of 22 | Direct evidence of missing denominator |

The value **6.57%** should therefore be reported as “aggregate reported actual-production-to-reported-running-capacity ratio,” not “actual compost conversion,” unless the original station-level raw data show that the running-capacity field was collected organic input.

The published **1%–15%** conversion range should be retained as the study’s own reported conversion result, but its station-level denominator and full reconstruction are not available in the current archived table.[1]

## 5. The real gap

There are at least four distinct gaps, not one.

### 5.1 Definition gap

We do not yet know whether design capacity, running capacity, collected organic waste, accepted organic waste, processed feedstock, and finished compost use the same material basis, moisture basis, period, or facility boundary.

### 5.2 Observation gap

The summary table does not expose accepted input, rejected loads, stock, residuals, moisture, downtime, equipment condition, or external destinations.

### 5.3 Process gap

The transformation from feedstock to finished compost takes months and includes segregation, size reduction, formulation, pile construction, active composting, turning/aeration, moisture management, maturation, sieving, packing, and possibly storage. A monthly finished-output field cannot reveal which stage constrained or delayed material.

### 5.4 Accountability gap

Even if a material balance were constructed inside the plant, it would still need a downstream residual account. Rejects, leachate, stock, unsold compost, and contaminated material may move beyond the station boundary.

## 6. Corrected Anavaratha model

The model should be represented as a partial-observation graph:

```text
Reported capacity fields
        │
        │ definition and boundary unknown
        ▼
Possible accepted input ──?── rejected input
        │
        ▼
Process stages: preparation → active composting → maturation → sieving
        │             │              │              │
        │             │              │              ├── oversize/rejects ?
        │             │              ├── stock ?
        │             ├── moisture/volatile change ?
        │             └── downtime/maintenance ?
        ▼
Reported finished compost output ──?── sales/use/stock
        │
        ▼
Residuals, leachate, contamination, overflow, external transfer ?
```

Every question mark is an explicit evidence requirement, not a blank to be filled by intuition.

## 7. What data would identify the gap

A minimum forensic campaign requires one matched time period and one defined station boundary:

1. weigh every incoming load and record source and material type;
2. record rejected or diverted loads separately;
3. record daily operating status and machine downtime;
4. assign batch IDs and record batch start, turning, moisture, temperature, and maturation dates;
5. weigh finished compost at production, storage, and sale;
6. record opening and closing stock;
7. weigh and trace all rejects and residuals;
8. record external transfers and informal channels where ethically and practically possible;
9. record water, fuel, electricity, labour-hours, and maintenance events;
10. verify final destinations and environmental controls.

Only then can Anavaratha calculate a bounded material balance and decide whether the main issue is definition, input quality, process delay, downtime, stock, diversion, residual disposal, measurement error, or genuinely low conversion.

## 8. Revised conclusion

The evidence does **not** currently tell us exactly where 2,234.3 t/month went, because it does not establish that 2,234.3 t/month of material entered the station or that running capacity is an input denominator.

What the evidence does establish is narrower and stronger:

> **The 22-station dataset contains a large mismatch between reported capacity fields and reported finished-output fields, while the study’s own questionnaire identifies many unreported middle-process variables needed to explain it. The published 1%–15% collected-organic-waste conversion finding is separate from our derived 6.57% output-to-running-capacity field ratio. The immediate research problem is therefore denominator and boundary reconstruction, not allocation of an assumed waste quantity.**

This correction makes Demonstrator 01 more useful. It turns an apparent answer into a precise forensic question.
