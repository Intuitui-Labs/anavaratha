# Anavaratha Evidence-Coding Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix is the first-round sense-making instrument for reading existing research, books, policies, project reports, datasets, and documented failures. The goal is to compare evidence without flattening differences between places, populations, scales, time periods, or institutional capacities.

## Coding dimensions

| Dimension | Questions to ask | Suggested coding |
|---|---|---|
| Source | What kind of knowledge is this? | Policy, law, plan, academic study, project report, dataset, media, interview, book |
| Location | Where was the evidence produced? | Province, district, local authority, facility, route, neighborhood, national |
| Spatial scale | What geographic scale does it describe? | Site, route, settlement, local authority, district, province, country |
| Population | Who is represented or affected? | Households, businesses, institutions, workers, informal actors, officials, residents |
| Population size | What population or activity denominator is used? | People, households, establishments, tonnes, vehicles, area, unknown |
| Time | What period does it represent? | Year, season, month, event, project duration, long-term trend |
| Waste stream | What material is considered? | Mixed MSW, organics, plastics, recyclables, wastewater, e-waste, C&D, hazardous |
| System boundary | What is included and excluded? | Generation, collection, recovery, treatment, disposal, leakage, ecological impact |
| Capacity | What can the system actually do? | Land, vehicles, staff, finance, technical, data, governance, market, social |
| Constraint | What limits performance? | Land, funding, policy, politics, behavior, technology, labor, market, geography |
| Actor | Who has power, responsibility, exposure, or knowledge? | Authority, contractor, household, business, worker, recycler, community |
| Intervention | What was proposed or implemented? | Separation, collection, composting, recycling, landfill, enforcement, education, data system |
| Failure mechanism | How did the system fail or underperform? | Design mismatch, coordination, capacity, operations, regulation, market, data, spatial, temporal |
| Outcome | What happened? | Improved, unchanged, displaced, failed, uncertain, unintended effect |
| Evidence strength | How reliable is the claim for our use? | A primary/direct, B credible secondary, C partial/proxy, D unverified |
| Transferability | Can it inform another place? | High, conditional, low, unknown |

## Required source summary

For each source, write a paragraph answering: **What did the source study? What did it observe? What failure or success mechanism did it identify? Who was affected? What capacity and constraint conditions were present? What can Anavaratha responsibly learn from it?**

## Proportional comparisons

Never compare two local authorities only by total tonnes or total budget. Pair absolute values with proportional measures whenever possible:

| Absolute measure | Proportional or normalized companion |
|---|---|
| Tonnes collected per day | Tonnes per person, household, establishment, or served area |
| Number of vehicles | Vehicles per route, population, or tonnes collected |
| Staff count | Staff per tonne, route, facility, or population served |
| Disposal-site capacity | Remaining capacity relative to inflow and expected horizon |
| Budget | Waste-management cost per person, tonne, or service unit |
| Hotspot count | Hotspots per square kilometre, road length, population, or drainage area |
| Recovery quantity | Recovery rate relative to generation and contamination |
| Project duration | Outcome persistence after project support ends |

Normalization is not automatically fairness. A per-capita value can hide dense commercial activity, tourism, seasonal populations, or unequal service access. Every normalized indicator must retain its denominator and context.

## Time structure

Read sources in at least four time views: **snapshot** (one measurement period), **seasonal** (wet/dry or peak/off-peak), **project cycle** (before, during, and after an intervention), and **structural trend** (multi-year change). A project that appears successful during its funded period may fail after maintenance, staff, or financing ends. A facility that appears adequate in one year may become constrained as population and inflows change.

## Spatial structure

Use nested spatial levels: national, province, district, local authority, settlement, route, facility, hotspot, and receptor. Track both the location where waste is generated and the location where burden is transferred. A local area may improve its visible cleanliness by exporting residuals or risks to a neighboring area. Anavaratha should record origin, pathway, destination, and receiving burden.

## Perspective structure

Every source should be read from at least four perspectives:

1. **Generator perspective:** cost, convenience, information, trust, and available choices.
2. **Operator perspective:** route, equipment, staffing, safety, maintenance, and records.
3. **Authority perspective:** mandate, finance, land, coordination, politics, and enforcement.
4. **Affected-community and worker perspective:** exposure, dignity, livelihood, health, voice, and unequal burden.

Add the ecological perspective where possible: drainage, waterways, soils, vegetation, animals, and long-term accumulation.

## Example coded insight

A statement such as “source separation failed” is incomplete. The matrix should ask whether bins and collection routes existed, whether separated fractions were kept separate, whether markets or facilities accepted the materials, whether households trusted the service, whether workers were incentivized, whether the intervention was maintained after the pilot, and whether waste was shifted to another pathway. The failure code should capture the mechanism rather than the slogan.

## Output of the first round

The first evidence round should produce a coded source library, a failure-mechanism taxonomy, a cross-location comparison table, a capacity-constraint map, a time-and-space matrix, a list of unresolved contradictions, and a shortlist of desk hypotheses for future validation.
