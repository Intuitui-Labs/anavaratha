# Anavaratha GIS-to-Local Descent Gate v0.1

## Principle

A place must not become a case study merely because it appears in an OSM cluster, lies near water or forest, or resembles an anecdote. A local site enters the study only as a controlled test of a previously defined national system type and hypothesis.

## Current national-level conclusion

The broad GIS layer is sufficiently developed to define system types and data gaps, but not yet to rank localities by risk or to assert illegal disposal. Population denominators are complete at district scale. Mapped OSM features are exploratory and uneven. Official NSDI utility data is useful but domain-specific. A current, national, comparable solid-waste facility and operational-capacity inventory remains the most important missing layer.

## Descent gates

| Gate | Question | Minimum evidence required | Current state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Denominator gate | Is there a defensible population/activity denominator? | DCS population, households, housing, and where possible activity/tourism or seasonal population data | Passed at district scale; activity seasonality incomplete |
| 2. Stream gate | Is the material stream defined? | Organic, plastic, residual, e-waste, hazardous, wastewater, or mixed stream definition and period | Partially passed; national stream framing exists, spatial stream data is incomplete |
| 3. Facility gate | Is the alleged facility or disposal point verified? | Official registry, council record, permit/licence, inspection, dated imagery, or multiple independent sources | Not passed nationally; OSM alone is insufficient |
| 4. Capacity gate | Can inflow, throughput, and usable capacity be compared? | Measured or source-documented design/usable capacity, throughput, fleet/staff/maintenance information | Not passed |
| 5. Pathway gate | Can transport and receiving pathways be represented? | Roads, routes, hydrology/catchment, terrain, seasonality, and site access layers | Partially passed; authoritative roads/hydrology and route operations still needed |
| 6. Legal-reality gate | Can law on paper be compared with material practice? | Legal status, licence, inspection, complaint, enforcement, remedy, monitoring, and persistence records | Initial framework passed; site-linked current records incomplete |
| 7. Receiving-environment gate | Is ecological exposure represented by authoritative layers? | Protected areas, forests/reserves, wetlands, waterways, and preferably hydrological linkage | Not passed; current OSM layer is screening-only |
| 8. Typology gate | Does the locality instantiate a national system type? | Explicit typology rule and comparable evidence across more than one candidate | Not yet passed |
| 9. Falsifiability gate | What result would disconfirm the hypothesis? | Pre-written alternative explanations and field/desk tests | Must be written before selection |
| 10. Field gate | Is fieldwork necessary and safe? | Clear unresolved question, access plan, ethics/OHS plan, source triangulation, and data-capture protocol | Deferred |

## Candidate system types, not candidate places

The next descent should classify national systems before naming localities. Plausible types include dense metropolitan and peri-urban systems; facility-linked regional systems; rural dispersed systems with long transport distances; coastal and drainage-linked systems; tourism- or event-intensive systems; and mixed systems where formal collection coexists with informal recovery and residual leakage.

A locality may later be selected if it is a strong, evidence-supported instance of one type and if it helps distinguish competing explanations. For example, a tourism-intensive site may test whether seasonal population and rainy-season access create a capacity mismatch; a peri-urban site may test whether population concentration and land scarcity shift burdens to receiving environments; and a remote site may test whether transport distance and weak enforcement create persistence of disposal practices.

## Controlled verification protocol for a future site

For any future disposal or facility lead, including the deferred Moonplains/Nuwara Eliya lead, record the exact coordinates or boundary, source and date, responsible authority, facility category, legal and permit status, operating status, inflow, throughput, residual destination, source separation, vehicle and route conditions, staff and maintenance, leachate or drainage, relation to streams/wetlands/forest/protected areas, complaints, inspections, enforcement stage, remedy, monitoring, and current persistence.

The 2016 JICA Moonplains record is historical evidence of a disposal-site configuration and operating context at that time. It is not proof of current illegality or current condition. It can be revisited only if the national typology and evidence gates identify the site as a valid test case.

## Prohibited shortcuts

OSM tag density must not be converted into capacity, illegal dumping, pollution, or causal burden. Proximity to water, wetlands, forest, or protected areas must not be converted into contamination or legal breach. A policy target or compliance report must not be treated as an outcome. A court judgment must not be generalized into national prevalence. A present-day claim must not be inferred from an old survey without a current-status check.

## Decision rule

Proceed to a named local case only when the case satisfies Gates 1–4, 6, and 8; when Gates 5 and 7 are sufficiently resolved for the chosen hypothesis; and when the unresolved question cannot be answered responsibly from desk evidence alone. Otherwise, remain at the national typology or data-acquisition stage.
