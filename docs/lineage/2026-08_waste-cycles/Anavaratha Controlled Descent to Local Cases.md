# Anavaratha Controlled Descent to Local Cases

## Purpose

Localities should enter Anavaratha only after the general waste model and national variation have been explored. A place is not selected because it is familiar, dramatic, or easy to reach. It is selected because it provides a valid test of a prior system type or hypothesis.

## Descent sequence

| Step | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. General ontology | What counts as waste, flow, stock, value, burden, leakage, recovery, and control? | Definitions and boundary rules |
| 2. General system model | What are the universal generators, pathways, capacities, actors, receptors, and feedbacks? | Causal system model |
| 3. National material architecture | What materials enter Sri Lanka, how are they used, discarded, recovered, imported, exported, or accumulated? | National material-flow structure |
| 4. National institutional architecture | Which institutions define, finance, regulate, collect, recover, dispose of, and report waste? | Responsibility and power map |
| 5. Waste-stream profiles | How do organics, plastics, e-waste, construction waste, hazardous waste, wastewater, and residuals differ? | Stream-specific cycle models |
| 6. National variation | How do density, population, economy, settlement form, climate, land, infrastructure, and governance vary? | Variation matrix |
| 7. System typology | Which recurring types exist? | Typology of system contexts |
| 8. Hypothesis selection | Which mechanism requires empirical testing? | Primary hypothesis and alternatives |
| 9. Case-selection logic | Which locality best tests that mechanism with available evidence? | Primary and shadow case |
| 10. Field design | What observation would confirm, reject, or revise the hypothesis? | Targeted validation protocol |

## System typology before named places

| System type | Defining characteristics | Typical research question |
|---|---|---|
| Dense metropolitan | High population/activity density, mixed land use, limited local land, complex formal services | Does high service intensity reduce total burden or transfer it elsewhere? |
| Facility-linked regional | Multiple origins feed a shared disposal or treatment node | Does the receiving node have usable capacity and environmental control? |
| Coastal and drainage-connected | Waste moves through drains, canals, rivers, shoreline, or marine pathways | Which actors and events drive land-to-water leakage? |
| Small local authority | Limited finance, staff, land, equipment, and technical capacity | Which low-cost pathway fits the authority’s actual operating conditions? |
| Rural or dispersed | Long distances, low density, weak formal service coverage, self-disposal or burning | How do distance and low service density reshape waste pathways? |
| Tourism or event-intensive | Transient population and peak activity alter generation and service pressure | How much does the denominator change by season or event? |
| Industrial or production-linked | Commercial or industrial material flows may dominate municipal flows | Where do production residues enter or bypass municipal systems? |
| Crisis or transition | Disaster, facility closure, political change, or new infrastructure alters pathways | Does crisis produce durable institutional learning or temporary displacement? |

## Case-selection criteria

A named locality should be selected only after it can be assigned to a system type and linked to a clearly stated hypothesis. The selection should consider analytical relevance, evidence compatibility, pathway visibility, variation from other cases, validation feasibility, institutional access, worker and community safety, and the risk of stigmatizing a place.

## Primary and shadow cases

The preferred design is one primary case and one shadow comparison case. The primary case receives detailed analysis. The shadow case is used only to test whether the mechanism appears under a different system condition. The shadow case should not expand the project into an unfocused national comparison.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do not start with a locality and generalize its features into a national model. Do not select a place only because it has a famous landfill or crisis. Do not treat a policy target, facility, project, or dashboard as proof of performance. Do not use a composite score to hide incompatible evidence. Do not collect field observations before defining the hypothesis and decision they are meant to inform.

## Final descent gate

Before a locality is named in the core research design, the project must show: the relevant general mechanism; the national or system-type variation that makes the locality informative; the reason the locality is a valid test; the data boundary and denominator; the pathway to be observed; the alternative explanations; and the decision that the result could change.

> **Local evidence should test the model; it should not silently become the model.**
