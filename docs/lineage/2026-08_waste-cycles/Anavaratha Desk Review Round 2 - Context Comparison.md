# Anavaratha Desk Review Round 2: Context Comparison

## Comparison principle

The current source base is not a single synchronized dataset. It contains national plans, provincial studies, local-authority cases, coastal-city research, and international methodologies. The purpose of this comparison is to identify transferable mechanisms while preserving differences in scale, population, period, geography, and evidence type.

## What can be transferred across contexts

The following mechanisms recur sufficiently to become core Anavaratha hypotheses: waste systems need different approaches at different local-authority scales; collection and recovery pathways are interdependent; material quality and destination markets matter; facilities require recurrent operation and maintenance; institutional and political conditions affect implementation; informal actors can be undercounted; data continuity is a management capacity; and burdens can move across administrative and ecological boundaries.

## What cannot be transferred automatically

A collection rate from one place cannot be applied to another without checking population, land use, settlement density, route distance, commercial activity, season, and waste definition. A composting or incineration proposal cannot be copied without checking feedstock quality, contamination, technical capability, environmental controls, cost, maintenance, and market or disposal conditions. A policy target cannot be interpreted as an achieved result. A national plastic estimate cannot be combined with a local-authority total municipal-waste estimate.
