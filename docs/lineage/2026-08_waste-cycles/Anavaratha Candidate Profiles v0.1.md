# Anavaratha Candidate Profiles v0.1

## Status

These profiles are provisional desk-based hypotheses. They are not current operational diagnoses. “Unknown” means the desk evidence is insufficient, not that the condition is absent.

## Candidate A: Dompe/Gampaha-connected system

| Dimension | Desk profile |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | CEA identifies Dompe Sanitary Landfill and the Gampaha District Integrated Solid Waste Management Project within its special-project context; the Cabinet Office describes a sanitary-landfill initiative in Dompe, Gampaha District.[1] [2] |
| Spatial logic | A facility-linked system with potential origin local authorities, transport pathways, and receiving-site burden |
| Waste lens | Collection, transport, recovery, final disposal, facility operation, and receiving-community context |
| Capacity hypothesis | Clear disposal node and institutional project history may make records and actors identifiable |
| Constraint hypothesis | Usable capacity, current operating status, access, maintenance, upstream collection, and community acceptance must be established |
| Population/activity need | Local-authority populations, generator mix, route coverage, and facility inflow denominators are currently unknown |
| Time need | Historical project period versus current operation and post-project persistence |
| Main value for Anavaratha | Strong candidate for studying origin-to-destination flows and whether a disposal node is integrated into a reliable pathway |
| Main risk | Treating the existence of a sanitary landfill as proof of system success |
| Desk priority | Verify current facility status, receiving local authorities, flows, capacity, records, and downstream recovery |

## Candidate B: Colombo Municipal Council system

| Dimension | Desk profile |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | The Colombo case record describes a post-2017 response including source segregation, awareness, home composting, recycling markets, e-waste separation, route mapping, GPS, CCTV, enforcement, by-laws, and waste-to-energy.[3] |
| Spatial logic | Dense commercial and administrative city, with significant daily activity and limited land inside city boundaries; residuals and facilities are spatially separated |
| Waste lens | Crisis response, source separation, collection logistics, disposal transition, technology mix, and institutional coordination |
| Capacity hypothesis | Stronger visibility of formal actors, route systems, and policy responses than in a small authority |
| Constraint hypothesis | Land scarcity, public resistance to receiving sites, high activity pressure, historic accumulation, and potential dependence on external destinations |
| Population/activity need | The case record gives population and area figures but also describes a large floating population; these must be harmonized before per-capita calculations.[3] |
| Time need | Compare pre-2017 crisis, response period, 2019 changes, and current persistence; distinguish case-study claims from independent evaluation |
| Main value for Anavaratha | Strong candidate for studying crisis-driven system change and whether technology, segregation, enforcement, and data systems work as a chain |
| Main risk | Accepting a self-reported or case-database “success” narrative without testing burden transfer, worker conditions, residuals, and persistence |
| Desk priority | Extract independent evaluations, route/facility data, receiving-site relationships, and post-implementation evidence |

## Candidate C: Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia coastal system

| Dimension | Desk profile |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | A peer-reviewed coastal study covers 579 households, 182 commercial properties, and 103 institutions and examines polythene and plastic waste, stakeholder roles, and environmental release through canal networks.[4] |
| Spatial logic | High-density mixed land use, coastal setting, and canal connectivity create a clear source-to-waterway pathway |
| Waste lens | Plastic generation, private and public collection, awareness, enforcement, alternatives, burning, landfill, and water-body discharge |
| Capacity hypothesis | Multiple generator types and private collection create a rich stakeholder and pathway profile |
| Constraint hypothesis | Limited council capacity, irregular collection, weak enforcement, poor awareness, negative attitudes, and unavailable practical alternatives |
| Population/activity need | Household, commercial, and institutional denominators are unusually useful for proportional comparison |
| Time need | Establish study period and determine whether findings persist under current service conditions |
| Main value for Anavaratha | Strong candidate for studying multi-actor plastic pathways and leakage into canals and marine systems |
| Main risk | Assuming coastal plastic dynamics represent inland or facility-linked systems |
| Desk priority | Extract the study’s definitions, sample design, flow quantities, private-collection role, and canal or water-body pathway assumptions |

| Informal recovery importance | To verify | To verify | To verify |
| Cross-boundary transfer relevance | Strong | Strong | Strong through canals and coastal pathways |

## Provisional recommendation

Keep all three profiles active during the desk phase. Do not select a final pilot yet. Dompe/Gampaha is strongest for a facility-linked origin-to-destination study; Colombo is strongest for a high-density crisis-response and institutional-change study; Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia is strongest for a multi-actor coastal plastic-leakage study. The final selection should depend on the research question, data access, cooperation, and the ability to validate a specific hypothesis safely.
