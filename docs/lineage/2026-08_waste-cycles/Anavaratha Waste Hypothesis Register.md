# Anavaratha Waste Hypothesis Register

## How to use this register

Each hypothesis is provisional. It must be linked to a location, population or activity group, time period, waste stream, capacity condition, constraint, mechanism, expected evidence, alternative explanation, and future decision. The register is designed to stop the literature review from becoming a collection of disconnected observations.

## Initial hypothesis families

| ID | Provisional hypothesis | Mechanism to investigate | Desk evidence needed | Future validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-01 | A one-size-fits-all waste policy performs differently across local-authority scales | Policy design does not match population, density, land, finance, or service geometry | Policy documents, local-authority classifications, population, budgets, facility and route evidence | Compare selected authorities using matched indicators |
| H-02 | Waste collection failure is often a capacity mismatch rather than only a behavior problem | Demand exceeds usable vehicles, staff, routes, finance, or facility capacity | Service records, asset lists, population/activity estimates, budgets, schedules | Route and service verification |
| H-03 | Source separation fails when downstream recovery pathways are unreliable | Households and businesses lose incentive when separated material is remixed, rejected, or has no market | Collection design, facility records, buyer destinations, program reports | Observe separated collection and destination chain |
| H-04 | Visible cleanliness can conceal spatial transfer of waste burden | Waste is moved from public spaces to disposal edges, neighboring authorities, waterways, or informal workers | Origin-destination records, facility locations, hotspot reports, receiving-community evidence | Trace selected flows across boundaries |
| H-05 | Short project cycles produce temporary improvement without system persistence | Awareness, staff, maintenance, or funding declines after project closure | Project documents, before/during/after reports, budget continuity | Follow-up measurement after support ends |
| H-06 | Informal recovery is an essential but undercounted flow | Informal actors divert materials before or outside official records | Research, interviews, buyer networks, facility discrepancies | Ethical actor mapping and flow tracing |
| H-07 | Organic waste offers large diversion potential but requires quality and market conditions | Contamination, transport, processing, and demand determine whether composting is viable | Composition studies, facility records, market and land evidence | Sampling, contamination checks, output validation |
| H-08 | Data-system failure is itself a waste-system constraint | Incompatible definitions and missing records weaken planning and accountability | Database studies, reporting forms, metadata, source comparisons | Data-audit and repeat-record test |
| H-09 | Seasonal and event-related changes materially alter service pressure | Rain, festivals, tourism, harvests, school cycles, or disasters change generation and access | Time series, calendars, rainfall, facility and route changes | Repeat measurement across selected periods |
| H-10 | Ecological and social exposure is unevenly distributed | Location, income, land value, drainage, and political power shape who receives residual burdens | Spatial layers, population, land use, hazard, facility, and complaint records | Receptor and exposure validation |

## Sense-making matrix

Score or classify each candidate location only after defining the underlying evidence. Avoid a composite score at the beginning.

| Dimension | Low | Medium | High | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material pressure | Low reported generation/activity | Moderate or uncertain | High generation or intense activity | Measured/reported/estimated |
| Service capacity | Adequate and reliable | Partial or variable | Insufficient or unreliable | Record/observation/proxy |
| Facility capacity | Available and controlled | Limited, aging, or uncertain | Overloaded, absent, or uncontrolled | Facility evidence |
| Data capacity | Regular, compatible, auditable | Partial or inconsistent | Sparse, conflicting, or opaque | Data audit |
| Recovery capability | Multiple viable pathways | Some recovery with constraints | Minimal or inaccessible recovery | Market/facility evidence |
| Accumulation pressure | Rare or nonpersistent | Localized or seasonal | Persistent or expanding | Repeat observations/reports |
| Spatial inequality risk | Burden broadly distributed or low | Mixed exposure | Concentrated receiving burden | GIS and social evidence |
| Temporal volatility | Stable | Seasonal or periodic variation | Rapid change or crisis-sensitive | Time evidence |

## Failure-to-intervention logic

A failure code is useful only when it can lead to a different decision. “Lack of awareness” should trigger questions about information, trust, service reliability, collection design, incentives, and enforcement. “Lack of capacity” should identify which capacity—land, vehicle, staff, finance, data, or market—is binding. “Lack of technology” should be tested against maintenance, skills, financing, input quality, and downstream demand.

## How contradictions should be recorded

When two sources disagree, record both claims, their periods, boundaries, methods, and incentives. Possible explanations include real change over time, different definitions, selective sampling, reporting incentives, or transfer of material between formal and informal systems. The contradiction is not a nuisance to be averaged away; it may reveal where the system is least visible.
