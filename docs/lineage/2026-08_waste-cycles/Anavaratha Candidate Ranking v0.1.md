# Anavaratha Candidate Ranking v0.1

## Ranking principle

This is a provisional research-design ranking, not a ranking of which place has the “worst” waste problem. A strong first candidate should expose a meaningful mechanism, have a clear system boundary, offer enough desk evidence to form hypotheses, and permit later validation without pretending that existing case-study claims are already verified.

## Criteria

| Criterion | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Research value | Does the candidate illuminate an important waste-system mechanism? |
| Pathway clarity | Can origin, collection, destination, recovery, disposal, and receiving burden be traced? |
| Evidence readiness | Is enough desk evidence available to define a credible baseline? |
| Validation feasibility | Can a small future study test the central hypotheses? |
| Transferability | Can the lessons inform other Sri Lankan contexts? |
| Risk control | Can self-reported success, boundary confusion, or stigmatization be managed? |

## Why Colombo is provisionally first

Colombo offers the clearest desk-based intervention timeline: a crisis event, subsequent segregation and awareness measures, route mapping, GPS, enforcement, by-laws, facility changes, and waste-to-energy. This makes it suitable for studying **system change over time**, which is central to Anavaratha. Its main weakness is that the available case record describes achievements and may not independently evaluate burden transfer, worker outcomes, residuals, or long-term persistence.

## Why Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia remains a strong alternative

The coastal study provides a useful generator structure across households, commercial properties, and institutions. It is well suited to proportional comparison and the analysis of private/public collection, awareness, enforcement, alternatives, and canal leakage. It is the strongest candidate if Anavaratha’s first waste stream is specifically plastic and the main ecological pathway is waterborne leakage.

## Why Dompe/Gampaha should not be discarded

Dompe/Gampaha is analytically valuable because it centers the receiving node and makes spatial transfer visible. It is the strongest candidate if the project’s first question is about landfills, facility capacity, transport routes, regional flows, and receiving communities. It should not be selected solely because a sanitary landfill exists; the first desk gate must establish present operation, usable capacity, inbound authorities, outflows, environmental controls, and records.

## Selection gate

The ranking should be revised after three checks: whether current secondary data can be obtained; whether a defined boundary and denominator can be constructed; and whether a future validation study could test one or two consequential hypotheses. If Colombo’s independent evidence remains weak, Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia may be the more validation-ready first case. If facility-linked data are strong, Dompe/Gampaha may become the best first case.
