# Anavaratha Desk Baseline v0.3

## Status and scope

This is the third desk-based working baseline for the waste side of Anavaratha. It narrows the national problem into three candidate contexts without selecting a final pilot prematurely: the Colombo Municipal Council system, the Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia coastal system, and the Dompe/Gampaha-connected system.

The profiles are hypotheses based on existing sources. They do not constitute current operational audits, and they do not establish a current national mass balance. Reported values, targets, and case-study outcomes remain tied to their source definitions and periods.

## Core interpretation

The waste problem is best represented as a **pressure–capacity–pathway system**. Population and activity generate material pressure. Physical, operational, financial, technical, institutional, market, social, and informational capacities determine how much of that pressure can be managed. Pathway reliability determines whether material is separated, collected, transported, recovered, treated, disposed of, leaked, or accumulated.

A useful working relation is:

> **System gap = pressure − usable capacity, under a defined place and time condition.**

This is not yet a predictive equation. It is a discipline for comparing locations without confusing total waste with waste per person, nominal capacity with usable capacity, or visible cleanliness with overall system performance.

## Candidate profiles

| Candidate | Strongest analytical value | Current desk evidence | Main unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colombo Municipal Council | Crisis response, high-density activity, source separation, logistics, technology, institutional coordination, and persistence | Rich case record and life-cycle study; independent outcome verification remains necessary | Whether improvements persist and whether burdens are transferred to receiving facilities or communities |
| Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia | Household–commercial–institutional plastic pathways and canal/coastal leakage | Peer-reviewed study with 579 households, 182 commercial properties, and 103 institutions | Current status of collection, private recovery, enforcement, and leakage pathways |
| Dompe/Gampaha | Facility-linked origin-to-destination flows, landfill operation, receiving burden, and regional integration | Official institutional/project context and report metadata | Current facility status, usable capacity, inbound authorities, outflows, environmental controls, and community context |

## Provisional research ranking

Colombo is provisionally ranked first for a general waste-system study because it provides the clearest documented intervention timeline after the 2017 Meethotamulla collapse. The record includes segregation, awareness, home composting, recycling markets, e-waste separation, route mapping, GPS, CCTV, enforcement, by-laws, and waste-to-energy. This makes Colombo suitable for testing whether a crisis response becomes an integrated and durable system. The risk is accepting a case-study success narrative without independently checking residuals, receiving burdens, worker outcomes, and post-project persistence.

Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia is provisionally ranked second and may become first if the initial research question is specifically plastic leakage. Its mixed sample of households, commercial properties, and institutions supports proportional comparison, and its canal-connected coastal setting makes origin-to-receptor pathways visible. Its limitation is narrower transferability to inland and facility-centered systems.

Dompe/Gampaha is provisionally ranked third for the general first case but remains highly important. It may become first if the project centers on landfills, facility capacity, transport, receiving communities, and cross-boundary burden transfer. The existence of a sanitary-landfill project is not itself evidence of a functioning system; present operation and usable capacity must be established first.

## Candidate comparison logic

| Comparison dimension | Colombo | Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia | Dompe/Gampaha |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population/activity pressure | Dense city plus large daily floating activity | Dense mixed residential, commercial, and institutional activity | Local-authority generators connected to a receiving facility; exact activity profile to verify |
| Main material lens | Mixed MSW, organics, recyclables, e-waste, residuals | Plastic and polythene, with canal and coastal pathways | Mixed MSW, disposal residuals, facility inflow, recovery and transport |
| Main capacity lens | Route systems, source separation, technology, institutional coordination | Public/private collection, awareness, enforcement, local alternatives | Facility operation, transport, usable disposal capacity, upstream collection |
| Main constraint hypothesis | Land scarcity, receiving-site resistance, high pressure, persistence and transfer | Irregular collection, limited council capacity, weak enforcement, alternatives and awareness | Current operation, facility capacity, access, maintenance, upstream records and acceptance |
| Main time lens | Pre-crisis, post-2017 response, 2019 changes, current persistence | Study period versus current conditions | Project period versus current facility operation and post-project persistence |
| Main spatial lens | City origin to external or receiving facilities | Generators to canals, water bodies, and marine environment | Origins to facility and receiving community; possible inter-authority transfer |

## Evidence gaps before fieldwork

The immediate desk gap is not lack of more general literature. It is lack of harmonized candidate-level evidence. Before field validation, Anavaratha should attempt to obtain or reconstruct population/activity denominators, waste-stream definitions, collection coverage, route and facility records, recovery destinations, usable capacity, time series, and receiving-location evidence for each candidate.

The project should also distinguish four types of evidence: **current measured condition; historical measured condition; policy target or institutional claim; and desk inference**. These categories must not be blended in one score.

## Pre-field decision gate

Field validation becomes justified only when the following conditions are met:

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| G1: Defined question | The candidate has one primary research question and no more than two supporting questions |
| G2: Defined boundary | The study states what is inside and outside the system, including receiving destinations |
| G3: Defined denominator | Population, households, establishments, visitors, route length, facility inflow, or another activity denominator is specified |
| G4: Provisional pathway | The desk review maps origin, collection, transport, recovery/treatment, disposal, leakage, and accumulation with confidence labels |
| G5: Binding-constraint hypothesis | The field phase is designed to test a specific suspected constraint rather than “understand everything” |
| G6: Decision consequence | It is clear which intervention or research choice could change based on the result |
| G7: Ethical and practical feasibility | Permissions, safety, consent, worker/community protection, and data handling are considered |
| G8: Evidence gap value | The unknown is both consequential and realistically measurable in a limited field round |

If these gates are not met, continue desk research and do not begin broad fieldwork.

## Recommended next step

Continue with Colombo, Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia, and Dompe/Gampaha in parallel during the next desk pass. Extract page-level evidence, verify the historical and current status of each candidate, and fill a comparison table only where definitions are compatible. After that, select one primary candidate and one fallback candidate.

The most elegant first research design is likely a **single primary case with a small comparative shadow case**. For example, Colombo could be the primary system-change case and Dehiwala-Mt. Lavinia the plastic-leakage comparison; or Dompe/Gampaha could be primary if facility-linked flow and receiving burden become the central question.
