# Anavaratha Desk Review Round 1: Cross-Source Comparison

## Purpose

The current evidence base is heterogeneous. Some sources describe national policy, others study a province, a local authority, a coastal city, a database, or a small community. This comparison identifies what can be learned across sources and what must not be combined quantitatively.

## What the comparison currently suggests

Across the sources, the most stable common finding is a **capacity–pressure–pathway relationship**. Waste-system problems intensify where generation or activity pressure exceeds usable service, facility, institutional, or market capacity. The sources also suggest that the first binding constraint changes by place: land, collection vehicles, staff, finance, technical skills, policy fit, market access, or data continuity may dominate in different contexts.

A second common finding is that waste-management outcomes are **chain-dependent**. Source separation cannot be judged apart from collection reliability, sorting, cleaning, destination markets, and residual disposal. A project cannot be judged only by whether a facility was built. It must also be judged by use, maintenance, output quality, worker safety, financial continuity, and persistence after external support.

A third finding is that the system is **spatially and temporally distributed**. Materials and burdens can move across local-authority boundaries and downstream markets. Project performance may change with season, funding, political leadership, population, land use, or maintenance. Therefore, Anavaratha should compare pathways and stocks over time, not only snapshots at one location.

## Current evidence gaps

The first round does not yet support a current, fully comparable national or pilot-level mass balance. The most important gaps are compatible local-authority denominators, current route and facility records, time series, formal and informal recovery quantities, persistent hotspot records, and comparable capacity measures.

This is not a failure of the desk review. It is one of its outputs: it identifies what must be requested, measured, or validated next and prevents false precision.
