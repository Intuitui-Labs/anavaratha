# Anavaratha Eagle-View Waste Model

## Central proposition

Anavaratha studies waste as the visible and invisible result of **material pressure moving through systems with unequal capacity, power, pathway reliability, and ecological receiving limits**. The model follows materials from entry and use to discard, recovery, residuals, leakage, and accumulation, while also following responsibility, value, risk, and information.

## Layer architecture

| Layer | Object | Main question | Required output |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1: Ontology | Waste, resource, residue, burden, value, flow, stock | What are we naming and measuring? | Definitions |
| L2: General system | Generators, pathways, nodes, actors, receptors, feedbacks | How does any waste system operate? | Causal model |
| L3: Material | Inflows, use, discard, transformation, recovery | What material is moving and changing? | Material-cycle map |
| L4: Stream | Organics, plastics, paper, metals, glass, textiles, e-waste, hazardous, wastewater, residuals | Why do different streams require different systems? | Stream profiles |
| L5: Pressure | Population, activity, consumption, season, economy, land use | What creates demand for management? | Pressure indicators |
| L6: Capacity | Physical, operational, financial, technical, institutional, social, market, informational, ecological | What can the system actually do? | Usable-capacity inventory |
| L7: Constraint | Binding limits, trade-offs, bottlenecks, path dependence | Where and why does the pathway fail? | Constraint mechanism map |
| L8: Actor and power | Responsibility, control, knowledge, value, risk, voice | Who decides, benefits, bears burden, or remains invisible? | Actor-power map |
| L9: Time | Daily, seasonal, project, political, economic, long-term | What changes, persists, accumulates, or returns? | Flow-stock timeline |
| L10: Space | Origins, routes, nodes, destinations, boundaries, receptors | Where do material, value, and burden go? | Spatial network |
| L11: Evidence | Measurements, estimates, targets, claims, gaps, contradictions | What do we actually know? | Evidence-quality register |
| L12: System type | Metropolitan, facility-linked, coastal, rural, small-authority, tourism, industrial, crisis | Which conditions shape the mechanism? | Typology |
| L13: Case | Named locality selected as a test | Where can a hypothesis be tested? | Controlled case design |

## Three linked ledgers

Anavaratha should maintain three ledgers through every layer.

| Ledger | Tracks |
|---|---|
| Material ledger | Mass, quality, energy, toxicity, movement, transformation, recovery, residuals |
| Burden ledger | Cost, labor, exposure, land, odour, pollution, risk, inconvenience, future liability |
| Knowledge ledger | Definitions, measurements, estimates, reporting gaps, contradictions, confidence, accountability |

A material-flow map without a burden ledger can hide inequality. A burden map without a material ledger can lose the mechanism. Either map without a knowledge ledger can create false confidence.

## Core equations as reasoning devices

`Net pressure = material/activity demand − usable management capacity`

`Pathway balance = inflow − recovery − controlled treatment/disposal − observed leakage − unknown remainder`

`Stock change = inflow to stock − outflow/remediation`

`Burden distribution = who receives cost, risk, exposure, land occupation, or future liability`

These are conceptual relations until compatible data are available. They are not invitations to fabricate missing quantities.

## Hypothesis families by layer

| Layer | Example hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Ontology | Conflicting waste totals partly arise because institutions use different boundaries and categories |
| Material | Upstream product and packaging choices shape downstream management difficulty |
| Stream | Organic, plastic, hazardous, and residual streams fail through different mechanisms |
| Pressure | Population alone does not explain pressure; activity, density, commerce, tourism, and season also matter |
| Capacity | Nominal assets overstate performance when maintenance, staffing, fuel, access, or markets are missing |
| Constraint | The first binding constraint differs by system type and scale |
| Actor/power | Official data and visible cleanliness may reflect the interests of some actors more than exposed communities or informal workers |
| Time | Project improvements may not persist after financing, leadership, or attention changes |
| Space | Management can transfer material and burden across administrative or ecological boundaries |
| Evidence | Data-system weakness reduces both analytical accuracy and management capacity |
| System type | The same intervention performs differently when density, land, route geometry, or receiving capacity changes |

## Completion standard

The model is ready to descend when every layer has a definition, a causal role, possible indicators, known blind spots, and at least one alternative explanation. A named locality is not necessary for any of these first layers.

## Final research logic

The project should move in this order:

> **Define → theorize → classify → measure → compare → hypothesize → descend → validate → revise.**

It should not move in this order:

> **Choose a place → collect interesting facts → generalize the place into a theory.**
